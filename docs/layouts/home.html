Host your own shit

serving 01 // fuck their tos // fuck their server // they can de-platform this dick

Big platforms change their rules whenever it suits them, and rarely in your favor. One update and the “naughty” art, jokes, and unpopular opinions are banished, along with whoever posted them. If you built a community there, surprise! you own nothing and have no say in it.

The fix is to host it yourself. A few simple steps turn your Dens install from just a client into a server as well. These steps include forwarding a few ports on your router and pointing a domain at your IP. That’s the fucky part, so there’s a step by step guide written for people who’ve never done either.

Encrypted and pseudonymous by default

serving 02 // fuck mass surveillance // smile, you're on camera, not here though

Dens seals your direct messages end to end, so only the two of you can read them. It knows you only as a username and a key that never leaves your computer: no email, no phone number, no account that follows you from den to den. And it strips the location and camera details out of your photos before they’re sent, and never writes your IP address to disk.

Pseudonymous, not anonymous{{ partial "landing/arrow.html" . }}

In a den you go by a name you pick, not your real one, and you never hand over an email or a phone number. But the den still sees your IP address while you’re connected, like any server does, so it’s no hiding place from whoever runs it.

It’s not perfect, nothing is. Here is how its flavor of privacy compares to similar alternatives.

under the hood

Open source{{ partial "landing/arrow.html" . }}

Every line is on GitHub under the MIT license. Read it, fork it, run it, rip it apart. If anything on this page is a lie, you can check.

Built for home internet{{ partial "landing/arrow.html" . }}

Dens is meant to run on a normal PC on a residential connection, where upload speed is precious. A den is sized for about a thousand members.

Why it asks for admin{{ partial "landing/arrow.html" . }}

Installing and updating ask for admin, but Dens itself never runs as admin. That’s how Dens gets its own locked-down system account: it runs while you’re logged out, and your keys sit in a separate process that your browser, your games and that Skyrim tiddy mod you downloaded can’t read without admin rights.

What seals your DMs{{ partial "landing/arrow.html" . }}

Standard parts, not homemade ones: X25519 and post-quantum ML-KEM-768 set up each DM’s key, and XChaCha20-Poly1305 seals what you send, all from Go’s own crypto libraries. How they fit together is Dens’s own design, public but not yet independently audited. Your key is made on your computer and the den never sees it, so it carries your DMs without being able to read them. See Privacy for what Dens protects you from and what it doesn’t.

Dens has nobody to pay back. There’s no company and no central server: each den runs on a computer belonging to one of its members, so the only running costs are the domain, GitHub Actions and Cloudflare R2, which build and host the downloads. It didn’t need investors to get built either, because I built it with heavy help from AI. I don’t know where that technology is going, but while it’s here, I’d rather point it at something like this. The code’s all public, so you can check the work. Dens is free, open source software, nobody makes money off your den (not even me), and it’ll stay that way.
