Host your own shit, you lazy bastards

serving 01 // fuck their terms of service // their server, their rules // your server, your rules

Large platforms are unstable, they change rules frequently, often for the worse. Banning “naughty” content and opinions that advertisers or payment processors have been bullied into censoring, often by fascist christian nationalists. Which can obviously be upsetting for users who built a home / community there.

A remedy for this is to stop being lazy and host your own shit. Dens makes this about as easy as possible. A single toggle can turn an install from just a client into a server as well. All you need to do then is forward a few ports and point a domain at your IP. Here is the step by step guide for non-technical individuals.

Encrypted and pseudonymous by default

serving 02 // fuck mass surveillance // smile, you're on camera // not here though

Dens seals your direct messages end to end, so only the two of you can read them. It knows you only as a username and a key that never leaves your computer: no email, no phone number, no account that follows you from den to den. And it strips the location and camera details out of your photos before they’re sent, and never writes your IP address to disk.

Pseudonymous, not anonymous{{ partial "landing/arrow.html" . }}

In a den you go by a name you pick, not your real one, and you never hand over an email or a phone number. But the den still sees your IP address while you’re connected, like any server does, so it’s no hiding place from whoever runs it.

It’s not perfect, nothing is. Here is how its flavor of privacy compares to similar alternatives.

under the hood

Open source{{ partial "landing/arrow.html" . }}

Every line is on GitHub under the MIT license. Read it, fork it, run it, rip it apart. If anything on this page is a lie, you can check.

One binary{{ partial "landing/arrow.html" . }}

Dens is a single Go program with its database (SQLite) and media tools (FFmpeg) compiled in. No Docker stack, no database server to babysit, and every third-party tool in the build is pinned by checksum.

Why it asks for admin{{ partial "landing/arrow.html" . }}

Installing or updating asks for admin, never running it. That’s how Dens gets its own locked-down system account: it runs while you’re logged out, and your keys sit in a separate process that your browser, your games and that sketchy mod you downloaded can’t read without admin rights.

Your side does the work{{ partial "landing/arrow.html" . }}

Your own install strips your photos, seals your DMs and keeps an encrypted cache of what you’ve already seen, so a den only has to send you what’s new.

Built for home internet{{ partial "landing/arrow.html" . }}

Dens is meant to run on a normal PC on a residential connection, where upload speed is precious. Members pick up where they left off instead of reloading everything, presence is batched, and pictures load as previews until you open them. A den is sized for about a thousand members.

Dens has nobody to pay back. There’s no company and no central server: each den runs on a computer belonging to one of its members, so the only running costs are the domain, GitHub Actions and Cloudflare R2, which build and host the downloads. It didn’t need investors to get built either, because I built it with heavy help from AI. I don’t know where that technology is going, but while it’s here, I’d rather point it at something like this. The code’s all public, so you can check the work. Dens is free, open source software, nobody makes money off your den (not even me), and it’ll stay that way.
