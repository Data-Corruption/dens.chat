# Dens — Milestones

What each milestone built, and the plan for the one under way; ideas no milestone has taken up yet are in [loose-ideas.md](loose-ideas.md). The design they build toward is [design.md](design.md), with each deployment form's in [installed.md](installed.md), [flatpak.md](flatpak.md) and [android.md](android.md): a section named here, such as Global shortcuts, is one of theirs unless it's one of this file's.

A milestone's plan is written here first, and reviewed before anything is built. Once the milestone is done, what its plan still teaches, such as spike findings and measurements, moves into the design docs' sections, and the rest goes; Git keeps it.

Each milestone ends usable on its own and is tested on Linux and Windows, in Chromium and Firefox. As a rule, each step of a milestone is tested by the automated checks: the harnesses, the Android e2e in the emulator, and the browser checks in the container's Chromium and Firefox. The manual tests, on the phones and in browsers, run together once, at the milestone's end. A spike runs its own trials, and a test that can't wait for the end gets an exception. All platform-specific code lands in M0, while the seams are still cheap to change; later milestones are shared code, but for M7's keys and the helper's start at login, M9's Flatpak, and M12's Android app and the helper's notifications.

M13.1:

- *Which windows.* Options: (a) width alone: every window under 768 pixels, Tailwind's `md`, gets the phone layout and its touch ways, on every form; (b) the form: only the Android app's page; (c) the layout by width, as the page already switches the list and the channel at 768 pixels, and the ways of working it by the pointer: a long press, sheets and bigger targets for a coarse pointer, hover for one that hovers. Pick (c): a narrow desktop window, beside another or in a tiling window manager, gets a layout that fits it rather than a squeezed desktop one, worked by its mouse; the browser checks can prove the phone layout in Chromium and Firefox; and a phone held sideways, or a tablet, wider than 768 pixels gets the desktop's layout, worked by touch. The breakpoint stays at 768.
- *Three panes.* Below 768 pixels a den's page lays its three panes in a row: the list, the open channel or DM in the middle, and the members, or beside a DM the other member's profile. One is on screen at a time. The side panes take 85% of the width, so the channel shows at their edge, and a tap there goes back to it, as phone chat apps do. A pane off screen is `inert`, so focus and a screen reader stay on the one shown. The member list is the pane to the right there, not a toggle the browser keeps open; wide windows keep their toggle. The address names the channel, and the history's entry the pane, so a channel that a link, a notification's click or Forward opens shows.
- *Swipes.* Options: (a) the browser's own: the panes in a row that scrolls sideways and snaps to each (CSS scroll snapping), the browser doing the gesture, its momentum and its feel at the ends, and the page watching which pane settles; (b) the page's own: pointer events tracking the finger and moving the panes, as Discord's app does, which could slide the list under the channel; (c) none, only the header's buttons and Back. Pick (a): it feels like the platform; the page needs no code to tell a sideways swipe from scrolling the list; and a block of code, or anything else that scrolls sideways, keeps the swipes that start on it, as nested scrollers do. `scroll-snap-stop` on the channel keeps a fling from skipping from the list to the members. Android's gesture navigation takes a swipe that starts at the screen's edge for Back, which goes up a level, toward the list, as a swipe to the right does; a swipe starts a little in from the edge, as for any Android app's drawer. In a narrow desktop window a touchpad's sideways swipe moves the panes too.
- *Back.* Each pane, dialog and sheet, and the viewer, takes an entry in the page's history as it opens, so Back closes what's on top, and otherwise goes up a level: from the members to the channel, from the channel to the list, from the list to Home. Home is the history's root, so Back there leaves the page: on Android the app closes, or during a call goes behind the others, as the shell does now. A page opened at a channel, as a notification's tap opens it on Android, puts Home and the list beneath it, so Back goes up from there too. A browser's Back works the same in a narrow window, and in a wide one closes a dialog rather than leaving the page under it. Closing a dialog by its ✕ or a tap outside it takes its entry back.
- *Between dens.* Options: (a) Home stays the way between dens, Back from a den's list, its rows marked with what waits in each, unread messages and mentions counted, and the list's Home button marked while another den has something; (b) a switcher: the den's name in the list's header opens a sheet of the dens; (c) a rail of the dens down the list's side, as Discord's. Pick (a): another den is two taps away, Back and Open, as on the desktop, and the marks say when there's something to go for. The service already counts each den's unread messages and mentions for its page; the dens list the page gets gains them, which the protocol never sees.

*M13.1 as built,* where it adds to the plan: in a wider window a den is one level of the history, so its channels take each other's place and Back goes from the den to Home, as from the phone layout's list, where Back once walked the browser's history from channel to channel. The list's own entry keeps the den's address alone, with the channel last shown behind it. The Android app's shell keeps where the page's latest load began in the WebView's history, so Back at Home leaves the app even after a load put an older page beneath it; and a notification's tap on a page that shows opens there in place, through a fragment that says where (`#open=<path>&message=<id>`), rather than loading the page again, which ended a call on it. Firefox's `scrollIntoView` moved the row of panes, so the page's jump to a message, and a long message folding back, scroll their list alone. Home's marks come from each den's status, which gains `unread`, its channels and DMs with messages unread, and `mentions`. The phone check drives Chromium's touch with raw touch events, its synthesized gestures scrolling nothing headless, and in Firefox, whose driven touch scrolls nothing either, a sideways scroll stands in for a swipe; it fails on getting around, a screen it can't reach, and a den's panes scrolling the page sideways, and prints the audits' other findings, which M13.2 and M13.3 make fail as they fix them.

M13.2:

- *A long press.* Options for a message's actions on a touch screen: (a) the ⋯ on every message, always shown, as M11 has it; (b) a long press on the message opens its actions as a sheet, as phone chat apps do, and the ⋯ shows only for a mouse and the keyboard; (c) both. Pick (b): every message's text gets back the 32 pixels the ⋯'s column takes, 294 of 390 rather than 262, and it's what fingers already know. The sheet names the message's author and its time in full, so a compact message's time is a long press away, and holds Reply, Edit, Copy text, Copy link and Delete as rows a finger fits. A press that moves, as a scroll or a swipe starts, isn't a long press; and the long press takes the place of the WebView's own text selection on a message, so copying part of one waits for a Select text in the sheet, should members miss it. M11's done-when holds by keyboard, and by touch through the long press. The channels' ⋯ and those of members in a call stay, in lists with room for them.
- *Sheets.* On the phone layout the page's menus open as sheets from the screen's foot, a message's actions and the composer's + among them, with rows of 48 pixels, closed by a tap above them, a swipe down or Back. Wide windows keep their menus, touch or not.
- *Targets.* For a coarse pointer, a list's rows (channels, DMs, members, a sheet's) are 44 pixels tall, and small buttons take room around them up to 44 pixels without growing to the eye. No control is under 24 pixels at any width, WCAG 2.2's minimum, which the phone check asserts: the composer's ?, a group's row, the description's button, the tips' ⓘ, a photo chip's link and a video's seek bar grow.
- *What hover shows.* What only a hover says reaches touch by a tap: a tip opens on a tap, closes on the next, and stays on the screen; the den's state dot, staff only's lock, a muted bell and LIVE get tips where titles hold what they mean; and a tap shows a video's controls. A title that repeats a button's label stays a title.
- *Touch's hints.* Where a hint names keys, a touch screen gets its own: the writing guide's Sending says the button sends and ↵ starts a line; an edit says nothing of keys, beside its Save and Cancel; comparing says to hold a finger on the picture to see the other one, which does it.
- *The composer.* Options: (a) Enter sends, as now, so no message written on a phone has a second line; (b) for a coarse pointer, a send button beside the box, and ↵ starts a line, as in Signal's, WhatsApp's and Discord's apps; (c) the button, with ↵ sending too. Pick (b), since a phone's keyboard has no Shift. The box asks the keyboard for a return key (`enterkeyhint`), a window with a mouse keeps Enter sending, and the button, 48 pixels, shows once there's something to send. While the keyboard is up the box grows to 5 lines rather than 8, so the list keeps room, and what stands above the box (a reply, files, who else may edit) scrolls within a cap. The composer already stays above the keyboard; the e2e holds it there.
- *Fields' keyboards.* Each field asks the keyboard for what it takes: usernames, invites and den addresses without capitals or corrections (`autocapitalize`, `autocorrect`, `spellcheck`), an address as a URL, sizes and counts as numbers, as the check digits already ask for numbers.

*M13.2 as built,* where it adds to the plan: a press held half a second within 10 pixels is a long one, and the click a browser may send as it's let go goes nowhere, nor onto the sheet opened under the finger; in a wider window the long press opens the message's menu beside it. With a finger Ctrl+Enter still sends, as from a tablet's keyboard. A call member's ⋯ opens a sheet on the phone layout already, its volume a slider of 24 pixels, which M13.4 makes one a finger can hold. Tips are reckoned from their i, which stays put, rather than from the tip, whose move to its last place may still be under way: reckoned from the tip, its transition kept a moved tip moving, and froze the page. A guide's i, a link to dens.chat, opens its tip on the first tap and the guide on the second. Besides the den's state, staff only's lock, a muted bell and LIVE, tips say what a DM's codes to check, a call's members' marks, the call's state, who watches a share and a file's removed metadata mean, and the marks in a list's rows stand beside the row's button, which reaches across the row under them. The share player's controls always show on a touch screen. A label holding a checkbox or radio is at least 24 pixels tall, as is a slider and a task's box. The phone check's audit counts the controls a finger can reach, under no dialog, leaving out words within a line of text, which WCAG leaves to the text's size, and a disabled control's hiding; it takes a list's row for a menu's item, or a control across most of its list item or its list; and it notes the controls whose room reaches less than 44 pixels each way, where its probes 21 pixels out from their middle land. The Android e2e's long press is a swipe that stays where it starts, and its three lines go in by `input text` and `KEYCODE_ENTER`; its page steps send by the composer's button, a touch screen's ↵ starting a line.

M13.3:

- *Dialogs.* Options on the phone layout: (a) a card over the page with a margin round it, as now; (b) a sheet from the screen's foot, as tall as its content up to the screen's height, its title and ✕ at its top, scrolling within; (c) every dialog a page. Pick (b) for the dialogs, a channel's or a group's settings, a profile, deleting a message, removing a member, adding a den and the writing guide among them, and pages for the two with sections, below. One frame, `Dialog` and those like it, changes for all of them.
- *Settings and Manage.* Options for their sections on the phone layout: (a) a row of tabs that scrolls sideways, as now; (b) a list of the sections, each opening as a page with ← back, as Android's own settings; (c) a select. Pick (b): every section shows by name, and Back walks out of a section, then out of the settings. They take the whole screen, and their cards lose the dialog's margin and their own padding, giving their text 358 of 390 pixels rather than 278.
- *What runs past the edge*, as the survey found it: labels wrap, the staff-only toggle's and the recovery codes' checkbox's among them, rather than keeping DaisyUI's `nowrap`; tips open within the screen; comparing stacks its Smaller and Full size; the recovery codes stand in one column, each code whole; and Home's rows put Manage and Open under the den's name.
- *The viewer.* On the phone layout it's full screen: the picture the screen's width, a swipe to the message's next or last picture rather than ‹ and ›, two fingers or a double tap to zoom and a finger to pan, a swipe down or Back to close it, and the name, size, Download and Close in a bar at its foot. Zoom works on every form, with a double click and the wheel on a desktop, and so leaves loose-ideas.md. Comparing a photo's copies takes touch too: a finger held on the picture shows the other one, and two fingers zoom.
- *A phone's settings.* What a phone can't do leaves its settings, as the speaker's choice already has: the keyboard shortcuts, where the service says its form has no keys, as it says its page may play sound without a click, and the Screen share card, where the browser can't share. Push to talk stays, held on the call's button (M13.4). Add a den offers to host only where the service can, and a refused microphone says where Android allows it.
- *A phone's words.* Options for naming the device: (a) "device" where a phone shows the words too, with "computer" kept where only a desktop does (pairing, hosting, the shortcut helper); (b) the service names its device to the page, computer or phone. Pick (a): it needs nothing new of the service, and a form reaches the page only as what it can do. "This browser" becomes "this page" where the Android app shows it. The den lists the phone among its devices by its model, which the shell gives Dens (`Build.MODEL`), rather than as "localhost (android)".
- *The system bars,* a suggestion. The shell's status and navigation bars follow the phone's light or dark mode, as the page's System theme does, but not a theme the member picks. The page tells the service its theme's background as it changes, the service passes it to the shell over its stream, and the shell paints the bars with it, with icons to match. Pick: in, since the bars frame every screen a phone shows.

M13.4:

Pick (b): the call shows wherever the member reads, for 40 pixels while in a call, and the microphone is the call's button's, by the thumb. Home keeps its call box, which fits already.
- *The call's button.* On a touch screen in a call, a round button of 64 pixels holds the microphone: a tap toggles mute, or a press mutes while held, or talks while held, as the member sets it, as Discord's app has a button for push to talk. Options for where it sits: (a) floating at the screen's foot on the right, over every pane and above the composer, where a thumb rests; (b) in the composer's row on a channel, and in the call's block on the list; (c) in the call's bar at the top. Pick (a): a button held to talk wants the thumb's place, and the same place whichever pane shows; with the ⋯ gone from messages on touch (M13.2), the list's right edge has room for it. Its role takes the place of Sending your voice on a touch screen: Toggle mute, the default, and Push to mute send by voice activity, and Push to talk sends only while it's held, ringing for the call as the key does (M11). It's one more source of presses for the actions the keys and the helper drive, and a held press lets go however it ends: lifted, taken by the system, as Android's back gesture or its notification shade takes it, its pointer's capture lost, or the page hidden or out of focus; so the microphone never stays open. It shows the microphone's state: talking in the ring's color, muted struck through.
