# Dens — Milestones

What each milestone built, and the plan for the one under way; ideas no milestone has taken up yet are in [loose-ideas.md](loose-ideas.md). The design they build toward is [design.md](design.md), with each deployment form's in [installed.md](installed.md), [flatpak.md](flatpak.md) and [android.md](android.md): a section named here, such as Global shortcuts, is one of theirs unless it's one of this file's.

A milestone's plan is written here first, and reviewed before anything is built. Once the milestone is done, what its plan still teaches, such as spike findings and measurements, moves into the design docs' sections, and the rest goes; Git keeps it.

Each milestone ends usable on its own and is tested on Linux and Windows, in Chromium and Firefox. As a rule, each step of a milestone is tested by the automated checks: the harnesses, the Android e2e in the emulator, and the browser checks in the container's Chromium and Firefox. The manual tests, on the phones and in browsers, run together once, at the milestone's end. A spike runs its own trials, and a test that can't wait for the end gets an exception. All platform-specific code lands in M0, while the seams are still cheap to change; later milestones are shared code, but for M7's keys and the helper's start at login, M9's Flatpak, and M12's Android app and the helper's notifications.

**M16, end-to-end encryption without the ceremony.** A DM opens today only once both its members have typed each other's half of a 32-digit code. That keeps the den from slipping in keys of its own, but costs every DM a ceremony, which members cut short by pasting the digits into a channel the den controls, proving nothing. M16 takes Signal's way of checking keys and keeps Dens's way of keeping history. Each member has one identity in each den, made from their DM seal, so every device holding the seal holds it; a member's Dens keeps the identity the den gives for someone the first time it needs it, and says when it changes; and comparing codes is each member's choice, at any time, and nothing the den can see. DMs, group DMs and calls all seal their keys to identities, so one comparison covers all three. There's no ratchet: its forward secrecy comes from forgetting keys, while a DM's history opens from the den on each of a member's devices (see loose-ideas.md's Probably not doing). Approving a new device keeps its typed digits, since a device the den slipped past it would take the seal, and every DM with it. Events and badges move to M17 (below).

M16.1:

- *An identity.* Each member's identity in a den is two key pairs: one for HPKE (RFC 9180) with X-Wing, which is X25519 and ML-KEM-768 together, that keys are sealed to (`crypto/hpke`'s `MLKEM768X25519`, in Go from 1.26); and one for Ed25519, which signs what the member's Dens makes. Both come from their DM seal by HKDF, salted with the den's ID, each under a label of its own, so every device holding the seal holds the identity with nothing new to hand over, and no den sees what the member is at another. The service makes them from the seal as it uses them and clears them after, as it opens DM keys now. A member who starts over gets a new identity with their new seal.
- *On the den.* A member's Dens publishes the identity's two public keys, 1,248 bytes, as it signs in, where the den lacks them or holds others; a Dens without the seal, as after a recovery code until the member types it, publishes nothing. A member's record carries a hash of them (`identity`), so a change reaches everyone as `member.updated`. The keys themselves come on request, a few members at a time, as a Dens first needs them: as a DM opens, a group lists its members (M16.3), or members join a call (M16.2).
- *Keeping them.* A member's Dens keeps each identity the first time it needs it, by den and member, trusting it on first use as SSH does a host's key, and checks every one it meets after against what it kept. One that differs is a change: the Dens keeps the new one and says so wherever that member shows (below). So a den that hands out keys of its own must do it the first time two members meet, both ways, and keep doing it for as long as they talk, which any comparison catches.
- *Codes.* Options: (a) a code for each pair of members, from an exchange in commit-first order, as a DM's is now, which stays short since the den gets one blind guess, but takes an exchange through the den for each pair who meet, 45 for a call of ten, and again whenever one starts over; (b) a code for each member, from their identity, long enough that the den can't make one to match. Pick (b): nothing passes through the den to make or show it, and it's the same code for everyone who compares with that member, who reads it out once to a call or a group. It's 20 digits in groups of four, made from the identity, the member's ID and the den's ID by 5,200 rounds of SHA-512, as Signal makes its safety numbers, so an identity made to match one takes about 10^20 tries of those. Manage's You in this den shows the member's own.
- *Comparing.* Whenever they choose, from the profile beside a DM, a member's profile card, the DM's lock, a group's members (M16.3) and a call's lock (M16.2): the member's own code, to read out, and a box for the other's, typed as they read it out, so a check can't pass by copying one's own screen, as now. A match marks the other member verified, with a green circled check beside their name, until their identity changes; digits that don't match say someone may be in the middle. Each compares the other, as each typed the other's half before, and the advice on where to compare stays as it is.
- *Nothing for the den to see.* Comparing sends nothing: the code comes from the identity the Dens kept, and the mark stays with the member. Now a member's copy of a DM's key reaches the den only once they've checked, and showing the digits asks the den for the exchange, so the den learns who compares, and when, which would tell it whose DMs it could tamper with unseen.
- *Where the marks are kept.* Options: (a) each device keeps its own; (b) one record of the identities a member's Dens kept, and those they verified, sealed with their seal and kept on the den, written as it changes; (c) the same record, written every hour by each of their connected devices whether it changed or not, padded to a power of four from 16 KiB. Pick (c): a comparison reaches the member's other devices within the hour, while the den sees each device write once an hour whatever happened, where (b) would tell it when a member compared. Devices merge it as they write it, against its revision, and two that kept different identities for someone show it as a change.
- *A change.* When a member's identity changes, every DM and group with them shows a line where it did, "Bob's keys changed", with Compare, and their mark goes. Where the member had verified them, the conversation holds: the composer says their keys changed since the two compared, with Compare and Send anyway, and sends nothing until one is chosen. A call shows the change beside them (M16.2). The member's own devices say they started over, as now.
- *DM keys.* A DM's key is 32 random bytes, made by either member's Dens when the DM has no live key both can use: before its first message, after a member started over, or after an identity changed. It's sealed with HPKE to each member's identity, bound to the den, the DM, the key and the member, and its record names both identities by their hashes and is signed by its maker's. A Dens uses a key only once the signature checks against the identity it kept for its maker, or its own, and the record names the identity it kept for each member; so a den that gave one side keys of its own must give both, and a key that names another identity for this member says someone may be in the middle. A DM opens at once, with nothing to type, and a member whose Dens is away reads it once back. Each DM has one live key, as now; two made at once see `409 key_exists`, and the slower takes the live one. Messages, files and reactions are sealed under it as now.
- *The DMs there are.* Options: (a) move every DM's key to its members' identities; (b) leave them: a key both members checked stays live, and its history reads as it does, from each member's copy sealed with their seal, and only a DM that needs a new key gets one sealed to identities. Pick (b): nothing to move, and those keys were compared as they were made. An exchange still running, or checked by one member alone, retires as M16.1's migration runs (`restarted`), so its DM's next message makes a key the new way; what the member who checked sent under it stays theirs alone, as it would have. Those comparisons checked DM keys, not identities, so nobody starts verified.
- *On the page.* A DM no longer waits on a check: its composer shows at once, and the DM list and its tab lose their Check marks. The lock in its header, and the note at its start, say it's end-to-end encrypted, with Compare.
- *Approval* stays as it is: the commit-first exchange between the new device and one of the member's others, 16 digits each way typed into each other, then the seal handed over, from which the new device makes the identity. A device the den slipped past it would take the seal, and with it every DM, and no comparison of codes would show it, since the DMs themselves were never touched; so it stays the one check nobody can skip. Fewer digits would do, since the den gets one blind guess, but an updated device would then show fewer than one that isn't asks for.
- *Older Dens.* An older Dens publishes no identity, so a DM with its member that needs a new key waits, saying their Dens needs an update, while one whose key both members hold goes on both ways. On an older den, which keeps no identities, a newer Dens does the same. A den with M16.1 starts no exchange for a DM any more (`400 invalid_field`), so an older Dens can't start a key the newer one couldn't use.
- *The protocol.* A member gains `identity`. New requests publish the member's identity and fetch others', and read and write the member's record, against its revision. M16.1's keys come in `ready` and in events of their own, each member seeing their own copy, beside `dm_keys`, whose copies an older Dens checks to be its own 72 bytes; a request makes one. Additive, so no version bump.

*M16.1 as built,* where it adds to the plan: the cryptography is `denproto/identity.go`. An identity's hash is SHA-256 of the hash of its X-Wing key and its Ed25519 key, so each signed key carries its maker's identity in 64 bytes (`maker`), from which a reader checks the signature against any identity it kept for the maker, the current one for sending and any of the eight before it for reading: without it, a DM's history from before its other member started over wouldn't check. A copy is HPKE's base mode with X-Wing, HKDF-SHA-256 and ChaCha20-Poly1305, 1,168 bytes, bound to the den, the DM, a random 16-byte ID its maker gives the key (`kid`), since the den's ID for it comes only once it's made, and the member. Base mode doesn't say who sealed a copy, so the statement commits to the key (`commit`, SHA-256 of the key and its ID), and a copy that opens to another is refused. The den keeps a signed key in `den_dm_keys`, its statement as JSON beside its signature, at stage `revealed`, and each member's copy where M1.7's are, so sending checks a copy as before; a key replaced after an identity changed retires as `restarted`, since the table checks a retired key's reason is one of M1.7's. Requests: `PUT /api/me/identity`, `GET /api/identities?members=`, `GET` and `POST /api/dms/{id}/signed-keys`, and `GET` and `PUT /api/me/record`; events `dm.signed_key` and `record.updated`; `ready`'s `signed_keys`, which a den older than M16.1 doesn't send at all, as a client tells it keeps no identities. The den takes a key only when it names the identities published now and its maker's signature checks, and refuses an exchange's start with `400 invalid_field`; the answer, reveal and seal of an exchange's key, which no live key needs once the migration ran, are gone. Leaving the den takes the member's identity and record, as starting over does. The record is JSON, the identities kept by member, with the hash, when it was kept, verified, held, the eight before and the latest sixteen changes; a device that finds an older revision on the den, as a den replaying one would show it, merges nothing. Each conn writes it from a random moment within `RecordEvery`, an hour, 20 seconds for a development instance, reading and merging before each write; and keeps it in `joined_dens.identities`, sealed with the data key, which M16.1's migration adds. A Dens keeps the identity of each member it shares a DM with at each snapshot and new DM, and one it opens comparing with. An opened signed key is kept in memory sealed with the seal, as M1.7's copies are, since opening its copy takes 70 microseconds of X-Wing. The page gets every key as `dm_keys`, signed ones marked, and what was kept as `identities`, by member, which the service sends again as the page event `identities`; signed keys' dividers say only where this member started over, the line where the other's keys changed saying the rest. A message under a key that names identities other than those kept is `locked: "keys"`. The local service's routes are `GET /api/dens/{den}/members/{member}/code`, `POST …/compare` and `POST …/send-anyway`. Go's tests check identities, codes, copies, statements and records against fixed cases, the den's rules for keys, identities and records, the client's keeping, merging, verdicts, codes, hold and Send anyway, two writing at once, a key from before M16.1 read and written, and the migration from v1.6.0's, v1.7.0's and v1.8.0's schemas; the page's tests check the gate, the lines, the dividers, comparing and the hold. The `codes` check runs as the plan says, in Chromium and Firefox; the browser checks' `checked_dm` is `open_dm`, which waits for both members' identities; the phone check's `dm-codes` screen, comparing from the DM's lock, takes the place of the DM waiting for its check; the den e2e writes a DM at once from both members and compares codes, in bash and PowerShell; the Android e2e's DM with desk opens at once and the two compare codes; and the lifecycle e2e checks a DM on v1.0.0 with a member on a second instance of it, which both read and write after the update, under the same key.

M16.2:

- *What the den forwards.* Each member's page seals every frame its encoders make, their voice's, their screen's and their share's sound's, before it's sent, and opens every frame it receives before it's decoded, with WebRTC's Encoded Transform (`RTCRtpScriptTransform`), in a worker of its own. The den goes on forwarding each packet's payload under its own headers: its SFU never reads one, budgets them by size alone and asks for keyframes over RTCP, so it needs no change, and the den hears and sees nothing of a call it carries.
- *The frames.* SFrame (RFC 9605), in its AES-128-GCM suite, which WebCrypto has: a header with the key's ID and a counter, then the frame sealed with 16 bytes of tag, about 8 kbps on a voice at 50 frames a second. A member's key has one derived from it for each of their tracks, so no two tracks share a counter. A frame that doesn't open, names a counter its key has had, or comes in the clear on a member's track, is dropped. The page's tests take RFC 9605's own cases.
- *Keys.* A member's Dens makes their key for a call, 16 random bytes, as they join, and a new one whenever anyone joins or leaves, so one who joins can't open what the den might have kept from before, nor one who left what comes after. It seals the key to each other member's identity, as a DM's is, bound to the den, the channel, the member and the key's ID, and signs one statement naming each member it sealed to, with their identity's hash, so the checks of a DM's key hold here too. It goes over the den socket as `voice.key`; the den passes each member theirs alone, while both are in the call, and keeps the latest from each while the call lasts, so a member gets theirs as their call starts or is taken back, as with `voice.talking`. The receiving Dens checks it, opens it and hands the key to its page: the browser holds a call's keys, and only while it lasts, where it holds no other. The service sends the page the call's keys whole each time one changes, so one the page missed costs nothing, and the page asks for them again on a frame under a key it lacks. A page sends under its new key a second after it's made, and opens the last one's frames for ten seconds more.
- *Whom it seals to.* Every member of the call as the page shows it: the den chooses who's in a call, so a member it added in secret would have to show among them to hear a thing.
- *The soundboard* is the den's own track, which it plays its sounds on, so it stays in the clear: the page opens nothing on the `soundboard` stream, and takes nothing in the clear on any other.
- *Browsers.* Firefox has had `RTCRtpScriptTransform` since 117 and Chromium since 141; Chromium's older `createEncodedStreams` is left out, since every target browser has the newer. The page offers a call on a den that seals them only where the browser has it, and otherwise says why. Android's WebView has it from 141, so below that the page's place asks for a newer WebView before a call, as it does below its floor of 111. The worker is an asset beside the voice worklet, named to the page as the worklet is, and the CSP needs nothing more: a worker from the page's own assets falls under `script-src 'self'`.
- *A device without the seal*, as after a recovery code until the member types it, can't open a call's keys, so the page asks for the seal before it joins.
- *Codes in a call.* Options: (a) one code for the whole call, as Discord's Voice Privacy Code: the same for everyone who sees the same members and keys, so one comparison checks everyone at once, but 30 digits long, since a den that slipped in keys of its own could otherwise make it match, changing with every join and leave, and proving only that everyone sees the same call; (b) each member's own code, from M16.1. Pick (b): the call's lock, beside its state, opens the member's code to read out, and each other member with their mark or Compare, and a comparison made there holds in every call, DM and group with that member after. The call's list shows the mark beside each member verified, and that one's keys changed.
- *Older Dens.* The den's info says it seals calls (`sealed_calls`); on an older den, which doesn't, a call goes as now, and the call's lock says its calls aren't end-to-end encrypted. A den with M16.2 seals every call: it refuses a `voice.join` that doesn't say it seals (`"sealed": true`) with `update_needed`, since an older page would send in the clear, which every other page drops, and would hear nothing; an older page shows it as a call that failed.
- *What's logged.* As now, by ID alone: each key a Dens makes and passes on, by its ID, never its bytes; and in the page's notes, the frames dropped, by count.
- *The protocol.* `voice.join` gains `sealed`; the client frame `voice.key` carries the key's ID, each member's sealed copy, the statement and its signature, and the den's event `voice.key` the sender, with what's for that member alone; the den's info gains `sealed_calls`. `voice.key` has a limit of its own, 30 in 10 seconds a member, since each join and leave sends one from every member, and a call's keys for 30 members fit in a frame. Additive, so no version bump.

*M16.2 as built,* where it adds to the plan: the cryptography is `denproto/callkeys.go`. A call's key is 16 random bytes with a random 16-byte ID (`kid`), as M16.1's keys have; its copy, 1,152 bytes, its commitment and its statement have contexts of their own, so none passes for a DM key's, and the statement names the members it was sealed to, its maker not among them, beside its maker's identity in 64 bytes (`maker`), as a signed DM key carries it. M16.3:

- *A group DM.* A DM of three to ten members of the den, as Discord's group DMs. Any member makes one from the DM list's +, picking the others, or from a DM's profile, with Add people, which makes a group of the two and those added and leaves the DM as it is. It may have a name, which its members change; one without is named by its members. It lists among the DMs, and beside it, where a DM's profile shows, are its members, each with their mark or Compare, and Add people and Leave.
- *Who changes it.* Any member adds others, up to ten in all, each a member whose Dens has an identity there; the one who made it removes members, and once they've left, the member who's been in it longest; anyone leaves. A member who leaves the den, or is removed or banned, leaves its groups.
- *Changes the den can't make.* Each change is a statement signed by the member who made it: the group made, with its members; a member added, removed or leaving; a new name. The den keeps them in order and sends them with the group, and every member's Dens checks each against the members it had, that its signer was one and could make it, and knows the group's members from them alone: someone the den lists without a member's signed add gets no key and shows nowhere. The den's own changes are removals alone, of members who left the den, which take nobody in. Each change shows in the group as a line, "Alice added Carol". WhatsApp's servers could once add members to groups, since its changes weren't signed (Rösler, Mainka and Schwenk, 2018).
- *Keys.* A group has a key for each membership. The member whose change makes a membership makes its key, 32 random bytes, sealed to each member's identity and signed, as a DM's is; after a member leaves or the den takes one out, the first remaining member's Dens to send makes it. A member's new identity makes a new key too. Options: (a) a member added holds the keys from their joining on, as in Signal and WhatsApp; (b) whoever adds them seals the group's earlier keys to them too, as Discord shows a group's history to those added. Pick (a): what the others wrote, they wrote before that member was there to read it.
- *Messages.* Sealed under the group's live key as a DM's are, and signed by their author's identity inside what's sealed, since with a key every member holds, a member and the den together could otherwise write as another; an edit is signed again, and so is each member's set of reactions. Files, replies, edits, deletes, tasks and read state work as in a DM. Every message counts toward each other member's unread count and notifies as a DM's does, under Direct messages; no polls, and no @everyone.
- *The name* is sealed under the group's key, so the den never reads it, nor any it had.
- *Where they're kept.* A group DM is a channel of a new kind, `group_dm`, with a table of its members and one of its signed changes, in order, and its keys in the DMs' tables, which already keep a copy for each member. SQLite changes a table's checks only by making the table again, and `den_channels` allows only its three kinds, so M16.3's migration makes it again with the fourth: with foreign keys off for that step, as SQLite's procedure for it says, since dropping the old table with them on would take every message with it, and `foreign_key_check` before it commits.
- *Limits.* A member is in at most 100 group DMs, which count toward no channel limit, as DMs don't; making one, and each change, count toward the member's other writes.
- *The protocol.* An older Dens closes its connection, as the den breaking the protocol, on a channel of more than two members or a message with more than two members' sets of reactions. So M16.3 raises the protocol to version 2, which the den serves beside 1 (`Dens-Protocol-Range: 1-2`): to a Dens speaking 1, it sends nothing of group DMs, so an older device of a member in groups goes on without them, while a Dens speaking 2 gets them as channels, in `ready` and events, with their members, changes and keys. Requests make a group and its changes.

*M16.3 as built,* where it adds to the plan: the cryptography and the rules are `denproto/groups.go`, which the den and every member's Dens both check changes by. M16.4:

- *The setting.* Any member of a DM or a group sets its messages to disappear 1 hour, 1 day, 1 week or 4 weeks after they're sent, or Off, which a new one starts at; it applies to messages sent after, and a line in the conversation says who set it, and when. A time counts from sending, which the den knows and both sides see alike. The den knows the setting, as it must to delete them.
- *Deleting.* Each message carries when it goes (`expires_at`). The den deletes it then, as its author's delete would: its row with its files and reactions, `secure_delete` on, `message.deleted` to its members, and the change log, so an install that was away drops it as it comes back. It runs on the timer that ends polls (M15.4), and as the den starts, so one restored from a backup deletes what passed meanwhile. Each member's Dens also drops what it holds past its time, from memory, the cache and its notifications, so a den offline shows none of it from the cache. A reply to one says it's gone, as for a deleted one; an edit doesn't extend it; and the den's retention takes what passes its period first.
- *Keys.* Options: (a) deleting alone, under the DM's or group's key as now: gone from the den and every device, which is what members ask disappearing messages for; but a den changed to keep copies would keep their sealed keys too, which anyone who later held a member's DM recovery key, or a device of theirs, could open; (b) forward secrecy as well: each device has keys of its own for disappearing messages, made at random, never from the seal, published signed by the member's identity and changed each day, and a disappearing conversation's key, changed each day too, is sealed to each of its members' devices, never their identities, each device forgetting a key once everything it sealed has passed. Then neither a recovery key nor a device taken later opens what passed, wherever copies went; but a new device reads none of a conversation from before it, every key goes to every device, and it's a second way of sealing keys beside identities. Pick (a), with (b) in loose ideas: the privacy model leaves compromised devices and targeted investigations outside what Dens protects against, and (b) guards against a den that keeps what it should delete and later gets a member's recovery key or device.
- *The protocol.* A request sets a DM's or a group's time, which `ready` and its events carry (`expires_after`), and a message carries its `expires_at`. Additive.
