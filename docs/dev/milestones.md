# Dens — Milestones

What each milestone built, and the plan for the one under way; ideas no milestone has taken up yet are in [loose-ideas.md](loose-ideas.md). The design they build toward is [design.md](design.md), with each deployment form's in [installed.md](installed.md), [flatpak.md](flatpak.md) and [android.md](android.md): a section named here, such as Global shortcuts, is one of theirs unless it's one of this file's.

A milestone's plan is written here first, and reviewed before anything is built. Once the milestone is done, what its plan still teaches, such as spike findings and measurements, moves into the design docs' sections, and the rest goes; Git keeps it.

Each milestone ends usable on its own and is tested on Linux and Windows with all four target browsers. All platform-specific code lands in M0, while the seams are still cheap to change; later milestones are shared code, but for M7's keys and the helper's start at login, M9's Flatpak, and M12's Android app and the helper's notifications.

M13 and M14 get their plans once M12 is done.

The spike on the phones (`spikes/android`) tried android.md's design on Android's emulator, a Galaxy S24 Ultra and a Pixel 9 Pro, all on Android 17, and android.md has what it found. It changed four things: a call needs the page held as shown to the WebView, or Chromium freezes it a minute after the screen goes off; a call's sound plays as media unless its audio is made after the microphone; the updater hands each update to Android's installer, since Play Protect calls an app that updates itself without a prompt harmful; and a sleeping phone stops Go's timers, which M14 deals with.

**M12 steps.** M12 lands as four pull requests, each built and committed in layers, tested in the emulator and on the S24 Ultra, and checked in the four target browsers where it touches the page:

| --- | --- | --- |

M12.1:

- *The build.* `scripts/build.sh` builds Dens for `android/arm64`, and `android/amd64` for the emulator, with cgo through NDK r30 for API 31 and `-checklinkname=0`; and the APK around it without Gradle, from aapt2, kotlinc, d8, zipalign and apksigner, as the spike does in about 11 seconds. vendor.sh pins the NDK, the SDK's build-tools, platform and platform-tools, Kotlin and a JDK. A check against the SDK's API database stands in for Android Lint's NewApi, which a build without Gradle lacks: in the spike it caught two calls that crash Android 12. Releases are `chat.dens.Dens`; development builds are `chat.dens.Dens.dev`, labelled Dens (development), under a key of their own, so Play Protect's verdict on one never reaches the other.
- *The form.* `layout.Form` gains Android, a session form as the Flatpak's is: its paths are under the app's files and cache, which the shell names in the environment, and it publishes its own lifecycle state and migrates under its lifecycle lock (lifecycle.md). Android's seams join `internal/platform/host` as `_android.go` files: locked memory falls back to ordinary memory past Android's 64 KiB; the data key comes from the shell over a pipe; and the control endpoint lives in the app's files. `TZ` and `TMPDIR` come from the shell.
- *The shell*, in `android/`: Kotlin with no AndroidX, as the spike's is. A foreground service of the `specialUse` type runs Dens from the native library directory and starts it again when it dies, and `BOOT_COMPLETED` and `MY_PACKAGE_REPLACED` start the service. A Keystore key, in StrongBox where the phone has one, wraps the data key. The shell asks the service for a pairing link and loads it, and holds one stream with the service, which M12.2 to M12.4 use.
- *The WebView.* No JavaScript bridge, and every address but the page's opens in the phone's browser, YouTube's player in its frame aside; the file picker; downloads, named from their `Content-Disposition` and saved to Downloads; full screen; Back through the page's history, then out; and the keyboard, with the shell applying the insets edge to edge. The app checks the WebView's version against a floor, since the page's CSP needs `'wasm-unsafe-eval'`, and asks for an update below it.
- *The page* copies through `document.execCommand('copy')` where the Clipboard API is refused, as it is in the WebView, and says so when that fails too. It already hides sharing where there's no `getDisplayMedia`.
- *Docs.* android.md as built, and AGENTS.md's table gains `android/` and the form.

M12.2:

- *The microphone.* When the page asks for it, the shell asks Android for `RECORD_AUDIO` if it hasn't been granted, adds the microphone's type to its service, then grants it; ten seconds after Android's recording callback says the page stopped, it drops the type.
- *Held through the screen going off.* While the page has the microphone, the shell tells the WebView its window shows, whatever the screen does, so Chromium never freezes a call's page; after the call, the WebView sees its window as it is. In every browser, the page plays its audio elements again when it thaws from a freeze, on `resume`.
- *The call's sound on the call's route.* Where audio may start without a tap, as in the WebView, the page makes its call's audio context once the microphone is open and plays the members' voices through it, so Android plays the call as a call, under the call's volume and on the route the shell sets. The page learns it may from the service, as one of the things its form can do. The spike showed contexts made after the microphone play as a call's; voices played through one is this step's to confirm.
- *The route.* The shell routes a call with `setCommunicationDevice`: to a Bluetooth headset when one is connected, asking for `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT` then, and otherwise the loudspeaker, with the others as buttons on the call's notification.
- *Logs.* The page's notes on a call add what it sends a second and how far its voice processor runs behind, which tells the Pixel's rougher sound off screen apart from Opus's rate adapting on mobile data.

M12.3:

- *What's notified.* Notifications stay off until the member turns them on, as design.md has it: DMs and mentions, each on its own, with a den or a channel muted from its menu. Today the page notifies nothing, on any form. The service decides, since a page may be hidden, frozen or closed: a DM, or a mention of the member, in a den and channel they haven't muted, unless a page shows that channel on screen. Each goes to one place, with the den, channel and message, the sender's name, and the message's start unless the member turns that off; nothing about it is logged but its IDs. The settings are the service's, so every browser paired with the instance shares them, as it shares the voice keys.
- *Where it shows.* On Android, the shell posts it. On a desktop, a page that's open but not showing the channel shows it through the browser's notifications, which the setting asks the browser for as it's turned on, and a click brings that tab forward at the message. With no such page, the helper shows it as the desktop's own notification, and a click opens the page at the message, as `dens open` does. Each page tells the service whether it's on screen and whether it may notify, so a notification shows once, and reading the channel clears it wherever it shows.
- *The helper*, which runs in the desktop session from login and holds a stream with each instance, takes notifications on that stream (`notify`) beside the keys, and shows them through the freedesktop notification service on Linux and as a toast on Windows, which needs Dens's own app ID on a Start menu shortcut that the installer makes. The Flatpak's service, in the member's session, shows them through the notification portal it already holds.
- *On Android* the shell posts each in a Messages channel, grouped by den, after asking for `POST_NOTIFICATIONS` the first time there's one to post, and a tap opens the page at the message.
- *Docs.* design.md gains a section on notifications, in place of its line saying they come later; protocol.md is untouched, since nothing changes between client and den; and the public docs say how to turn them on, on each platform.

M12.4:

- *The APK in each release*, as `releases/vX.Y.Z/android-arm64.apk`, listed in `checksums.txt` under its cosign bundle as the binaries are; release.md's layout gains it. CI signs it with the APK key, which it keeps encrypted, with copies offline in two places, since every update must carry it.
- *The updater.* The service checks the root `version` as the installed form's update check does, downloads the new APK, and checks it against the release's checksums and their signature. The shell then posts that an update is ready, and its tap hands the APK to Android's installer through `PackageInstaller`, which asks the member to confirm. The app asks for `REQUEST_INSTALL_PACKAGES` alone. When F-Droid installed the app, the updater steps aside; when Android refuses an update as from an unverified developer, the notification says how to turn the advanced flow back on.
- *The install guide*, a page for Android: downloading the APK, the browser's warning, letting the browser install apps, Play Protect's scan, Auto Blocker on newer Galaxy phones, Never auto sleeping apps, and that a Restricted battery setting keeps Dens from staying connected; Google's advanced flow where it applies; and that phones without Google's certification, such as those on GrapheneOS or LineageOS, check nothing.

The tests that touch Android's seams run in the emulator, through the harness.
- Page tests: audio elements playing again after `resume`; the call's audio context made after the microphone where the page may; the copy's fallback; and turning notifications on, with the browser's permission refused and granted.
- In the container, both browsers: a DM's notification from a tab in the background, and its click.
- The Android e2e (`./scripts/test.sh -android-e2e`), from the spike's container and scripts, with the emulator pinned in vendor.sh: the APK installed, paired and joined to a den in `dens-browsers` behind Caddy; chatting and a photo; a call with a fake microphone on each side, held through two minutes off screen, one of them silent; a notification from the background, and its tap; and an update to a fixture release.

On Windows, and on Linux under GNOME and KDE, installed and as the Flatpak: a DM's notification with Dens's tab in the background and with no tab open, and its click.
