# Dens — Milestones

What each milestone built, and the plan for the one under way; ideas no milestone has taken up yet are in [loose-ideas.md](loose-ideas.md). The design they build toward is [design.md](design.md), with each deployment form's in [installed.md](installed.md), [flatpak.md](flatpak.md) and [android.md](android.md): a section named here, such as Global shortcuts, is one of theirs unless it's one of this file's.

A milestone's plan is written here first, and reviewed before anything is built. Once the milestone is done, what its plan still teaches, such as spike findings and measurements, moves into the design docs' sections, and the rest goes; Git keeps it.

Each milestone ends usable on its own and is tested on Linux and Windows, in Chromium and Firefox. As a rule, each step of a milestone is tested by the automated checks: the harnesses, the Android e2e in the emulator, and the browser checks in the container's Chromium and Firefox. The manual tests, on the phones and in browsers, run together once, at the milestone's end. A spike runs its own trials, and a test that can't wait for the end gets an exception. All platform-specific code lands in M0, while the seams are still cheap to change; later milestones are shared code, but for M7's keys and the helper's start at login, M9's Flatpak, and M12's Android app and the helper's notifications.

**M15, emoji, reactions and polls.** A den's members get the ways of answering each other that other chat apps have: the den's own emoji, reactions and polls, and @everyone for staff. Animated pictures go first, since the den's animated emoji take their path: cropping or scaling an animation makes every frame again, which the member's local service does in Go. Emoji are the den's named files, as the soundboard's sounds are (M14.3), and the list of Unicode's emoji the picker shows is the one the den checks reactions against. Events and their badges wait for M16 (below).

**M15 steps.** M15 lands as four pull requests, each built and committed in layers and tested by the automated checks (see M15 testing); the manual tests, on the S24 Ultra and in Chromium and Firefox on Windows, wait for M15's end:

M15.1:

- *What stops them now.* The den takes only still pictures, and the crop dialog draws the framed part of an image's first frame into a new image, so an animation would keep its first frame alone. Cropping or scaling an animation means making every frame again.
- *Where the frames are made.* Options: (a) the media module, which would need FFmpeg's GIF codec, its WebP decoder, and the filters that crop, scale and make palettes, none of which it's built with (it's built without avfilter); (b) the page, decoding the frames with WebCodecs' `ImageDecoder` and writing the GIF with code of its own; (c) the member's local service, in Go: the standard library reads and writes GIF, and `golang.org/x/image`, already a dependency, decodes WebP and scales. Pick (c): nothing new to build or depend on, and the den checks what it makes as it checks any GIF.
- *Making one.* The crop dialog frames the first frame, as for a still. For an animated GIF or WebP the page then sends the original, with the frame's rectangle in its pixels, to the upload the service already strips on its way to the den, and the service: reads the frames' sizes and count, refusing an animation past a budget before decoding any; composes each frame over those before it, as the format's disposal and blending say; crops and scales each to the picture's size; gives each a palette of its own, of at most 256 colors, a pixel that's mostly transparent becoming GIF's transparent one; and writes a GIF with the original's timing and looping. An animated picture is 256 pixels square, and an animated banner 600 × 200, half a still's sides, since each of a GIF's frames costs its full size: at most 2 MiB and 4 MiB, and one past that is refused as too long. An animated PNG, rare among pictures, frames as a still of its first frame, as now.
- *The den's check.* A picture is a still, as now, or an animated GIF, square, 64 to 256 pixels and at most 2 MiB; a banner a still, or an animated GIF three times as wide as high, 300 to 600 pixels wide and at most 4 MiB. An animated picture is a GIF alone, since only the service makes them. A member's picture says whether it's animated (`animated`, beside its size), from what the den keeps of every image.
- *Still, and playing.* The den makes every image's preview from its first frame, so a page shows an animated picture's preview while it isn't playing and its file while it is; the client's cache keeps both, as it keeps any picture.
- *Animations.* Options: (a) animated pictures always play; (b) they play only under the pointer; (c) a setting picks. Pick (c): Appearance's *Animations*, Always or On hover, which the browser keeps, as it keeps the theme; Always unless the system asks for less motion (`prefers-reduced-motion`), and On hover then. It covers pictures and banners, and from M15.2 emoji. On hover plays a picture while the pointer is over its row, not over the picture alone: its message, its member in the list or a call, its DM in the list; a profile card, and the profile beside a DM, play theirs while they show, since the member opened them. A touch screen has no hover, so there On hover plays pictures in profiles alone. A GIF in a message stays as it is: its preview in the list, the file in the viewer.

*M15.1 as built,* where it adds to the plan: the service's path is `media.CropAnimation` (`animate.go`, with `frames.go` reading frames and `quantize.go` picking palettes), which `denclient.UploadAnimated` runs one at a time, behind the upload route the page already uses, given `picture` (`avatar` or `banner`) and `crop` (`x,y,width,height` in the image's pixels). The budget the plan left open: a file of at most 32 MiB, 1,000 frames, a canvas and each frame of at most 4 megapixels, and 2^28 pixels decoded and scaled in all; the worst it allows took under 3 seconds on a fast desktop (a Ryzen 7 9800X3D). Three picks the plan didn't make: the GIF is the part framed at its own size within a picture's, never enlarged, so a small GIF isn't blown up; each frame holds only the rectangle that changed since its pixels were last drawn, give or take 4 levels a channel, so a picture is as large as its motion; and a frame of more colors than its palette is dithered, since the check's gradient banded without: twice the size for a gradient's frame, a few percent for a busy clip's. A GIF is read as browsers read one, cut short or with junk after a frame, a still GIF or WebP becomes a GIF of one frame, and a panic in the WebP decoder refuses the file. The page tells an animated GIF or WebP by its bytes (`animatedImage` in `crop.jsx`: two whole frames, or VP8X's flag), rounds the frame to whole pixels (`wholeRect`), and the crop dialog says the picture keeps moving, with its limits. Animations is `animations.js`: rows are `HoverRow`s, which a hovering pointer plays and a finger doesn't; profiles are `Shown`; and `usePicture` gives a picture its file, its preview, or its preview with the file over it while it plays. Beyond the plan's rows, a reply's quote plays with its message, the member's own panel, a DM's header and the delete dialog's line play as rows, and the member's own profile plays while they edit it. The den has `animated` on `denproto.Image`, from `den_files.animated`, and the plan's rules in `checkPicture`. Go's tests compose each format's frames against references, a GIF's through the standard library and a WebP's by its specification's formula, from lossless frames a test encoder writes, with libwebp's lossy fixtures, and check the crop, scale, timing, loops, palettes, dithering, transparency, the budget and the size, with `FuzzCropAnimation` over the readers; the page's tests check Animations' default and choice, rows on hover and under a finger, the bytes' check and the whole rectangle. The `pictures` check has each member crop a GIF and a WebP into a picture and a banner, in Chromium and Firefox, and checks Animations' first choice with and without less motion, each kind of row under the pointer and away from it, a profile open, a tap, and Always.

M15.2:

- *The den's emoji.* An emoji is one of the den's named files, of kind `emoji`: a name, a file, and who added it and when. Its name is 2 to 32 of `a-z`, `0-9` and `_`, lowercased on entry as a username is, so `:name:` reads the same in every message; a sound's name stays free-form. Staff add an emoji in Manage's new Emoji section, under This den beside the Soundboard, by a file and a name the page suggests from the file's, and there rename or delete each. An emoji's file opens to every member, where a sound's opens to staff alone, since every page draws it. Like a sound's, it's the den's: retention leaves it, and it counts toward the den's space, not its uploader's.
- *Its file.* An emoji shows at 48 pixels at most, so it's at most 128 on its longer side, for dense screens. The page draws a still one into a new PNG of that size, as it crops a picture, which leaves the original's metadata behind; an animated GIF or WebP becomes a GIF of that size by M15.1's path. The den checks the sides and the size: at most 256 KiB, or 512 KiB animated.
- *The owner's limit.* Manage's Limits gains Emoji beside the Soundboard: how many emoji the den keeps, 50 by default, 0 to 500, 0 turning emoji off and keeping them. It holds as an emoji is added, as the soundboard's limits do, and the den keeps it in a column of its row (`emojis`) and sends it in its info.
- *In the text.* Options: (a) the text holds the emoji's name, `:custom_heart:`, as Slack's does; (b) its name and ID, `<:custom_heart:123>`, as Discord's does, which the composer writes as it sends. Pick (a): a notification's text, an older page, a DM's sealed text and Copy text read it as it was written, the markdown subset gains nothing its readers can't read, and the den never parses emoji. The cost: renaming an emoji turns its uses back into text, and a name deleted and given to another emoji shows the new one in old messages, which the Emoji section says beside Rename and Delete.
- *The rule,* the page's alone: `:`, then 2 to 32 of `a-z`, `0-9` and `_` in any case, then `:`, the first colon not after a letter, digit or `_` and the second not before one, so a time such as 10:30:45 holds none; never in code or a link. A name the den has shows its emoji, `:name:` in its tip, in a square box of the line's height that the picture fills as it loads, so the list never moves; one it hasn't stays text. A message of nothing but emoji, ten at most, Unicode's or the den's, shows them at 48 pixels. They show wherever the markdown subset does: messages, reply quotes, bios and channel descriptions; and a DM, which lives on its den, has its den's.
- *Fetching them.* The client fetches the den's emoji files as their list arrives and changes, into its cache, rather than as messages show them, so no fetch tells the den that an emoji showed in a DM. The owner's count bounds what that takes; the install's own den isn't cached, its files being on its disk.
- *Unicode's emoji.* The picker lists them, and the den checks reactions against them (M15.3), from one list. A generator reads Unicode's `emoji-test.txt` and CLDR's English annotations, both pinned in `scripts/vendor.sh`, and writes each fully qualified emoji with its name, its group, its keywords and whether it takes a skin tone, for the page and for `denproto`; both files are committed, and CI writes them again and fails on a difference, as it does for RNNoise's module. Emoji draw in the system's font, as the rest of the text does, so no image set ships. The list stops at the newest Emoji version, which each line of `emoji-test.txt` names, that Windows 11 and Noto Color Emoji draw, and a phone too old for one shows a box. Unicode's license goes in the notices.
- *The picker.* A smiley beside the composer's box, drawn as the page's icons are, opens it: above the composer, and on the phone layout as a sheet from the screen's foot, as the page's menus are there. At its top is a search, over names and keywords; below it, a block of the member's most used emoji, up to four rows, then the den's, by name, then Unicode's, by group; and a skin tone, which the browser keeps, for those that take one. A pick goes in at the caret: a Unicode emoji as itself, so the text holds the emoji, and a den's as `:name:`, which the box, being plain text, shows by name.
- *Most used.* The browser keeps a score for each emoji the member uses: picked in the picker, put in by completing, sent in a message, a phone keyboard's among them, or added as a reaction (M15.3). Each use adds one, and every score halves in two weeks, so a favorite stays at the top while one used once fades. The picker's first block and the quick reactions (M15.3) both go by it. A den's emoji counts in that den alone, and goes as it's deleted; a few hundred scores are kept, the lowest dropping first.
- *Completing.* The composer completes nothing yet. In a channel or a DM, typing `:` and two characters where an emoji may start opens a list above the box: the den's emoji that match, then Unicode's. In a channel, typing `@` where a mention may start opens the members who can see it, matched by username and display name. ↑ and ↓ move through the list, Enter or Tab puts the pick in, and Escape closes it; a tap picks on a touch screen.
- *Animated emoji* play as Animations says (M15.1): always, or on hover over their message, and in the picker under the pointer.
- *The protocol.* The den's emoji come in `ready`, and their changes as `emoji.created`, `emoji.updated` and `emoji.deleted`, each with its name, its file, whether it's animated, its size, and who added it and when. Staff add, rename and delete them as they do sounds, an emoji taking an upload of theirs as its file. The count comes in the den's info as `emoji_limits`, and `emoji_name_taken` refuses a name another emoji has, as `sound_name_taken` does. An older page shows `:name:` as text, and an older den has no emoji to send.

*M15.2 as built,* where it adds to the plan: the generator's inputs are Emoji 18.0's `emoji-test.txt` and CLDR 49's `annotations/en.xml` and `annotationsDerived/en.xml`, cut at Emoji 16.0, which `scripts/emoji.sh` names (`UPTO`): Windows 11 has drawn 16.0 since 24H2, and 17.0 only as an update rolls out. It writes 1,906 emoji for the page, 323 of them with skin tones, as `internal/ui/assets/emoji.json`, 138 KB, which the page fetches as its picker or a completion first needs it, from the address the shell names (`data-emoji`), rather than carrying it in its script; and every fully qualified emoji for `denproto` (`emojilist.go`, `IsEmoji`), a Go test checking the two agree. The protocol's emoji carries its file as the `image` a profile's picture is, with its size and `animated`; `emoji_limits` is a pointer in `denproto.Den`, sent at 0 too, so a client tells emoji off from an older den. The den takes a still PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP, though the page sends only PNGs, and a sound and an emoji may share a name, each kind having its own. Off means messages show names as text and the picker and completion offer none. The client keeps the images ahead of their use in the cache (`fillEmoji`), as their list arrives and changes and whenever a page asks for one the cache let go of, and serves them from the cache alone while emoji are on; while they're off, Manage's list fetches each as it shows. The cache's files gain an owner of a named file (`named`), so its format is 3, and a cache of format 2 starts again from empty once, on the update. The page's parts are `emoji.js` (the rule, emoji alone, search, the skin tone, the most used and completion), `denemoji.jsx` (`EmojiContext`, which the den's page provides, and `DenEmoji`) and `emoji.jsx` (the picker, completions and Manage's Emoji). Picks the plan didn't make: a use counts once, an emoji the picker or completion put in counting as it goes in and its message counting the rest; Shift keeps the picker open on a pick, and Enter in its search picks the first found; a completion puts a space after what it puts in, and Escape closes it until the text changes; a sheet's head stays at its top as a long sheet scrolls, every sheet's, so the picker's search stays in reach; where only emoji show, the picker, completion and a message of emoji alone, the system's color emoji font comes first (`font-emoji`), since Chromium draws DejaVu Sans's black and white emoji over a color font named after it; the rule takes a delimiter's span's start as no letter, so `_:name:_` is an emoji in italics; and a spoiler hides the images in it. `quickRow`, the quick reactions, is built and tested for M15.3. Go's tests check the names, the limits, `IsEmoji`, staff alone adding, renaming and deleting, the image's checks, the count, the files opening to every member and staying through retention, the sweep and the uploader leaving, an animated emoji's GIF at its size, the client's fill, a page's fetch costing the den nothing, an emoji the cache let go of bringing the rest, a deleted emoji's files leaving the cache live and while away, and the migration from v1.7.0's schema; the page's tests check the rule against its cases, emoji alone, search, the skin tone, the most used, completion and the owner's count in Limits' draft. The `emoji` check has alice add a still emoji and an animated one in Manage, and bob write, pick and complete them, in Chromium and Firefox; the phone check's `emoji` part walks the picker's sheet and completion at 390 pixels in its three modes.

M15.3:

- *What a reaction is.* A member's emoji on a message: one of Unicode's, from the list, or one of the den's, by its ID. A message holds at most 20 different emoji. Each reaction counts toward its member's other writes, and none notifies anyone.
- *In a channel.* The den checks a reaction against the list, or that it has the emoji, so a reaction carries no text: no bidi characters, no words spelled in letters. It keeps reactions in a table of their own, a row each, which go with their message, and with one of the den's emoji as it's deleted. History pages carry a message's reactions, each emoji with the members who reacted with it; live, `reaction.added` and `reaction.removed` each carry one member's one emoji, which a page applies to a set, so applying one twice changes nothing, where resending a message's whole set on every tap would spend the den's upload. The change log records the message as changed, as it does an edit, though the message's revision stays, so an install that was away fetches it again with its reactions, and an edit under way meets no conflict; the cache keeps reactions with their message and applies the events to it.
- *In a DM.* The den can't read a DM, and reactions in the clear would tell it how one member answered the other. Options: (a) DMs have no reactions; (b) each member's reactions on a DM's message are one set, which their client seals as it seals the DM's messages. Pick (b). The set is sealed with the DM key, bound to the den, the DM, the message, the member and the key's ID, and padded to one length, so its size doesn't say how many. The den keeps it in a row for each message and member, replaced whole against the revision it was made against, as an edit is, so a member's two devices don't drop each other's reactions; it sends it to both members as `dm.reactions`, and in history with its message. A client opening one keeps only emoji from the list and names the den's emoji may have, and drops anything else. The den sees that a member's reactions changed, and when, not what they are.
- *Staff* take one emoji's reactions off a channel's message, those of members of a lower rank, as their other powers reach (`reaction.cleared`); a DM's are its members' alone. A ban that deletes a member's recent messages takes their reactions of that time too.
- *On the page.* Under a message, after its files and covers, its reactions show as chips, each emoji with its count, marked where the member reacted; a click adds the member's or takes it back, and a tip says who: You first, then names, up to 15, then how many more. A + at the row's end opens the picker. A smiley beside a message's ⋯, shown as the ⋯ is, opens the picker to react, and the ⋯'s menu gains Add reaction. M15.4:

- *Making a poll.* The composer's + gains Poll, which opens it above the box, where a reply and files stand: 2 to 10 choices, each up to 80 characters of plain text, cleaned as display names are, emoji included; whether a member may pick more than one; how long it runs, 1 hour, 4 or 8 hours, 1 day by default, 3 days, or 1 or 2 weeks; who voted for what; and when the totals show. The message's text is the question, which a poll must have, so notifications, reply quotes and older pages read it. Polls go in channels alone, since a DM's would have its votes sealed from the den that counts them, for a poll of two.
- *Who voted for what:* Never, the default; Once it ends; or Always. It's fixed as the poll is posted, and shown on it, since a voter decides with it in mind, and an author mustn't reveal votes cast under Never. The den holds to it: it sends who voted for what to nobody under Never, to everyone at the end under Once it ends, and with the totals under Always. Never keeps it from members, not from the den, which keeps who voted for what to count each member once and let them change their vote; the poll's tip says so.
- *When the totals show:* As votes come in, the default; or After voting. As votes come in shows everyone the totals as they change, and a member changes or takes back their vote until the poll ends. After voting shows a member the totals once they've voted, and makes their vote final: changing it after seeing them would let a member move to the winning side. The poll says so before they vote. The den holds to this too: until the poll ends it sends the totals only to those who voted, and to the rest only how many have.
- *Voting.* The den checks that the poll is open, the choices are its own, one of them unless several are allowed, and under After voting that the member hasn't voted. A vote counts toward the member's other writes. The den sends the totals in batches every second or two, as it batches presence, so a busy poll doesn't send an event for each vote, each batch to those who may see it: the hub gains an audience of a poll's voters, beside its audiences of one member and of two. The change log records the poll's message as changed, as for a reaction.
- *Ending.* The den closes a poll at its end, by a timer for the next to end, set as the den starts and as polls are posted, and sends everyone the final totals, with who voted for what where the poll shows them then; the poll's author can end it early. A poll can't be edited once posted, neither its question nor its choices, so no vote stands under a question it wasn't cast for. It goes with its message, deleted or past the den's retention.
- *On the page.* Under its question, the poll shows each choice, with a bar of its share once the member may see the totals, and their own marked; how many voted; how long it has left, or that it ended; and its rules in a few words, with a tip: Anonymous, Votes show when it ends or Votes show; and with After voting, Results after you vote, votes final. Where the poll shows them, a choice's voters show on hover over its count, and by a tap on a touch screen.
- *The protocol.* A message's `poll`: its choices, whether several may be picked, its end, its two rules, whether it ended, how many voted, and where the member may see them, the totals, their own votes and the voters. Requests vote and end a poll; `poll.updated` carries batched totals, and `poll.ended` the final ones. An older page shows the question alone.
- *@everyone.* Staff, the moderators and the owner, send it in a channel. The den counts their `@everyone` as a mention of each member who can see the channel but the author, a row each as for a mention by name, so the unread mention counts, Home's and older pages' among them, need nothing new; a den of 500 members writes 500 rows for it, which go with the message. It counts while the text holds it and staff put it there: a co-editor who isn't staff can take it out of a shared message but can't put it in. Notifications come as a message arrives, so an edit that adds it notifies nobody, as for any mention. From anyone else it's a mention by name, of a member named `everyone`: the name is a valid username, so a den may have one, who keeps it, and new members can't take it.
