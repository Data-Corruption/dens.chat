# Dens — Milestones

What each milestone built, and the plan for the one under way; ideas no milestone has taken up yet are in [loose-ideas.md](loose-ideas.md). The design they build toward is [design.md](design.md), with each deployment form's in [installed.md](installed.md), [flatpak.md](flatpak.md) and [android.md](android.md): a section named here, such as Global shortcuts, is one of theirs unless it's one of this file's.

A milestone's plan is written here first, and reviewed before anything is built. Once the milestone is done, what its plan still teaches, such as spike findings and measurements, moves into the design docs' sections, and the rest goes; Git keeps it.

Each milestone ends usable on its own and is tested on Linux and Windows with all four target browsers. All platform-specific code lands in M0, while the seams are still cheap to change; later milestones are shared code, but for M7's keys and the helper's start at login, M9's Flatpak and M12's Android app.

M11.1:

- *Dialogs that stay open.* A dialog closes on a click outside it only when the press started outside it too, so selecting text that ends past its edge leaves it open. Today the backdrop closes on any click whose target is itself, which a press inside and a release outside produce; the same check is in five places (the settings, the channel and group settings, Manage den, the viewer and the comparison), and one helper replaces them.
- *A key's capture.* A click elsewhere while the settings wait for a key cancels the wait, as Escape does. Today only a key or a side button ends it, so the other keys' buttons stay disabled until the settings close.
- *Setting keys up with the desktop.* Where Set up with your desktop can't be pressed, the keys' card says why, and what to do, instead of only greying it out: as when the desktop already bound the keys this session and now holds none of them, which its own settings change.
- *The two tabs.* Each opens what it lists, so nobody writes in a den channel thinking it's a DM, and what was last open in it, so going to the den to copy something and back returns to the same DM. Den opens the text channel last open in that den, or its first when that one is gone or out of sight; Direct messages opens the DM last open there, or a blank panel, at its own address (`/den/<den>/dms`), when there's none or it was closed since, and when the open DM closes: never a DM the member didn't pick, as the most recently active might be. The DM list still sorts the most recently active first. Today the tabs only switch the list, and the channel or DM stays open beside it.
- *A DM's profile.* Beside a DM, the member list's place shows the other member's profile, as their card shows it: banner and picture, names, role, how long they've been in the den, and their bio at its full length; its toggle reads Profile.
- *The New divider* marks where a channel's unread messages started when it opened, and nothing else: a channel opened with nothing unread gets no divider for the messages that arrive while it's open. Today the divider follows the read position the view opened with, so the first message after it gets one even when it arrived in front of the member.
- *A deafened member* shows the crossed-out microphone beside the crossed-out headphones, wherever a call's members are listed, since deafening mutes too.
- *Enter in a code block.* With the caret in a code block, after an opening fence of three backticks with no closing one before it, Enter starts a new line, in the composer and in an edit; Ctrl+Enter sends from anywhere. The composer's guide says so.
- *The message box's focus.* The message box, and a message being edited, show their focus by a darker border alone. Today DaisyUI's focus darkens the border to full strength and draws a second ring just outside it.
- *The composer's +* lines up with the message box, its top border no wider than the rest.
- *Folding channel groups.* Clicking a group's name folds it, and clicking again unfolds it; the browser keeps which groups are folded, by den, as it keeps the player's place. A folded group still shows the channel that's open and any voice channel with a call in it, and its row shows its channels' unread state and mentions.
- *A video Firefox plays out of step.* A 1.8-second Android clip (H.264 and AAC) plays in Firefox as its picture, then its sound, where both should play at once. Reproduced in the browser checks' Firefox: sent full size, as the module's copy, and as the copy the page makes in Chromium, it lasts 3.97 seconds and its sound starts 2.2 seconds in, while the phone's own file plays in time, and Chromium plays all three in time. Every MP4 the driver writes gives its movie the timescale FFmpeg 9 picks, the least common multiple of its streams' (4,410,000 here, where the phone wrote 10,000), and Firefox reads the sound's 22 ms empty edit against the sound's 44,100 instead. The driver sets the movie's timescale to 1000, FFmpeg's default before, at which the same file plays in time in Firefox. A den strips again what it receives, so a video plays in time once the den's computer runs this version too, whatever its sender ran; videos sent before stay as they are.
- *The browser the docs recommend.* The install guide's What it runs on recommends Ungoogled Chromium, where Dens has had the fewest problems, and says why: a screen share can carry a tab's sound, which no Firefox-based browser shares, and the page makes a video's smaller copy itself, several times faster than the module. Privacy names it as its privacy-minded browser, where it named LibreWolf, and so does design.md's privacy model.

M11.2:

- *The ⋯ menu.* A message's actions move into a menu that opens from a ⋯ on the message, which shows on hover or focus and always on touch screens, as the ⋯ on channels and on members in a call does: Reply, Edit, Copy text, Copy link and Delete, each where the member may. Arrow keys move through it, and Escape closes it. Right-clicking stays the browser's own, for copying text and links. Today the actions are a bar that shows only on hover, which a touch screen or a keyboard can't reach.
- *Deleting asks first,* in a dialog: it says the message goes for everyone, and staff deleting another member's message see whose it is. The inline Delete for everyone goes.
- *A message's link.* Copy link copies the den's address with the channel and message in its fragment, as in `https://den.example.com/#m/41/90210`. A browser never sends a fragment, so a link opened outside Dens tells the den nothing about which message, and the den's rewrite of links keeps a fragment as it is, in a channel and in a DM.
- *Opening one.* In a message, a link of that form to a den the member has joined shows as a chip naming the channel, and the den too when it's another, with the address on hover. A click opens the den and the channel and jumps to the message, as a reply's quote does, loading the page of history around it. When it can't, it says why: the member hasn't joined that den; the channel is gone or they can't see it, which look alike, so a staff-only channel stays hidden; or the message is gone, deleted or past the den's retention. A link to a den that's offline opens from the cache, as any of its channels would. Links in other forms stay plain links.
- *The den's address* answers a browser at its root with a line saying it's a den on Dens, whose links open in Dens. Today it answers 404.
- *Docs.* protocol.md gives the link's form; the composer's guide says how to copy one.

M11.3:

- *Push to talk rings while the key is held.* While a member holds push to talk and isn't muted, their ring lights for everyone in the call, with sound or without, as Discord's does. Their own page lights it at once. For the others, the page tells the den as the key goes down and up (`voice.talk`), and the den passes that to the call's members alone (`voice.talking`), at most five a second from each member, and drops it while the member is muted or staff-muted; releasing the key, leaving the call or the socket closing turns it off. Both messages are new and additive. Voice activity's rings stay as they are, measured from what each page plays. Today a ring follows sound alone, the member's own above −50 dBFS as sent.
- *A share in its own window.* Each share in the player gains a button that opens it in a window of its own, which the member can move to another monitor, resize and make full screen. The player keeps its place in the meantime, saying where it went, with a button that brings it back. Closing the window brings it back; stopping watching, leaving the call or the share ending closes it, and so does closing or reloading the page. The window is a blank page of the page's own origin, which the page fills with the share and its controls, and it plays the stream the page already receives, so it needs no sign-in and no connection of its own. A browser that opens no windows, as Android's WebView, gets no button.
- *Logs that follow a call.* Today's logs can't say why a call dropped: a production instance logs at `warn`, the den logs a call's start and end at `info`, and nothing logs why a socket closed, on either side, or what the page saw. These name dens, channels and members by ID, never by address:
  - In the member's service, at `info`: each close of the page's stream and of a den connection, with its cause (the page went away, a ping went unanswered, a write stalled, the stream fell behind, the den closed it with a code) and how long it had been up; and a call ending because the page that held it went away.
  - In the den, at `info`: each close of a member's socket, with its cause.
  - In the member's service, at `debug`: the call's signaling as it passes (offers in, answers out, ICE restarts and resumes) with how long each took; and the page's notes, which it sends over its stream: each change in its connection to the den's media ports, each restart it asks for and each time it joins again, with why; whether its tab is hidden; and at each change and every 10 seconds of a call, a summary from `getStats`: the path's protocol and candidate types, packets and bytes each way, loss, jitter and round-trip time. The service logs those fields alone, so no text the page holds reaches the log.
  - In the den, at `debug`: each call's ICE and DTLS state changes, offers sent and answered or timed out, and restarts.
- *Downloading the logs.* The settings' Logging card, under the level, downloads the newest 32 MiB of logs as one text file, and says what they hold: no messages, keys or addresses, but the dens' names and IDs, and what happened when. Today the logs are in the service's data directory, which an installed Dens's desktop user can't read.
- *Collecting the drops.* The member on Fedora KDE with Waterfox who drops out of calls sets their level to `debug`, as does the den's owner, and after the next drop both download their logs. The logs say which connection broke first: the page's stream to its service, the media path or the den socket. The first is a lead: when the page's stream closes while its service runs, the service ends the call, while the page expects the den to hold it, so a stream that drops and reconnects looks exactly like a leave and a join. A fix waits for the logs, in a later milestone.
- *Docs.* A section of the install guide on sending logs: the level, what the logs hold, and the download. design.md's Riding out a dropped connection says what's logged.

**M11 testing.**

- Go tests: a stripped MP4's and a copy's movie timescale is 1000; the den passes `voice.talking` to the call's members alone, none from a member muted or staff-muted, at most five a second, and turns it off when the member leaves or their socket closes; the logs' download holds the newest logs up to its cap, in order, as an attachment with `no-store`, for a paired browser alone; each cause of a page stream's close is logged, and a page's notes log only their own fields; the den's root answers its line; and the den's rewrite keeps a message's link as it is, among the shared link cases.
- Page tests: a click that started inside a dialog; a capture that a click cancels; the New divider; the caret in a code block, among the composer's cases; reading a message's link and each refusal; what a folded group still shows; and the push-to-talk ring with mute and staff mute.
- In the container, both browsers: the ⋯ menu by touch, through touch emulation, and by keyboard; the delete dialog; a link opened from one den to another; push to talk ringing for a second member; a share opened in its own window and brought back; and the logs downloaded.
- The manual test: on Windows and Linux, in the four target browsers, each of M11.1's fixes; the ⋯ menu on a touch screen; links between two dens; push to talk ringing in silence; a share full screen on a second monitor; and the logs from both sides of a call dropped on purpose, at `debug`.

**M11 choices,** picks to confirm before building:

- This is M11, and Android moves to M12–M14, so the table stays in the order things are built.
- The Waterfox drops get logs now, and a fix once the logs show the cause.
- Name colors wait for themes, after the phone layout, so both cover it.
- The ⋯ menu replaces the hover bar entirely, and right-clicking stays the browser's.
- A message's link is the den's address with the IDs in its fragment, and the den's root answers a browser with a line.
- The push-to-talk ring reaches everyone in the call, through the den, rather than lighting only on the member's own page.
- The browser keeps which groups are folded, as it keeps the player's place and volumes, rather than the den.
- Ctrl+Enter sends from inside a code block.
- Call logs use the levels there are, `info` and `debug`, with no setting of their own, and the download holds the newest 32 MiB.
- The three steps ship together, as v1.4.0.
