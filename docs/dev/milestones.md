# Dens — Milestones

What each milestone built, and the plan for the one under way; ideas no milestone has taken up yet are in [loose-ideas.md](loose-ideas.md). The design they build toward is [design.md](design.md), with each deployment form's in [installed.md](installed.md), [flatpak.md](flatpak.md) and [android.md](android.md): a section named here, such as Global shortcuts, is one of theirs unless it's one of this file's.

A milestone's plan is written here first, and reviewed before anything is built. Once the milestone is done, what its plan still teaches, such as spike findings and measurements, moves into the design docs' sections, and the rest goes; Git keeps it.

Each milestone ends usable on its own and is tested on Linux and Windows, in Chromium and Firefox. As a rule, each step of a milestone is tested by the automated checks: the harnesses, the Android e2e in the emulator, and the browser checks in the container's Chromium and Firefox. The manual tests, on the phones and in browsers, run together once, at the milestone's end. A spike runs its own trials, and a test that can't wait for the end gets an exception. All platform-specific code lands in M0, while the seams are still cheap to change; later milestones are shared code, but for M7's keys and the helper's start at login, M9's Flatpak, and M12's Android app and the helper's notifications.

**M18, share sound.** Members found that a screen share's sound reaches nobody: on Windows 11 and Fedora KDE, in Ungoogled Chromium, sharing a tab with its sound, a window or another app. Since M16.2 (v1.9.0) a share's sound from Chrome, Edge or any other Chromium-based browser has left the sharer's page unsealed, on every den, since every den with M16.2 seals its calls. Every watcher's page drops it, as a frame in the clear on a member's track, so nobody hears it, and the den has received it in the clear: a leak in M16.2's end-to-end encryption as much as a missing sound. Voices and screens are sealed, Firefox shares no sound, and the Android app shares no screen. M18 fixes the order, makes the page stop a sender the browser sends unsealed rather than trust the order alone, and ships as v1.10.1.

**What happens.** Chromium sends a sender around its transform for good when it has none by the end of the task in which `setRemoteDescription` resolves: a `sender.transform` set later, after any `await`, is taken without an error and never runs. The page's `answer()` seals the screen's sender, awaits its `replaceTrack`, and only then seals the sound's. The plan's probes showed it in the browser container's Chromium 156: the watcher's sound section receives 50 packets a second and decodes none, each frame starting with an Opus byte where SFrame's header goes, and the sharer's worker never sees them. In a page of two peer connections and no Dens, a transform set after a single `setTimeout` runs no more than one set after `setLocalDescription` does, while transforms set before anything awaits all run; the same order, simulated in the real page, has the watcher hear the sound with nothing dropped. The probes are kept locally, in `docs/local/m18-share-sound`, with how to run them. M16.2's checks missed it because they checked what was set rather than what arrived: the share check found an audio track in the player's stream, and the calls check a transform on each receiver and the call's frames in total.

| --- | --- | --- |

M18.1:

- *Sealing in time.* In `answer()`, right after `setRemoteDescription`, the page finds the sections it sends on, the microphone's, and from the call's first share the screen's and its sound's, and sets each one's direction and transform before anything awaits; only then does it put tracks on them. A later share in the call finds its senders sealed already, since the den keeps those sections. The page puts a track only on a sender it sealed in its offer's task, so a change that broke the order again would leave a sender silent rather than unsealed. This is the order Chromium allows, so there's nothing to choose in it.
- *A browser that skips a seal anyway.* A later Chromium could short-circuit sooner, before the page can seal anything. Options: (a) nothing more: the browser checks catch it on the container's Chromium, after members' browsers have updated; (b) the page compares, for each of its senders, the packets it sent (`sender.getStats()`) with the frames its worker saw on that track, a second after a track goes on and at each summary of the call's statistics, and stops a sender whose packets left without its frames passing through the worker: a share's sound goes silent and the share says it has none, a screen ends its share, and a voice leaves the call, each saying why, with a note in the call's log; up to a second of it reaches the den unsealed before it stops; (c) as (b), but each sender first carries silence, and its own track only once the worker has seen frames from it, so only silence could leave unsealed; it delays every call's voice and every share by a moment, and adds a silent source for each sender. Pick (b): it fails closed at a fraction of (c)'s parts, and a browser that changes this is rare enough that a second, said out loud, beats a delay on every call. A paused screen sends nothing, and a muted voice's packets of silence pass through the worker as its words do, so neither stops anything.
- *The logs.* The worker counts what it seals, opens and drops on each track, and why it drops each frame: in the clear, under a key the page lacks, on another track, at a counter it had, or not opening. The call's notes carry the counts, so a watcher's notes would have said at once that every frame of a share's sound came in the clear.
- *Older pages.* A den can't make an older page seal its share's sound, and a den that meant to listen wouldn't try, so the fix is the page's alone: a member sharing from v1.9.0 or v1.10.0 sends sound nobody hears until they update.
- *The release.* v1.10.1 carries the fix to every form; Android's app shares no screen, but plays shares' sound. Its changelog says plainly what went wrong, and since when: a shared screen's sound from a Chromium-based browser went out unencrypted, so the den could have heard it, while nobody watching did. It also ships what the one-line Windows install changed in the binary since v1.10.0, which its changelog lists too: the update notice's install line on Windows, `dens update` and the shortcut helper staging their elevated files where the member's programs can't swap them, and the page's links to the guides as they're laid out now. No migration, and the protocol stays at 3.

The docs land with it: design.md's Calls (the order, the check on each sender, and the counts by track), its security checklist's line on sealing a sender before its track goes on, which the share's sound broke, and the changelog. The public docs already say what M18 makes true.

**M18 testing.**

- *Page tests:* the sections the page sends on; every sender's direction and transform set before the first track goes on, against a stand-in peer connection that, as Chromium does, sends around a transform set after its offer's task; no track on a sender sealed late; the check on each sender: packets without frames stop it, and a paused screen, a muted voice and a sender just attached stop nothing; and the worker's counts by track and reason.
- *Browser checks:* the share check measures what arrives: alice's Chromium shares its fake screen with its fake sound, and bob's Firefox and a Chromium watcher each decode the sound, its samples and energy above zero, the workers counting it sealed and opened with none dropped; then, with a preload that sets the sound's transform a task late, as the page did, the page stops the sound within a second and says so. The calls check counts the frames opened on each member's track, not a transform on each receiver. The two-shares, talk and soundboard checks run again, since every answer changes order; each in Chromium and Firefox.
- *The Android e2e's call*, as CI runs it, since the app's page answers its den the same way.
- *The manual test*, at M18's end, on v1.10.1's build: in Ungoogled Chromium on Windows 11 and Fedora KDE, and in Chrome or Edge on Windows, a tab shared with its sound, a window where Chromium offers its sound, and on Windows the whole screen with the computer's sound, an app's reaching the watchers and the call's voices not; watched in Chromium, Firefox and the S24 Ultra's app, each hearing it. On Linux, Chromium shares a whole screen's sound only behind its flag (Voice and screen share's Known risks).
