# Dens — Milestones

What each milestone built, and the plan for the one under way; ideas no milestone has taken up yet are in [loose-ideas.md](loose-ideas.md). The design they build toward is [design.md](design.md), with each deployment form's in [installed.md](installed.md), [flatpak.md](flatpak.md) and [android.md](android.md): a section named here, such as Global shortcuts, is one of theirs unless it's one of this file's.

A milestone's plan is written here first, and reviewed before anything is built. Once the milestone is done, what its plan still teaches, such as spike findings and measurements, moves into the design docs' sections, and the rest goes; Git keeps it.

Each milestone ends usable on its own and is tested on Linux and Windows, in Chromium and Firefox. As a rule, each step of a milestone is tested by the automated checks: the harnesses, the Android e2e in the emulator, and the browser checks in the container's Chromium and Firefox. The manual tests, on the phones and in browsers, run together once, at the milestone's end. A spike runs its own trials, and a test that can't wait for the end gets an exception. All platform-specific code lands in M0, while the seams are still cheap to change; later milestones are shared code, but for M7's keys and the helper's start at login, M9's Flatpak, and M12's Android app and the helper's notifications.

M14.1:

- *The renewal.* A session lasts an hour, and the client's `renew` waits on a timer until ten minutes before it ends. Go's timers run on `CLOCK_MONOTONIC`, which stops while a phone is suspended, so a phone asleep renews late, its token lapses, and the den closes the socket with 4001 once an hour. Dens signs in again within a second, but the drop, and the minutes a sleeping phone takes to reconnect after it, make most of the gaps the spike measured (android.md's *A phone sleeps*). Options: (a) cap the timer at a minute, which on monotonic time fires every 20 minutes or so on a phone awake 5% of the time; (b) look at the wall clock each time the den's ping arrives, every 30 seconds, which is what wakes a sleeping phone, through the socket's ping callback (coder/websocket's `OnPingReceived`), keeping the timer for a computer, whose clocks don't stop. Pick (b), as the design has it: it adds no wakes, and the renewal goes out at the first ping after its time. The expiry and now are each read without Go's monotonic reading, since Go subtracts two monotonic readings when both times hold one. Reconnecting under a wake lock with an alarm behind it, the rest of staying connected, stays in loose-ideas.md.
- *Durations in the logs.* The den connection's log lines say how long it lasted by the monotonic clock, as "closed after 9m44s" for an hour's connection on a phone. They come from the wall clock, as android.md's design says, so the manual check's log reads true.
- *Links.* Options: (a) underlined always, as DaisyUI's `link` draws them; (b) underlined on hover and keyboard focus, set apart by their color the rest of the time (DaisyUI's `link-hover`, with focus added); (c) never underlined. Pick (b), for every link the page draws, a message's, a cover's and the page's own: the color marks a link in a line of text, and the underline still comes where the pointer or the keyboard is.
- *A YouTube cover's links.* The cover is one button that plays the video. Its title and its channel's name become links laid over it: the title to the video on YouTube, the name to the channel, each opening a new tab as a message's links do, while a click anywhere else on the cover plays it, as now. YouTube's oEmbed answer carries the channel's address (`author_url`), which the service keeps beside the name, only as an https address on `www.youtube.com`; any other, or none, leaves the name as text. Covers live in the service's memory and go to the page alone, so neither the den nor the protocol changes.

M14.2:

- *The sounds.* Seven, each one of Kenney's Interface Sounds:

  | Sound | Plays when | Kenney's file |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Message | A DM or a mention arrives, where the service places its sound (below) | Drop 4 |
  | Joined | A member joins the call, the member too | Confirmation 1 |
  | Left | A member leaves the call, the member too, as when staff disconnect them | Question 2 |
  | Share started | A member of the call starts sharing their screen | Maximize 6 |
  | Share ended | A share in the call ends | Minimize 6 |
  | Microphone on | The member's microphone opens | Drop 3 |
  | Microphone off | The member's microphone closes | Bong 1 |

  The files go in the page's assets, served by the client listener as the page's others are. CC0 asks no credit; the notices credit Kenney all the same, with CC0's text in `scripts/notices/`.
- *The microphone's.* Its sounds follow the microphone, whatever moves it: push to talk pressed and let go, push to mute let go and pressed, muting and unmuting, deafening and undeafening, from the keys, the helper, the call's button or the call's controls, and staff muting and unmuting the member. A change that leaves the microphone as it was, as deafening while muted, plays nothing, and others' mutes play nothing.
- *While deafened.* A deafened member hears nothing of the call, so its joins, leaves and shares play nothing, nor does the soundboard (M14.3); their own microphone's sounds and a message's still play, since the one answers what they did and the other comes from outside the call.
- *Where they play.* Options: (a) a context of their own, on the system's default output; (b) as the call's voices play: an audio element set to the speaker the member chose, and where the page may play sound without a click, as in the Android app, the call's own context, which Android routes as the call. Pick (b): a sound comes out where the call does, the headset rather than the laptop's speakers, and on a phone follows the call's route, the earpiece, the loudspeaker or Bluetooth, since Android routes audio by how it's made (M12.2). Outside a call the message's sound plays on the speaker chosen too. A browser lets a page play sound only once the member has clicked in it, so a Dens tab untouched since it opened plays none, as it plays no call.
- *The message's sound.* The service decides where a message notifies (M12.3), and it decides the sound too, so a message makes one sound, never two. Where a page shows the notification through the browser, the page plays the sound and asks the browser for a silent popup (`silent`, in Chromium and in Firefox from 132); where the desktop's helper or Android's shell shows it, the desktop's or Android's own sound plays, as now, and the page plays none. The sound doesn't wait on notifications being on: with DMs or mentions off, or the browser not letting the page notify, the service sends the sound alone to the page on screen most lately, while a page is open; with none open, nothing plays. A muted den or channel makes no sound, nor does a message in the channel a page shows on screen. Android's notification sound stays Android's: it's set per notification channel in Android's settings and fixed when Android makes the channel, so making Kenney's its default would take new channels and drop what members set on the old; the app's settings say where to change it.
- *The settings.* Options: (a) the message's sound in Chat & media's Notifications card, and the call's in Voice & video; (b) a new tab, Notifications & sounds, holding the Notifications card, moved from Chat & media, and a Sounds card; (c) a Sounds tab, the notifications staying in Chat & media. Pick (b): chat apps keep sounds with notifications (Telegram's section is named so, and Discord's sounds are under its Notifications), and Voice & video, the longest tab, takes nothing more. The tabs become Appearance, Chat & media, Notifications & sounds, Voice & video and System, and the phone layout's list holds the five. The Sounds card has a row for each sound, grouped Messages and Calls: its name, ▶ to hear it, a switch, a volume slider, and Replace…, or once replaced, Reset, which brings back the default. All seven are on by default, at volumes set once by ear, so they sit evenly with each other and under a call's voices.
- *A member's own sounds.* Options: (a) the browser keeps their files, as it keeps the voice settings; (b) the service keeps them, as it keeps the notification settings and the voice keys. Pick (b): every page paired with the install plays them, clearing a browser's data keeps them, and a backup holds them. Each sound's switch and volume go in the service's configuration beside the notifications, and a replaced sound's file in its database, in a table of its own: the first migration since v1.0.0. A file is at most 1 MiB, of a type `internal/media` names audio, which the service checks; the page checks before sending it that it plays and lasts at most 10 seconds. It's the member's own and stays on the install: the page plays it from the client listener, and its routes are on the client router alone, their writes guarded by `Origin` as every write is.

M14.3:

- *How a sound reaches the call.* Options: (a) each listener's page plays the den's file as the den says a member played it, as Discord's soundboard works; (b) mixed into the player's voice, as it's sent; (c) the den streams it into the call on a track of its own, which every member's page plays as it plays a voice. Pick (c). Members never fetch the files. The track goes the way voices go on every form: to the speaker chosen, along Android's call route, on with the screen off, and silent while deafened; and the browser's echo cancellation hears it as it hears voices, so a member on speakers doesn't send it back into the call. Everyone hears a sound at once, and a member joining partway hears the rest. (a) would take each listener's install fetching each file, a list of what plays kept in step on every page, and a player of the page's own beside the call's; in (b), noise suppression and push to talk would cut the sound. The cost is the den's upload while a sound plays, one Opus stream to each member of the call, about 1 Mbps for fifteen, small beside their voices.
- *The call's track.* The den gives each call one more audio track, added to every member's connection as they join, so a sound needs no new offer; it already makes a track of its own for each share's sound. The page knows the track by its stream, `soundboard`, and plays it as it plays a member, through an audio element on a computer and through the call's context in the Android app, at the member's soundboard volume. The largest call's offer grows by one audio section, and a test checks it still fits `MaxSDP` with `MaxCallMembers` in the call, watching the most shares.
- *Opus, from staff's page.* The den sends a sound's Opus packets as its file holds them, read from Ogg with Pion's reader, since re-encoding would take an audio encoder the media module doesn't have. So the page of the staff member adding a sound prepares it before it goes up: it decodes the file, any audio the browser plays, a video's sound too; evens its loudness to one level, its peaks under full scale; and encodes it as Opus with WebCodecs (Chromium from 94, Firefox from 130), at 64 kbps, in Ogg, which a small writer of the page's makes. A browser without WebCodecs' audio encoder can't add sounds, and says so. The one level takes the place of a volume for each sound, and the one bitrate makes a file's size follow its length. The den checks what arrives as it checks any file, stripped by the media module, which learns its length, and takes only Opus in Ogg, no longer than the owner allows, and no larger than that length at the bitrate makes, with room for Ogg's pages.
- *The den's sounds.* A sound is one of the den's named files: its name, at most 32 characters, unique in the den and cleaned as display names are; its file; and who added it and when. The den keeps its named files in one table with a kind, sounds first, so custom emojis and an event's badges use it later. Staff, the moderators and the owner, add a sound in Manage's new Soundboard section, under This den, by a file and a name, and there hear, rename or delete each. The sound takes its file as a profile takes its picture, so retention never removes it, and the file counts toward the den's space but not its uploader's, since it's the den's, not theirs. Members get the sounds' names; their files open for staff alone, since only the den plays them. The table, and the den's columns for its limits, are M14's second migration.
- *The owner's limits.* In Manage's Limits, a Soundboard section beside Calls, each limit conservative by default, as Discord's soundboard is short, 5.2 seconds:

  | Limit | Default | The owner sets |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | Sounds | 50 | 0 to 500; 0 turns the soundboard off, keeping its sounds |
  | Longest | 10 seconds | 1 second to 5 minutes |
  | A member's wait between sounds | 5 seconds | 0 to 10 minutes |

  A sound's size follows its length, so it has no limit of its own, and the den's file size limit still bounds each upload. Limits hold as a sound is added: lowering one leaves the sounds already added, which staff remove as they choose. The upload Calls estimates at full load counts the soundboard's stream to each member.
- *Playing.* A member in a call plays a sound from the soundboard. The den checks that they're in the call and not staff muted, the soundboard is on, no sound plays in the call, and the wait after their last sound has passed; then it reads the sound's packets and sends them on the call's track at their pace. One sound plays in a call at a time, since mixing two would take re-encoding: a sound asked for while another plays is refused. The den ends a sound early only as its player leaves the call, or staff mute or disconnect them, so a long one can't be used to wear a call down; there's no button to stop one, and a member who'd rather not hear it turns the soundboard down. While a sound plays, its player's ring lights for everyone in the call, through `voice.sounding`, since the page lights no muted member's ring by push to talk's list, and a member playing a sound is often muted.
- *The soundboard.* A button opens it: in the call's block, in place of the call's speaker, and to the left of mute on the phone's bar and in Home's box. At its top are a search field and a slider for how loud the soundboard plays for the member, 0 muting it, which the browser keeps, as it keeps each member's volume; below them are the den's sounds as tiles by name, a tap on one playing it for the call. While a sound plays, or the member must wait, the tiles say so and play nothing. It opens up and to the right from the list's block and down from Home's box, and on the phone layout as a sheet from the screen's foot, as the page's menus are there (M13.2). With no sounds in the den, members have no button, and staff's opens on Add sounds, in Manage.
- *The call's block, bar and box.* The call's block loses its speaker. The channel's row in the list shows the call's state on its speaker instead, green once connected and yellow while joining, connecting or reconnecting, as the block's did, with the state in its label. The block shows a line saying how the call stands only while it isn't connected, or while it's in another den, which the line names, a tap on it going there. The phone's bar gains mute, since a narrow window with a mouse has no call's button and so no mute on the bar at all, and the soundboard to mute's left; Home's box gains the soundboard.
- *Manage from the den.* The den's name at the top of its list becomes a button, marked ▾, that opens Manage for the den, as a Discord server's name opens its menu; on the phone layout it opens Manage's list of sections. Home's Manage stays, and the cog beside the member's name stays the app's settings, so the two keep apart what's the den's, for staff everyone's and for the member their own across their devices, and what's this device's Dens, for every den.
- *The protocol.* Additive, so no version bump. The den's soundboard limits come in its info, as its call limits do; its sounds in `ready`, changed by `sound.created`, `sound.updated` and `sound.deleted`; and the call's track in each member's offers, as stream `soundboard`. `voice.sound`, a client frame, names the sound to play, and `voice.refused` gains the reasons a sound is refused: one playing, the wait, the soundboard off. `voice.sounding` names the member whose sound plays in the call, or none, and goes to the sockets that hold the call's members' calls as a sound starts and ends, and as a socket's call starts or takes a held call back, as `voice.talking` does. A client ignores what it doesn't know: an older page plays the track as it would a member's voice, at full volume with no slider, and lights no ring; an older den sends no track, and its info has no soundboard to show. Playing counts toward a member's other writes, past the wait.

Each step's docs land with it: M14.1's in android.md's *A phone sleeps* and design.md's YouTube players; M14.2's in design.md, which gains Sounds beside Notifications, and in the public join.md, whose Notifications becomes Notifications and sounds; and M14.3's in design.md, which gains the soundboard, its track and the den's named files, and the soundboard's limits beside its calls', with the phone layout's call's bar and controls and Manage opened from the den's name, in protocol.md's voice and rate limits, and in the public join.md and host.md, which say how members play sounds and how staff add them and owners limit them, with privacy.md saying the den sees who plays which sound, as it sees who's in a call.
