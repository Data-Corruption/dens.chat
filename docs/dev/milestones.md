# Dens — Milestones

What each milestone built, and the plan for the one under way; ideas no milestone has taken up yet are in [loose-ideas.md](loose-ideas.md). The design they build toward is [design.md](design.md), with each deployment form's in [installed.md](installed.md), [flatpak.md](flatpak.md) and [android.md](android.md): a section named here, such as Global shortcuts, is one of theirs unless it's one of this file's.

A milestone's plan is written here first, and reviewed before anything is built. Once the milestone is done, what its plan still teaches, such as spike findings and measurements, moves into the design docs' sections, and the rest goes; Git keeps it.

Each milestone ends usable on its own and is tested on Linux and Windows, in Chromium and Firefox. As a rule, each step of a milestone is tested by the automated checks: the harnesses, the Android e2e in the emulator, and the browser checks in the container's Chromium and Firefox. The manual tests, on the phones and in browsers, run together once, at the milestone's end. A spike runs its own trials, and a test that can't wait for the end gets an exception. All platform-specific code lands in M0, while the seams are still cheap to change; later milestones are shared code, but for M7's keys and the helper's start at login, M9's Flatpak, and M12's Android app and the helper's notifications.

| --- | --- | --- |
| M0 | Foundation: Sprout fork trimmed to the single-process lifecycle, elevated installers for Linux and Windows, `dens@`/`dens-<name>` service, platform layer, two listeners, vault with envelope encryption, `dens open` pairing | On both platforms: browser pairs, vault survives reboot, backup restores on a second machine, including Linux to Windows and back |
| M1 | Text den: invites, key auth and fallbacks, roles, channels, groups, DMs, presence, uploads with limits and metadata stripping, end-to-end encrypted DMs | Two machines chat through a Caddy-fronted den |
| M2 | Voice: calls in voice channels through the Pion SFU, on a UDP mux with ICE-TCP fallback; the signaling relay, with offers from the den as members join and leave; mute; the call bar | A clear two-person call across two home networks in the four target browsers, over UDP and with UDP blocked, and from the den owner's own browser |
| M3 | Group voice: Opus at 96 kbps, speaking indicators, each member's own volume for everyone else, staff disconnecting and muting members in calls, calls that ride out a dropped connection, and RNNoise noise suppression unless a member turns it off. Compact links: Reddit, YouTube, X and Amazon product links in one short form, and tracking parameters off every link. Third-party notices in the binary | A call of everyone the manual test brings together stays stable for an hour, and a YouTube share link arrives without its tracking but with its timestamp, in a channel and in a DM |
| M4 | YouTube players, for members who turn them on. Screen share: PLI forwarding, owner limits, viewer caps; a voice channel's bitrate as an owner setting | A YouTube link plays where it's posted for a member who turned players on, and 2 shares with 20 viewers within owner limits |
| M5 | Message retention. Managing files: each member's uploads by size against their limit, deleting and swapping them. Smaller copies of photos and videos by default, compared with full size before sending. ← and → through a message's images in the viewer | A member at their limit frees space by deleting and swapping old attachments; a phone photo goes as a smaller copy unless its sender sends it full size, and a phone video too large for the den as a copy that fits; and a den with retention on removes messages and their files once they pass it |
| M6 | Sync efficiency: the client's encrypted cache of files and history, the changes since it last synced, cached member lists, offline reading, compressed large frames | A client restarted after a day offline downloads only what changed |
| M7 | Global shortcuts: push to talk and the other voice keys while another program has focus, through a helper in the desktop session (see Global shortcuts) | A member pushes to talk from a full-screen game, on Windows and on GNOME and KDE under Wayland |
| M8 | Hardening, from the audit before the first release: one budget for each member's messages, and the den's ring of events held to a size; a control endpoint and a service that stay up on Windows; no member's local address in a call's answers; text that can't freeze a page; no downgrades, and signature checks only a flag turns off; backups that hold every file; owner invites from the den's machine; a pass over the page's wording, placement and small details | One member flooding every channel and DM stays within their budget and the den within its memory; Dens on Windows keeps answering a client that closes the control pipe at once; the den finds no local address in answers from Chrome or Firefox; a channel of hostile messages leaves pages responsive; an older release won't install over a newer one |
| M9 | Hosting from the page, and a Flatpak: hosting as a switch in the settings, with nothing to choose at install; deployment forms in the code; a Flatpak for the distributions the installer refuses, running in the member's session with its key in their keyring, from Dens's own signed repository | An install made with no den option hosts once its owner turns hosting on, and an instance v1.0.0 installed to host still does after updating; on a distribution the installer refuses, a member installs Dens from the site's link, unlocks it with the local password and has it remember, is signed in to their dens at their next sign-in without touching anything, pushes to talk from a game under Wayland, and updates through their software center |
| M10 | Moving a den, and hosting from the command line: backups that hold up at any size; the den's export, which carries the den without its host's client; restoring it on another computer and hosting it there with no browser; the computer it left removing its copy, and reaching the den at its address as any member's install does; a den started on a server from the command line | A backup cut off partway is never kept as finished, and one larger than restore's old caps restores; a den created on a member's computer moves to a Linux server with no desktop: its members reconnect by themselves, and its owner's computer, once it removes its copy, reaches the den at its address as the owner, with its history and DMs as they were; and a den started on such a server from the command line gets its owner through an invite |
| M11 | Before Android: the small fixes from browser testing; each message's actions in a ⋯ menu that touch screens and keyboards reach, with links to messages; push to talk lighting its ring while the key is held; a watched share in a window of its own; and logs that follow a call from the page to the den | A member reaches every action on a message from its ⋯, by touch and by keyboard; a link to a message, pasted in another den, opens it there or says why it can't; a member holding push to talk in silence rings for everyone in the call; a watched share plays full screen in its own window on a second monitor; and logs downloaded from the page after a call drops say which connection broke, and when |
| M12 | Android, the app: the Go binary under a Kotlin shell, the page in a WebView with its permissions and files, calls that go on with the screen off, the APK on the release host with its updater (see [android.md](android.md)); and notifications on every form, through the shell on Android and through the browser or the helper on a desktop | A member installs the APK, pairs without typing a token, joins a den, chats, sends a stripped phone photo, talks in a call with the screen off, and gets a notification for a DM while the app is in the background, as a desktop does with no Dens tab open |
| M13 | Android, the phone layout: every screen of the page usable on a phone | Every screen the browser checks cover works at 390 pixels wide, in the WebView |
| M14 | Android, staying connected: the sleeping phone's fixes, the session's renewal by the wall clock and reconnecting under a wake lock with an alarm behind it; the warnings for a restricted battery and background setting; and the install guide's steps for them | A member's phone shows a DM within seconds, eight hours after they last opened Dens |

M13 and M14 get their plans as each begins.
