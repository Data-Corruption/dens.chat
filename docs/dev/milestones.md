# Dens — Milestones

What each milestone built, and the plan for the one under way; ideas no milestone has taken up yet are in [loose-ideas.md](loose-ideas.md). The design they build toward is [design.md](design.md), with each deployment form's in [installed.md](installed.md), [flatpak.md](flatpak.md) and [android.md](android.md): a section named here, such as Global shortcuts, is one of theirs unless it's one of this file's.

A milestone's plan is written here first, and reviewed before anything is built. Once the milestone is done, what its plan still teaches, such as spike findings and measurements, moves into the design docs' sections, and the rest goes; Git keeps it.

Each milestone ends usable on its own and is tested on Linux and Windows, in Chromium and Firefox. As a rule, each step of a milestone is tested by the automated checks: the harnesses, the Android e2e in the emulator, and the browser checks in the container's Chromium and Firefox. The manual tests, on the phones and in browsers, run together once, at the milestone's end. A spike runs its own trials, and a test that can't wait for the end gets an exception. All platform-specific code lands in M0, while the seams are still cheap to change; later milestones are shared code, but for M7's keys and the helper's start at login, M9's Flatpak, M12's Android app and the helper's notifications, and M20's capture of a computer's sound.

**M20, Relay.** A member with more than one device uses them together: headphones paired to the phone while the sound is the desktop's, or two computers behind a KVM with a call on one of them. Relay links two of a member's own installs straight across their network and carries two things, each with its own switch: the voice keys of one to the call the other holds, and one computer's sound to play on the other. The den only introduces the two, in boxes sealed between the member's devices, so it learns no local address and hears nothing. A spike came first (`spikes/relay/README.md`): the link got through Windows' firewall, firewalld, NixOS's firewall, the unit's sandbox, the Flatpak's and the Android app with no rule and no prompt; the helper captures on Windows and Linux; a browser's page plays for an hour without a gap, and a phone's with its screen off; and the sound crosses better as RTP than on a data channel (M20.2). Left out: a link across two networks or the internet, a microphone relayed, the Android app as a source, the sound compressed, a volume of the relay's own, and muting the source's speakers for the member. The small fixes and the call timer in loose-ideas.md wait for M21.

M20.1:

- *The introduction.* Options: (a) installs find each other on the network themselves, by multicast; (b) the den passes boxes between a member's devices. Pick (b): the den is where a member's devices already meet and already knows they're one member's, and nothing has to listen on the network. A frame, `relay.signal`, carries a box of 1 KiB to the member's other sockets, or to one device's by its key ID; the den forwards it to that member's sockets alone, as it sends a `voice.key` to one socket, keeps nothing of it, and limits it as it limits those, 30 in 10 seconds. The den's info says `relay`, so a client asks only a den that forwards.
- *The seal.* A box is sealed under a key made from the member's DM seal, as the notes' key is, under a label of its own, and its associated data names the den, the member and the two devices. It's padded to its size, so the den sees that two devices spoke, and when. Inside: the install's Relay ID, 16 random bytes made once, by which two installs that share two dens know they're one pair; its name, the label its dens list it under; what it offers, keys and sound; and one of four kinds: `who` and `here`, which fill the settings' list, and `hello` and `answer`, which carry a link's half: a certificate's fingerprint, ICE credentials made for the attempt, a UDP port, the install's local IPv4 addresses, and the time, which the other side takes within a minute. A device without the seal opens nothing, and isn't listed.
- *The link.* A package of its own, `internal/relay`, in the service: one Pion peer connection for a pair of installs, made when either has a reason, a call the other holds or a sound to play, and closed when neither has. Nothing more crosses the den than the two boxes: each side writes the other's description from its box, as the spike's probe did, the lower Relay ID offering. Both send from the answer on, for the half minute an introduction is good, since each side's sending is what lets the other's packets through its firewall, and Pion's own seven tries end after a second and a half. Host candidates alone, over IPv4, and private addresses alone: a box that names any other is refused, so none can aim an install at the internet. An ephemeral port, no STUN, nothing listening and no firewall rule; the service linking raised no prompt on Windows. Two data channels: `ctl`, reliable, and `now`, unordered and never sent again, for keys and a ping every 250 ms. SCTP's retransmission timer is capped at 100 ms, without which a cut of a fifth of a second stops the keys for a second. ICE's timeouts are 1 s, 3 s and 500 ms, so a lost link fails within 4 seconds and asks the den for a new introduction, backing off; one that never comes up says the two aren't on one network. Local addresses stay in memory: never logged, written, or given to the page.
- *Keys.* With Keybind relay on, an install that sees its own member in a call it doesn't hold, as the den's `voice.state` shows it, says `hello`, and the install that holds the call answers if its switch is on too. The first's helper is told a call is on, so it reads the bound keys as for a call of its own, each device's keys being its own, and the hub sends over the link what it would have dropped. They cross as states: every action held, and for each toggle a count of its presses, every 20 ms while any is held and every 100 ms otherwise. The other hub takes them as a second helper's: holds are kept by source, so push to talk stays open while any device holds it; a toggle fires as its count moves, so one never fires twice; and once nothing is heard for 300 ms, what that device held is let go. The page acts as it does on its own helper's presses, and only in a call it holds.
- *The settings.* Relay, a section after Voice. Keybind relay, a switch, off unless set. Your devices: the member's other installs that answered `who`, by name, with what each offers. The switches are the service's, kept in its configuration with the Relay ID, and `/api/settings` says what this install can do, keys and sharing its sound, so each form shows what it has. A line links to the guide.

M20.2:

- *How the sound crosses.* Options: (a) on a third data channel, as the spike first sent it; (b) as RTP beside the channels: SRTP under the link's own DTLS, 16-bit stereo at the source's rate, 240 frames to a packet, 5 ms, with no interceptors, so nothing is acknowledged or sent again. Both ran on 2026-11-12 (the spike's Sound as RTP). (b) took the phone's receiving from 31% of a core to 22% and Windows' sending from 3.8% to 2.3%; a short cut costs it the cut with no timer to cap; and the keys keep SCTP to themselves. (a) has one thing (b) lacks: Pion's SCTP sends a lost packet again even on a channel told not to, 50 to 150 ms late, which a phone's buffer would hide. Pick (b): nothing was lost in two million packets on the spike's wire and Wi-Fi, whose own retries already turn loss into lateness, and the player fades across a missing 5 ms.
- *Capture.* The helper reads the system's sound, since it runs in the desktop session, and the service links, since a desktop program that holds a port raises Windows' firewall prompt. The service asks on the helper's stream, and the helper opens a second control request that carries the sound, in 5 ms pieces; in the Flatpak the two are one process. Windows: WASAPI's loopback of the default output. Options: go-wca with go-ole, or the dozen COM calls written on the vtable helper the notifications use. Pick ours: no module for what `host` already does. Linux: the default output's monitor over PulseAudio's protocol, which PipeWire speaks too. Options: jfreymuth/pulse, or a client of our own. Pick the module: pure Go with nothing behind it, it carried the spike's hours, and the protocol's framing isn't worth owning; its license joins the notices. Both follow the default output, open again when a device goes, and fill a gap with silence.
- *Sharing and listening.* Relay's Audio relay card. On a computer that can capture: Share this computer's sound, a switch, off unless set; a computer that shares says so in its member's other devices' lists. Beside each: Listen. Listening starts at that click, which a browser needs before it plays anything, belongs to the page that clicked, as a call does, and ends with Stop or with the page; it never starts by itself. One listener to a source: a new one takes over, and the earlier is told. An install plays or sends, never both, since two devices playing each other would loop: listening pauses its own sharing. While either is on, a bar where the call's bar sits says so, on both ends, with Stop. The card says in a line what muting the source does: muted, Windows and PipeWire still send; turned down to nothing, Windows sends silence.
- *The player.* The service hands the page the packets on a WebSocket of their own, binary, four to a message, 20 ms, on Low and eight on High: on the phone, four took the app's three processes from 82% of a core to 70%, and eight to 65%. A worker holds the socket and an audio worklet plays, in a context of its own at the source's rate, so the page's thread carries none of it. The worklet keeps its buffer, or one and a half of its output's stride and 10 ms where that's more, plays up to 0.5% fast or slow to stay there, and fades across a gap. Buffer: Low, 40 ms, or High, 150 ms, the member's choice, kept by the browser; High on a phone unless set.
- *The Flatpak* gains `--socket=pulseaudio`. Its page refuses an update that asks for a new permission, so v1.12.0 reaches a Flatpak through the software center or `flatpak update`; the changelog and the guide say so.

M20.3:

- *The permission.* Android 17 keeps an app off its own network without `ACCESS_LOCAL_NETWORK`. The manifest declares it, and the shell asks for it as the member first turns a Relay switch on or presses Listen, told by Dens on the shell's stream as it's told to notify; refused, Relay says so in a line. Earlier Androids ask nothing.
- *Keys.* A phone has no keys to relay, and has the call's button. While another of the member's devices holds a call and Keybind relay is on, the page shows the button under that device's name, and its presses go to the service on the page's event socket and across the link as a helper's do, in the role the member gave the button.
- *As media.* While the phone listens, Dens tells the shell, which adds media playback's type to its service, holds the page shown as for a call, and keeps Wi-Fi awake, under `WAKE_LOCK`. The page's player is media to Android: under the media volume, on headphones paired to the phone, beside other apps' sound. The phone never shares its own.
- *In a call.* A relay playing as media goes silent on Bluetooth headphones once a call's microphone opens. So while the phone is in a call the relay plays through the call's own context, on the call's route, and goes back to its own as the call ends; on Bluetooth that costs a call's quality while the call lasts.

Nothing changes the database: Relay's settings are new fields in the configuration's row. The protocol stays at 3: `relay.signal` and the info's flag are additive, an older den never forwards, and an older Dens drops the event. Android's manifest gains `ACCESS_LOCAL_NETWORK`, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK` and `WAKE_LOCK`, and the Flatpak its socket.
