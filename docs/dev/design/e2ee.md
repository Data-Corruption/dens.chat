# End-to-end encryption

Part of [Dens's design](../design.md): what the den can't read, and how: the DM seal, identities and codes, DM keys, group DMs, notes to self and sealed calls.

DMs and their files are end-to-end encrypted: the den stores and relays them but can't read them. Channels stay readable by the den, since staff moderate them and the den does the work that needs their text: mentions, reply previews and compact links. Each member has an identity in each den, which a DM's keys are sealed to, so a DM opens at once, with nothing to type; members compare codes when they choose, which the den never learns. Calls and screen shares are end-to-end encrypted too, on a den with `sealed_calls`: each member's voice, screen and share's sound are sealed frame by frame in their page, under a key their Dens seals to each other member's identity (see [Sealed calls](#sealed-calls)). So are group DMs, of three to ten members, whose every change its maker signs, so the den can't add anyone (see [Group DMs](#group-dms)), and each member's notes to self, which only their own devices read (see [Notes to self](#notes-to-self)).

## What it protects

- The den's owner, and anyone holding the den's disk or backups, can't read DMs, group DMs or a member's notes, or see the photos in them, or a group's name.
- Keys pass through the den, so a den could hand out keys of its own and sit in the middle of a DM. It would have to give two members identities of its own the first time they meet, both ways, and go on doing it for as long as they talk; comparing codes, at any time, catches it (see [Codes](#codes)).
- A call's voices and shares, on a den with `sealed_calls`: the den forwards frames it can't open. It still sees who is in a call, when, and how much each member sends, and it chooses who's in a call, so a member it added in secret would have to show in the call's list to hear anything.
- Metadata stays visible: the den sees who DMs whom, when, and how much, and it can hold messages back.
- Members who compare codes over a channel the den's owner controls, or with someone pretending to be the other member, aren't protected, nor are those who never compare, with a den that tampers. Nothing here is bulletproof, and the app says so.
- There's no forward secrecy: a stolen device, or a leaked seal together with a copy of the den's data, opens all of that member's DM history. A ratchet's forward secrecy comes from forgetting keys, while a DM's history here opens from the den on each of a member's devices (see [loose-ideas.md's Probably not doing](../loose-ideas.md#probably-not-doing)).

## The DM seal

All of it uses Go's standard library, plus the XChaCha20-Poly1305 that already seals data at rest.

- Each member has a DM seal: 256 random bits, made by their Dens the first time they join or create a den, and kept in the vault. It never reaches the den. Their other devices get it through approval (see [Approving new devices](auth.md#approving-new-devices)), sealed to that device.
- One seal serves every den an install joins. Each den's DM keys are sealed under a key derived from the seal and the den's ID, so no den sees the seal, and what one den stores is useless at another. An install that joined dens before it was linked to the member's other devices can hold a different seal for some dens; each den's DMs use the seal they were set up with.
- The page shows the seal beside the recovery codes whenever the member joins a den, saying when it's the one the install's other dens use, and again on request after the local password: "Reads your DMs if you lose every device. Keep it secret." The codes and the seal save as one text file named for the den, with the den's address and the member's username, or copy as its text, either of which lets Done through; Android's app saves no file the page makes, so there they copy.
- The seal never changes. A member who loses it and every device starts over (see [History and starting over](#history-and-starting-over)).
- The seal is what the member's identity in each den comes from (see [Identities](#identities)), so it guards every DM, every call, every group, the key of the member's notes (see [Notes to self](#notes-to-self)), and the boxes between their devices (see [Relay](helper.md#relay)).

## Identities

- A member's identity in a den is two key pairs: one for HPKE (RFC 9180) with X-Wing, X25519 and ML-KEM-768 together, which keys are sealed to (`crypto/hpke`'s `MLKEM768X25519`), and one for Ed25519, which signs what the member's Dens makes. Both come from their DM seal by HKDF, salted with the den's ID, each under a label of its own, so every device holding the seal holds the identity with nothing new to hand over, and no den sees what the member is at another. The service makes them from the seal as it uses them and clears the signing key after; the X-Wing key's copies are the library's, which go with the garbage collector. A member who starts over gets a new identity with their new seal.
- A member's Dens publishes the identity's two public keys, 1,248 bytes, as it signs in, where the den lacks them or holds others; a Dens without the seal, as after a recovery code until the member types it, publishes nothing. A member's record carries the identity's hash (`identity`), SHA-256 of the hash of its X-Wing key and its Ed25519 key, so a change reaches everyone as `member.updated`. The keys themselves come on request, a few members at a time, as a Dens first needs them: as it makes a DM's key. Leaving the den takes the identity, as does starting over, and the member's Dens publishes it again as they come back.
- A member's Dens keeps the identity the den gives for each member the first time it needs it, for the members it shares DMs with and any it compares codes with, trusting it on first use as SSH does a host's key, and checks every one it meets after against what it kept. One that differs is a change: the Dens keeps the new one, the old among those kept before, and says so wherever that member shows (see [Changes](#changes)).

## Codes

- A member's code is 20 digits in groups of four, made from their identity's hash, their ID and the den's ID by 5,200 rounds of SHA-512, each round taking the identity again, as Signal makes its safety numbers; four chunks of five bytes, each modulo 100,000, make five digits each. An identity made to match one takes about 10^20 tries of those. It's the same for everyone who compares with that member, who reads it out once to a group or a call.
- Comparing is each member's choice, at any time, from the profile beside a DM, a member's profile card and the DM's lock, a call's lock and a group's members: the member's own code, to read out, and a box for the other's, typed as they read it out, so a check can't pass by copying one's own screen. A match marks the other verified, with a circled check beside their name, as Appearance's Verified mark shows it (see [In the app](#in-the-app)), until their identity changes; digits that don't match say someone may be in the middle. Each compares the other. The dialog says only what to do, and names nobody, since a member's name could read as part of its words; its i opens the guide, in a new tab, which says where to compare and why.
- Comparing sends nothing: the code comes from the identity the Dens kept, and the mark stays with the member, so the den doesn't learn who compares, or when, which would tell it whose DMs it could tamper with unseen. Your code, in the settings on the den's page, shows the member's own.
- The guide says where to compare, best first: in person; a voice or video call; another den one of them owns, never the den the DM is on, even when it's theirs, since whoever tampered with it could change the digits sent there too. It also says why: a tampered den can set up an impostor elsewhere ("I'm @Y on Twitter, let's compare there"), and hosting your own den narrows that risk, but nothing removes it.

## The record

- What a member's Dens kept, and which members they verified, is their record: for each member, the hash of the identity kept, when, whether it's verified, whether DMs with them hold, the identity they sent anyway at, the identities kept before, eight at most, whose keys still open what was sealed under them, and when it changed, sixteen times at most; and the member's own identities before they started over, eight at most, which signed the changes to their groups they made before. Each device keeps it sealed with the data key, and on the den sealed with the member's seal, bound to the den, the member and the revision it was written at, its length first and padded to a power of four from 16 KiB, so the den learns little from its size. Past 256 KiB, each member keeps the latest of the identities kept before and the last two changes, then the members kept longest ago go, those neither verified nor held first; the member's own identities from before stay.
- Each of the member's connected devices writes it every hour, from a moment picked at random, whether it changed or not, merging what the others wrote, over the revision it merged; one written meanwhile is a conflict, which it merges and writes over in turn. The den tells the member's sessions of each revision, so their other devices merge it at once: a comparison reaches the member's other devices within the hour, while the den sees each device write once an hour whatever happened. A development instance writes it every 20 seconds, for the browser checks.
- Two devices that kept the same identity for a member agree on it: verified where either compared, and held where either holds, unless either compared or sent anyway at it, since a device that never held, as one signed in since, can't tell the member chose. Two that kept different ones show it as a change: the one kept later stands, the other joins those kept before, and the DM holds where either held or the member had verified the older, unless they compared or sent anyway at the later. With three devices at two identities, the order the den's writes come in can lose a Send anyway at the older, so the member chooses again.
- An older Dens of the member's holds only where both devices held, and leaves out where its member sent anyway: its Send anyway lets go on that device alone, and a newer device asks again.
- Starting over takes the record from the den, since the old seal sealed it; the device that started over keeps its own copy, and writes it sealed with the new seal.

## Changes

- When a member's identity changes, every DM with them shows a line where it did, "Bob's keys changed", with Compare, and their mark goes. Where the member had verified them, the DM holds: in the composer's place, their keys changed since the two compared, with Compare and Send anyway, and the service sends nothing until one is chosen, on any of the member's devices, whose record takes it to the rest. The member's own devices show where they started over.

## DM keys

1. A DM's key is 32 random bytes, made by either member's Dens when the DM has no live key both can use: before its first message, after a member started over, or after an identity changed. It's sealed with HPKE to each member's identity, bound to the den, the DM, a random 16-byte ID its maker gives it, and the member.
2. Its statement names the den, the DM, the key's ID, a commitment to the key, its maker, and each member with the hash of the identity it was sealed to, and its maker's identity signs it. Anyone can seal a copy to an identity, so the commitment, SHA-256 of the key and its ID, keeps the den from setting a copy of another key beside a member's statement.
3. A Dens uses a key only once the signature checks against its maker's identity, the one it kept for them or its own, and the statement names the identity it kept for each member: for sending, the one kept now; for reading, any it kept, since what was sealed before a change still opens. So a den that gave one side keys of its own must give both, and a key that names another identity for this member says someone may be in the middle. The key carries its maker's keys, the hash of the X-Wing key and the Ed25519 key, which hash to the identity named for its maker, so a key from before its maker's identity changed still checks.
4. A DM has one live key: two made at once see `409 key_exists`, and the slower takes the live one; one made after an identity changed retires the live key, whose statement names an identity no longer published, as replaced. The den checks a key names the identities published now, and its maker's signature, which keeps honest clients in step.
5. So a DM opens at once, with nothing to type, and a member whose Dens is away reads it once back. A key's event can come a moment after the first message sealed with it, so history that names a key a connected Dens doesn't hold fetches the DM's keys before calling the message changed. Its messages, files and reactions are sealed under its key.

## Legacy DM keys

- A DM's key both members checked before v1.9.0 stays live, both sending and reading under it, each member's copy sealed with their seal, until one of them starts over; then the DM gets a key sealed to identities. Those comparisons checked DM keys, not identities, so nobody starts verified.
- Before v1.9.0 a DM's key came from an exchange of one-time keys between the two members' Dens, whose 32-digit check code each member typed the other's half of; [protocol's Legacy DM keys](../protocol/e2ee.md#legacy-dm-keys) has the exchange, the copies' format and how the den retires one. An exchange still running as the den updated to v1.9.0 or later, or checked by one member alone, retired as its migration ran, so its DM's next message makes a key the new way; what the member who checked sent under it stays theirs alone, as it would have.
- The den starts no exchange for a DM, so an older Dens can't start a key a newer one couldn't use.
- An older Dens publishes no identity, so a DM with its member that needs a new key waits, saying their Dens needs an update, while one whose key both members checked goes on both ways. On a den older than v1.9.0, which keeps no identities, a newer Dens does the same. An older Dens never sees a key sealed to identities, which it would take for the den breaking the protocol, so messages under one don't open there until it updates.

## Messages and photos

- The local service encrypts a DM's text with the DM key, bound to the channel, the author, the message's nonce and the key's ID, before sending, and decrypts what arrives before the page sees it; one that arrives in the clear is the den's own, and shows as one that doesn't open. The browser never holds a key. A channel's ID keeps its kind for good: the service remembers the kind of every channel it held or opened, deleted since or not, with the den's state in its cache, and takes one that comes as another kind, in an event or a snapshot, as the den breaking the protocol, since a DM taken for a text channel would have its next message go unsealed. What's sent to an ID that was a DM, a group or notes is sealed, or fails.
- The den keeps doing everything that doesn't need the text: ordering, history pages, edits with revisions, deletes, read state and unread counts (in a DM every message counts, so the den needn't read one), typing and closing.
- Photos: the sending service strips metadata and makes the thumbnail itself, with the same code the den uses for channel uploads, then encrypts the original and the thumbnail with a fresh key per file. The file's key, dimensions and type travel inside the encrypted message. The den stores two opaque blobs and counts their size against the upload limits.
- Reactions: each member's reactions on a message are one set, which their service seals with the DM's live key, padded to one length, and the den keeps whole against its revision; the den learns that a member's reactions changed, and when, never what they are (see [Reactions](messages.md#reactions)).
- A DM has no polls: the den counts a poll's votes, which a DM's would have sealed from it, and no @everyone, which is staff's in a channel.
- Work that moves to the client: reply quotes (the client decrypts the original itself), search, and compact links. Task checkboxes tick as an ordinary edit against the current revision.
- A call's frames are sealed under keys of their own for each call, apart from the DM's (see [Sealed calls](#sealed-calls)).

## Group DMs

- *What they are.* A group DM is a DM of three to ten members of the den, as Discord's group DMs are. Any member makes one from the Direct messages tab's +, picking two to nine others, or from a DM's profile with Add people, which makes a group of the two and those added and leaves the DM as it is. It may have a name, which any member changes; one without is named by its other members, the first two and how many more past three. Its member pane, where a DM shows the other member's profile, lists its members in the order they joined, each a picture and a name, with their verified mark, and Add people, Rename and Leave. The one who removes others has a line under their name, Made the group, or Can remove members once its maker left; Compare codes and Remove from the group are each row's ⋯ and right click, a sheet on the phone layout.
- *Who changes it.* Any member adds others, up to ten in all, each a member of the den whose Dens publishes an identity. The first of its members in the order they joined removes others: the one who made it, and once they've left, the one who's been in it longest. Anyone leaves. A member who leaves the den, or is removed or banned, leaves its groups, and a group nobody is left in goes, with its messages. A member removed or gone loses the group from their list: it's no longer theirs to see.
- *Changes the den can't make.* Each change is a statement its maker's identity signs: the group made, with its members; members added; a member removed or leaving; a new name. Each names its number in the group's changes, the hash of the change before it, and the group's members after it, in the order they joined, so the den keeps them in order and can't fork them. Every member's Dens checks each change against the members it had: that it follows the one before, by the group's rules, and that its signer, a member who could make it, is the identity it kept for them, keeping it on first use as for a DM. It knows the group's members from its changes alone: someone the den lists without a member's signed add gets no key and shows nowhere. The den's own changes are removals of members who left the den; a member's Dens checks that each takes one member out and nobody in. A change that doesn't check marks the group broken: its member's Dens makes no key for it and sends nothing there, and the page says someone may be in the middle. One signed by an identity the Dens can't place, as a member's before they started over, which it never met, is checked again from the group's start as the record brings the identities the member's other devices kept: the record holds, beside those, the member's own identities before they started over. A member's Dens knows a group from the add that took them in, which names its members then, and checks its changes once it read the record since its latest snapshot. WhatsApp's servers could once add members to groups, since its changes weren't signed (Rösler, Mainka and Schwenk, 2018).
- *Its first change.* Every statement binds the den's ID for the group, as a DM key's binds the DM's, so a group is reserved for its maker first, a channel nobody sees until they make it with its first change, its first key and its name. A member who reserved one and didn't make it gets it again, and one reserved an hour ago goes as the next is reserved. The den tells the group's members of its channel as it's made, an event a moment behind its answer to the maker, so a change the maker makes straight after waits for it, up to five seconds.
- *Keys.* A group has a key for each membership. The member whose change makes a membership, the group made or members added or removed, makes its key, 32 random bytes sealed to each member's identity and signed, as a DM's key is, and sends it with the change. After a member leaves, or the den takes one out, the live key retires, and the first remaining member's Dens to send makes the next. A member's new identity makes a new key too, as in a DM. A member's Dens sends under a group's key only where its statement names the group's members as its changes make them, with the identities kept now. A member added holds the keys from their joining on, as in Signal and WhatsApp: what the others wrote before, they wrote before that member was there to read it, and the den shows them the group from their joining. One removed holds no key made after.
- *Messages.* Sealed under the group's live key as a DM's are, and signed by their author's identity inside what's sealed, since with a key every member holds, a member and the den together could otherwise write as another: the sealed text starts with its signer's ID, their identity's keys in 64 bytes, and the signature, over the message's binding and its text. An edit, or a tick, is signed by whoever made it, the author or one of the members they named; a message whose signer its key doesn't name, or who isn't its author or an editor, shows as one that doesn't open. Each member's set of reactions is signed the same way, at a DM's sealed size. Files, replies, edits, deletes, tasks and read state work as in a DM, and every message counts toward each other member's unread count and notifies as a DM's does, under Direct messages, its title the group's name; no polls, and no @everyone. A member's reactions stay on the messages they reacted to after they leave, as a member's messages do, up to twenty members' sets a message.
- *The name* is sealed under one of the group's keys, padded to one length, so the den never reads it, nor its length; a naming seals it under the live key, and each new key carries it again, so a member added reads it. A name this Dens can't open, under a key it doesn't hold, stays as the den has it.
- *What the den keeps.* A group DM is a channel of kind `group_dm`, with its members in the order they joined, each with the change that took them in and the group's last message as they joined, and its changes in order, each a member's statement and signature; its keys are in the DMs' tables, which keep a copy for each member. The Group DMs migration made `den_channels` again to take the fourth kind (see [Versioning and compatibility](../design.md#versioning-and-compatibility)).
- *Older Dens.* An older Dens closes its connection, as the den breaking the protocol, on a channel of more than two members or a message with more than two members' sets of reactions, so group DMs come with version 2 of the protocol, which the den serves beside 1: a Dens speaking 1 gets nothing of them, in `ready`, events or answers, and an older device of a member in groups goes on without them.

## Notes to self

- *What they are.* Each member has notes in each den: a conversation with themselves, which only their devices read, for what they want at hand on each of them. They're pinned at the top of the Direct messages tab, and open from Notes on the member's own profile card too. Text, files, replies, edits, deletes and ticks work as in a DM. Reactions, polls, mentions, others' editing, spoilers and disappearing times don't, and nothing in them waits to be read or notifies, since all of it is the member's own; they don't close. The den's retention, and the member's upload limit, apply as to a DM.
- *Their key* is made from the member's DM seal by HKDF, salted with the den's ID, under a label of its own (`dens-notes-v1`), as the identity is. Every device holding the seal has it, with nothing to make, keep or hand over, where a key sealed to the member's own identity would be one more thing kept on the den, and the den holds nothing of it. A note is sealed as a DM's message is, under that binding, naming its key by the notes' channel ID; each file under a key of its own, carried in the sealed text, as in a DM.
- *What the den keeps.* A channel of kind `notes` naming its member, one each, made as they first open it (`POST /api/notes`), which only they see; it takes sealed messages alone, each naming the channel's ID as its key. The Notes migration made `den_channels` again to take the fifth kind (see [Versioning and compatibility](../design.md#versioning-and-compatibility)). A member who leaves the den, or is removed, takes their notes with them, files and all, since nobody else could read them.
- *Starting over.* A new seal makes a new key, so the notes from before no longer open: they show as lost, as old DMs do, for the member to delete. A Dens can't tell a note sealed under an old seal from one the den changed, so either shows as lost; both are gone for good.
- *Older Dens.* An older Dens closes its connection, as the den breaking the protocol, on a channel of a kind it doesn't know, so notes come with version 3 of the protocol, which the den serves beside 1 and 2: a Dens speaking either gets nothing of them, in `ready`, events, answers or the change log, and a member's older devices show nothing of their notes until they update. The page offers Notes once its Dens and the den both speak 3.

## Sealed calls

- *Every call sealed.* On a den with `sealed_calls` every call is end-to-end encrypted: each member's page seals every frame its encoders make, their voice's, their screen's and their share's sound's, before it's sent, and opens every frame it receives before it's decoded, with WebRTC's Encoded Transform (`RTCRtpScriptTransform`) in a worker of the page's own, `sframe.worker.js`. The den's SFU forwards each packet's payload under its own headers, as it always has, budgets them by size alone and asks for keyframes over RTCP, so it hears and sees nothing of the calls it carries.
- *The frames.* SFrame (RFC 9605), in its AES-128-GCM suite (`AES_128_GCM_SHA256_128`, 0x0004), with WebCrypto: each frame is its header, the key's ID and a counter in the fewest bytes, then the frame sealed with 16 bytes of tag, the header as the AEAD's data. A member's key goes by a number, the first two bytes of its ID, and each of their tracks by a key ID of its own from it, four times the number and the track's: 0 the voice, 1 the screen, 2 the share's sound. SFrame derives each key ID's AES key and salt from the member's key with it, so no two tracks share a counter. A frame that doesn't open, names a counter its key had among the last 1,024, a key the page lacks or another track's key ID, or comes in the clear, is dropped. A frame with nothing in it, as Opus's DTX hands the transform between its packets of silence, goes on empty, which the browser sends no packet for, as it wouldn't without a transform, but for a few now and then, which reach the others' pages empty and are dropped as empty, not as frames in the clear. Sealing costs a voice about 22 bytes a frame, some 9 kbps at 50 frames a second. The page's tests take RFC 9605's own cases: its 289 headers and its frame under AES_128_GCM_SHA256_128 (`internal/ui/test/testdata/rfc9605.json`).
- *Keys.* A member's Dens makes their key for a call, 16 random bytes, as they join, and a new one whenever anyone joins or leaves, as the den's `voice.state` shows the call, at most one every half second, so a burst of joins and leaves makes one: one who joins can't open what the den might have kept from before, nor one who left what comes after. It seals the key to each other member's identity with HPKE, as a DM's key is, bound to the den, the channel, a random 16-byte ID its maker gives it, and the member, and signs a statement naming the den, the channel, the key's ID and a commitment to it, its maker, and each member it sealed to, with their identity's hash. Copies, commitments and statements have contexts of their own, so none passes for a DM's. The identities come from the den on request as members join, each checked against the one kept for its member, kept on first use as for a DM; a change shows as for a DM (see [Changes](#changes)), and the call seals to the new one, showing it beside them, where a DM would hold.
- *Passing them on.* A key goes over the den socket as `voice.key`, with each member's copy and its maker's identity in 64 bytes, as a DM's key carries it. The den passes each member their copy alone, while both are in the call, and keeps the latest from each while the call lasts, so a member gets theirs as their call starts, as when they move it to another device, or is taken back. A Dens takes another's key only once its statement names this member's own identity, its signature checks against the identity kept for its maker, and its copy opens to what the statement commits to. Each Dens makes its keys afresh as its call is taken back, and they go with the call. `voice.key` has a limit of its own, 30 in 10 seconds a member, which a Dens's one every half second stays within; a key for 29 others, each copy 1,152 bytes, fits in a client's frame.
- *The page's keys.* The member's Dens hands its page the keys: the browser holds a call's keys, and only while it lasts, where it holds no other. The service sends the page the call's keys whole each time one changes, its own and each other member's, without those of members who left, since the page's stream drops what it can't take, and the page asks for them again (`voice.keys`) on a frame under a key it lacks, at most twice a second. A page sends under its own new key a second after it gets it, while the call's other members' Dens pass it to their pages, and opens a member's last key's frames for ten seconds after their new one comes, as it does a member's who left.
- *Whom it seals to.* Every member of the call as the page shows it: the den chooses who's in a call, so a member it added in secret would have to show among them to hear a thing.
- *Sealing in time.* Chromium sends a sender around its transform for good when the sender has none by the end of the task in which `setRemoteDescription` resolves for the offer that made it: a transform set any later, after any `await`, is taken without an error and never runs. So as each offer is applied, in that task and before anything awaits, the page makes each section it sends on send, and seals it: the microphone's, the den's first, and the screen's and its sound's, which the den adds at the member's first share in the call and keeps for later ones. Only then do tracks go on, and only on senders sealed in that task: one sealed any later carries nothing, so a change that broke the order would leave it silent rather than in the clear. One wait between two seals is enough to break it.
- *Each sender checked.* A browser could still send a sender around its seal, as a later Chromium might sooner, so the page holds what each of its senders sent, as `getStats()` says, a screen's frames or a voice's or a sound's packets, against the frames its worker saw on that track, empty ones among them: as a track goes on a sender, every second until the worker has seen frames of that track, and at each summary of the call's statistics after. A sender that sent three or more while the worker saw nothing of its track was sent around its seal, and stops, saying why: a voice leaves the call, a screen ends its share, and a share's sound comes off, the share going on without it, and the den told it has none. That sender carries nothing more in the call, so a later share in it goes without its sound, or doesn't start without its screen. About a second of what such a sender sends reaches the den in the clear, which beats a delay on every call: each sender carrying silence until the worker had seen its frames would let only silence out, but would hold back every voice and share a moment, with a silent source for each sender. A paused screen sends nothing, and a muted voice's frames of silence, even those DTX leaves empty, reach the worker as its words do, so neither stops anything.
- *The soundboard* is the den's own track, which it plays its sounds on, so it stays in the clear: the page opens nothing on the `soundboard` stream, passing its frames as they are, and takes nothing in the clear on any other.
- *Browsers.* Firefox has had `RTCRtpScriptTransform` since 117 and Chromium since 141; Chromium's older `createEncodedStreams` is left out, since every target browser has the newer. On a den that seals its calls the page joins one only where the browser has it, and otherwise says why; Android's WebView has it from 141, so below that the page asks for a newer WebView, with a link to its page in the store, as the app does below its floor of 111. The worker is a module beside the voice worklet, named to the page as the worklet is (`data-sframe-worker`), and the CSP needs nothing more: a worker from the page's own assets falls under `script-src 'self'`.
- *A device without the seal*, as after a recovery code until the member types it, can't open a call's keys, so on a den that seals its calls the page asks for the seal before it joins, with Enter it.
- *The call's lock.* Among the rest of the call's controls in the list's block (see [Voice controls](calls.md#voice-controls)), and beside how the call stands on the phone's bar and in Home's box, a lock says the call is end-to-end encrypted, and opens the member's code to read out and each other member of the call, with their mark or Compare, which compares in place; a comparison made there holds in every call, DM and group with that member after. The call's list marks each member verified, and one whose keys changed since this member verified them, or since the call began, with a warning whose tip says when. On an older den, which doesn't seal calls, the lock is open and says so.
- *Older Dens.* The den's info says it seals calls (`sealed_calls`); on an older den, which doesn't, a call goes unsealed, as it did. A den of v1.9.0 or later seals every call: it refuses a `voice.join` that doesn't say it seals (`"sealed": true`) with `update_needed`, since an older page would send in the clear, which every other page drops, and would hear nothing; an older page shows it as a call that failed. A page that couldn't load the den's state joins as one that doesn't seal, which a den that seals refuses; one whose den came to seal its calls since it joined, as by the update, which ended the call as the den restarted, joins it again sealed. A page of v1.9.0 or v1.10.0 seals its share's sound too late for Chromium, which then sends it in the clear, and every other page drops it (see Sealing in time). A den can't make such a page seal it, and a den that meant to listen wouldn't try, so it refuses nothing for it: that member's shares go without sound until they update.
- *What's logged.* By ID alone, never bytes: each key a Dens makes, with how many members it was sealed to, and each it opens or refuses, with why, at debug, refusals at warn; the den, at debug, whom each key went to; and in the page's notes, the frames it sealed, opened and dropped, by count, on each track, the voices', the screens' and the shares' sound's, with those that came empty, and why it dropped those it dropped: in the clear, under a key it lacks, on another track, at a counter it had, or not opening, so a watcher's notes say at once that a share's sound came in the clear. A sender the page stopped as sent around its seal goes in the log at warn, with its track and what it sent.

## History and starting over

- A new device gets the seal from the device that approves it, makes the member's identity from it, fetches their DM keys, and reads their DM history. A member signing in with a recovery code, with no other device, types their saved seal.
- DM keys don't change on their own. Keeping history costs forward secrecy: whoever gets a device, or the seal, can read what that member can. Signal makes the opposite choice, giving new devices no old messages; Dens keeps history, since members expect their DMs on every device, as with channels.
- A member who has lost their seal and every device starts over: their Dens makes a new seal, and with it a new identity, and the DM history sealed for the old ones stays unreadable to them, while their partners keep theirs, as do their notes from before. Each of their DMs gets a new key, sealed to the new identity, with its next message, and the others see their keys changed. Their own devices show a divider where they started over.
- Starting over takes the den password, so a stolen session can't wipe a member's DM keys. It signs out their other devices there, since those hold the old seal, and they come back through approval. The new seal is the one the install starts new dens with from then on.
- A former member who joins again with another seal than the one they left with starts over the same way; with the same one, their identity, keys and history are as they left them.
- Starting over is also how a member shuts out a stolen device, which holds the seal: signing it out stops it fetching from the den, and a new seal keeps what's written afterwards from it.

## In the app

- The channel list has two tabs: Den and Direct messages. Each opens what it lists, so a den channel never sits open beside the DM list, nor a DM beside the channels, and nobody writes in a channel thinking it's a DM. Each opens what was last open in it, so a member who goes to the den to read or copy something comes back to the same DM: Den the text channel last open in that den, or its first when that one is gone or out of sight; Direct messages the DM last open there, or nothing when there's none or it was closed since, and nothing when the open DM closes, never another the member didn't pick. The DM list still puts the most recently active first. The phone layout's list stays on screen as a tab opens what it lists, which shows beside it.
- Beside a DM, the member list's place shows the other member's profile as their profile card shows it (see [Profiles](members.md#profiles)), and Compare codes under it.
- DMs say they're end-to-end encrypted, with a lock in their header, which opens comparing codes, and a note at their start, with Compare codes. Onboarding and the docs say the den can read channels; the page doesn't repeat it in each one.
- A member this member verified has a circled check beside their name, in the icons' color, in the DM's header, the DM list and their profile, in a call's list and a group's members. How it shows is Appearance's Verified mark, which the browser keeps, as it keeps the theme: Always, Hover, while the pointer is over the member's row or in a profile they opened, or Off, Hover until the member picks; on a touch screen, which has no hover, On or Off, Off until they pick. Where comparing is the subject, the codes and the call's lock, it says so in words whatever this is.
- A call's lock says whether its den seals it, and opens the member's code and Compare for each of the call's members (see [Sealed calls](#sealed-calls)): among the rest of the controls in the call's block, beside how it stands in Home's box, and on the phone's bar.
- A group DM's header shows its name, or its other members', with a lock that opens its members, where each compares codes; its changes show as lines among its messages, "Alice added Carol", by when they were made; and its composer's place says why it can't take messages: this device lacks the member's DM recovery key; its changes are still being checked, or don't check, so someone may be in the middle; nobody else is in it; a member's Dens needs an update; or a member's keys changed since this member verified them, with Compare and Send anyway.
- A DM that can't take messages says why in its composer's place: this device lacks the member's DM recovery key; the den, or the other member's Dens, runs a version too old to seal a new key; or the other's keys changed since this member verified them, with Compare and Send anyway.
- The member's notes are the first row of the Direct messages tab, whose recent DMs follow, and their own profile card has Notes beside Edit profile; the den makes them as either first opens them. Their header says they're end-to-end encrypted, with nothing beside it to open, and their start that only the member's devices read them; a device without the seal says so in the composer's place, with Enter it.
