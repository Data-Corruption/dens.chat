# Calls

Part of [Dens's design](../design.md): voice in a call, the browser's side, signaling, riding out a dropped connection, staff in calls, voice controls and the call timer.

The browser does all client-side media with standard APIs, and the den runs a Pion SFU that forwards packets without decoding. In the client role, the service relays a call's signaling and never touches its media; the Pion SFU runs only when the same install is also acting as a den. The service also links with the member's other installs on its network, which carry their voice keys and a computer's sound, uncompressed, which the service passes from its helper to the link and from the link to the page that listens, never a call's (see [Relay](helper.md#relay)).

## Voice channels

- Calls happen in voice channels. Clicking one joins its call: the member's browser opens one `RTCPeerConnection` to the den, which carries their microphone to the den and everyone else's audio back.
- Anyone who can see a voice channel can join its call, and sees who is in it, listed by name, and who is muted. A staff-only voice channel's call is staff's, as its text channels are.
- A call's rows show each name with its crown or shield, a long one scrolling to its end under the pointer as in the member list (see [Profiles](members.md#profiles)). Every other member's row has a ⋯ for their menu, which a right click opens at the pointer.
- A member is in one call at a time, on one device: joining from another device, or in another channel, moves them there. An install holds one call, which belongs to the page that joined it, so closing or reloading that page leaves the call.
- The den ends a member's call as soon as they can't see its channel: they leave or are removed or banned, the device is signed out, a role change hides a staff-only channel, or the channel is deleted.
- A call rides out a dropped connection: the den holds it for 30 seconds after its socket closes, and the page takes it back once it reconnects (see [Riding out a dropped connection](#riding-out-a-dropped-connection)). A den restart ends every call, and the page joins again once the den is back, trying a few times before it gives up and says why.
- Mute is the member's own: the page stops sending their microphone's sound, and the others see the mark. So is deafen: the page plays nothing of the call and mutes the member, and the others see that they hear nothing either, beside the muted mark, since deafening mutes too. Hearing again puts the microphone back as it was, and unmuting while deafened means hearing again too.
- A member's menu in a call holds what the member here does first, their volume and Mute, then staff's, Mute for everyone and Move to, and Disconnect last. Mute plays them at 0 for this member alone, and Unmute gives back the volume they had, full for one slid to 0 by hand; the browser keeps what Unmute gives back (`DENS_UNMUTED_VOLUMES`), as it keeps the volume, and a volume slid past 0 forgets it.
- Each member sets how loud everyone else plays for them, from 0 to 200%, in the call's list, 100% marked at the middle of their menu's slider. The browser keeps it by den and member, as it keeps the microphone and speaker, so someone turned down stays down in the next call, which their menu's slider shows; the list shows no figure. Up to 100% it's the volume of the media element that plays them. An element plays no louder, so a member past 100% plays through a gain in the call's context, into an element of its own, since Firefox's contexts can't choose a speaker; their own element plays on, muted, since Chromium decodes a stream only while an element plays it. Past 100% a loud voice may clip. A page from before v1.11.0 reads a volume past 100% as 100%. Android's app plays every member through Web Audio already (see [android.md's Calls](../android.md#calls)), so there the gain goes past 1. The voice settings' call volume turns everyone down at once, under their own levels, and stays at most 100%; the browser keeps it too: a phone's loudspeaker plays a call loud even at its lowest call volume.
- Staff can disconnect a member of a lower rank from a call, or mute them, which the den enforces (see [Staff in calls](#staff-in-calls)).
- Speaking indicators: each page measures the audio it plays for each member, and its own microphone's as sent, and rings the avatar of whoever is speaking. Only members in the call see them, since only their pages play its audio. A member holding push to talk rings too, sound or not, which the den tells the call's members alone (see [Voice controls](#voice-controls)).
- A voice channel's call holds 15 members, and a den's calls 30 in all, unless the owner sets other caps (see [Limits for calls](sfu.md#limits-for-calls)). Each member's audio goes out once to every other member of their call, so the den's upload grows with the square of a call's size: a full call of 15 sends about 3.5 Mbps while two people talk, and up to 26 if everyone does (see [the bandwidth table](sfu.md#bandwidth-at-target-scale-approximate)).
- Each voice channel has a bitrate, which staff set in its settings like the rest of the channel: from 16 to 128 kbps in steps of 8, and 96 for a new channel. The den writes it into every offer for the channel's call, so a change reaches the call in progress with each member's next offer. A lower bitrate costs the den less upload: each member talking sends it to every other member of the call.
- Calls are end-to-end encrypted: each member's page seals its frames with SFrame before they leave it, under keys their Dens seals to the call's other members, so the den forwards what it can't open (see [Sealed calls](e2ee.md#sealed-calls)). Media is encrypted between each browser and the den as well (DTLS-SRTP). On a den whose info doesn't say `sealed_calls`, calls go unsealed, as the call's lock says.
- DMs have no calls: calls stay in voice channels until members ask for calls of their own (see [loose-ideas.md](../loose-ideas.md)).
- The den's offer, which carries the fingerprint its DTLS must match, comes over the den socket, so a call reaches the den the member signed in to, as their messages do.
- Members in a call never see each other's addresses: everything passes through the den, which sees the address each member's media comes from, as it sees the address of each of their connections already.

## Client side (browser)

- Mic: `getUserMedia` with `echoCancellation`, `noiseSuppression` and `autoGainControl` on. The member picks the microphone and the speaker in the call's settings, the speaker through `setSinkId`.
- Opus at up to the voice channel's bitrate, 96 kbps unless staff set another, where a browser left to itself sends 32: every offer from the den asks for it (`maxaveragebitrate`), and a browser takes a new offer's parameters for its encoder. Pion builds each offer after the first from the parameters the browser answered with, which don't ask for it, so the den writes its own into every offer.
- Screen: `getDisplayMedia`, which goes through xdg-desktop-portal and PipeWire on Wayland (see [Screen share](sfu.md#screen-share)).
- The peer connection names no STUN or TURN servers, so a call contacts nothing but the den.
- The page offers calls only where `RTCPeerConnection` exists, and screen share only where `getDisplayMedia` does too, and says so where the browser can't, from what the browser has rather than what its user agent says: GNOME Web has had no WebRTC since WebKitGTK 2.54, and Android's WebView, which says it's Linux, has no `getDisplayMedia`. On a den that seals its calls it joins one only where `RTCRtpScriptTransform` exists too, which seals and opens each frame in a worker of the page's own (see [Sealed calls](e2ee.md#sealed-calls)).
- The page's `Permissions-Policy` grants the microphone and screen capture to the page itself (`microphone=(self)` and `display-capture=(self)`), and the camera, to scan a sign-in's code (`camera=(self)`), so no frame gets any of them.
- A page its browser froze plays its call again as it thaws: freezing pauses a page's media elements, and nothing plays them again, so the page plays them on `resume`. Chromium freezes a hidden page a minute after its sound stops, which Android's app keeps from happening to its call's page (see [android.md](../android.md#calls)).
- Noise suppression: browsers' own suppressor takes out steady noise such as fans and hum, but not keyboards, a TV or other voices. So RNNoise runs instead unless a member turns it off: Xiph's small noise-suppression network (BSD), compiled to WebAssembly and run in an AudioWorklet between the microphone and the call, at 48 kHz in 10 ms frames. The browser still cancels echo first, since that needs the raw microphone and what the browser plays. Only one suppressor runs at a time, so the browser's is off (`noiseSuppression: false`) while RNNoise is on, and the encoder gets the cleaned audio. Others' audio keeps playing through media elements, which the echo canceller hears; in Android's app, through an audio context, and a phone's own echo canceller hears what the phone plays. The browser keeps the choice, as it keeps the microphone and speaker, and a change applies mid-call by replacing the track the call sends, without a new offer. Where RNNoise can't start, as in a browser that won't run its audio, the call goes on with the browser's own suppressor, and the call's settings say so; the member's choice stands, for the next call or another browser.
- `scripts/rnnoise.sh` builds RNNoise with the wasi-sdk and binaryen the media module pins, and writes the module into the page's assets. Its code is RNNoise 0.2's release, which matches the v0.2 tag's file for file; its model is the one that tag names, since the release carries another, without the float copies the model keeps for debugging. RNNoise's generic vector code, which WebAssembly builds take, includes Opus's `os_support.h`, which RNNoise doesn't carry, so `internal/ui/rnnoise` supplies the one macro it needs. The module is committed, as the media module is: CI builds it again and fails if it differs.
- The module is 1.47 MB and imports nothing. The page loads and compiles it only when a member turns RNNoise on, and posts the compiled module to the worklet, which every target browser takes. Compiling WebAssembly takes `'wasm-unsafe-eval'`, which the page's CSP carries: it allows WebAssembly but not `eval`, and gives a script in the page nothing it couldn't do already.
- It costs about 220 µs of one core per 10 ms frame in every target browser, about 2%, and adds 30 ms to a call: RNNoise's own 20 ms, and 10 ms of gathering 480-sample frames from the browser's 128-sample blocks. It takes steady noise down by about 50 dB and keyboard clicks by 20, and keeps other voices, since it tells speech from noise, not one speaker from another. Dens uses the regular model: RNNoise's "little" one costs 40% less and is half the size, but does less on steady noise, and 2% of a core is cheap. The spike that measured all this is `spikes/README.md` at commit c5a882b.

## Signaling

The page signals over its event socket to its local service, which relays on the den socket. The den makes every offer and the page only answers, so the two never offer at once:

1. The page asks its local service to join a voice channel, and the service sends `voice.join` to the den.
2. The den sets up its side and sends an offer: one audio section that receives the member's microphone, and one that sends each other member's audio, labeled with their member ID.
3. The local service checks the offer, replaces any candidates it carries with the den's media addresses (see [Media addresses](sfu.md#media-addresses)), and hands it to the page that joined.
4. The page attaches its microphone and answers. The local service takes the browser's candidates out of the answer, and the addresses in its connection lines, and passes it to the den.
5. Media flows directly between the browser and the den, over UDP or, where UDP is blocked, TCP, encrypted with DTLS-SRTP.
6. When someone joins or leaves the call, the den sends each other member a new offer, which adds or retires that member's section. One offer is outstanding per member at a time, and one not answered within 15 seconds ends that member's call.
7. When the page's connection to the media ports breaks, as on a move to another network, it asks for an ICE restart. The den's next offer carries new ICE credentials, the local service writes the den's addresses into it afresh, and the browser checks its new paths. DTLS carries on, so the call keeps its keys and its sections.

The messages are in [protocol.md](../protocol/calls.md#voice).

## Riding out a dropped connection

A call's signaling and its media take different paths: the den socket runs from the member's local service through Caddy, and the media from their browser straight to the den's media ports. Losing one needn't end the call.

- When a call's socket closes, the den holds the call for 30 seconds. The member stays in it, their audio flows both ways if its path still does, and offers wait. That covers a Caddy reload, a blip that broke only the socket, and at least five of the client's reconnects.
- The page keeps its peer connection while its den is away, and asks to resume the call once the den is back. The den moves the held call to the new socket, which must be the same device's, and makes a new offer if the call changed meanwhile. The others notice nothing but a gap in that member's audio, if there was one. They see no mark during the hold, since the den can't tell whether the audio still flows.
- A socket the den closes for good ends its call at once: a removal, a ban, a revoked device, a new password, starting over, leaving and logging out all do. Any other close holds the call.
- An offer that was out when the socket closed waits, since Pion can't take an offer back, and goes again once the call is resumed. A page that already answered it sends the same answer again.
- A call that ends while held stays ended, and the resume says why, so a member disconnected by staff in that window isn't brought back by their own page. A call that can't be resumed, because the hold ran out or the den restarted, is joined again from the start.
- When the browser's own connection to the media ports breaks, as when a laptop moves from Wi-Fi to Ethernet or leaves home, the page asks for an ICE restart (step 7 above). The local service looks the den's addresses up again for it, since split DNS can give another address on another network.
- The local service holds nothing of a call across a dropped connection: it passes the page's resume on to the den, and writes the den's addresses into its offers afresh. So a call also rides out the service restarting, as on an update, if it's back within the hold. A service that stops closes its pages' sockets but leaves their calls alone, since only a page that goes away leaves its call.
- Closing or reloading the page ends its call at once, and a call whose media stops answering connectivity checks for 30 seconds ends as failed.

### What's logged

The logs say which connection broke first, and when: the page's stream to its service, the media path, or the den socket. They name dens, channels, members and pages by ID, never by address, a den by its ID as Manage den shows it, with its name beside its connection's lines.

- In the member's service, at `info`: each close of a page's stream, with how long it was open and why (the page went away or closed it with a code, the connection broke, a ping went unanswered, a write stalled or failed, it fell behind, or the service is stopping); each close of a den connection, with how long it was up and why (the den closed it with a code, the connection broke, a ping went unanswered, a frame didn't decode, or the den broke the protocol); and a call ending because the stream of the page that held it closed. A page whose stream drops while its tab stays open loses its call this way, while the page expects the den to hold it, so it looks like a leave and a join.
- In the den, at `info`: each close of a member's socket, with how long it was open and why, the same causes from the den's side, and the den's own closes with their codes; and each call's start, end and why.
- In the member's service, at `debug`: each den connection coming up, and one that fails to; the call's signaling as it passes (joins and resumes asked for, offers in, answers out, ICE restarts, and the den taking a call back), each with how long it took; and the page's notes, which it sends over its stream: each change in its connection to the den's media ports, with ICE's, each restart it asks for, each time it joins again or gives up, and why; whether its tab is hidden, and whether its browser froze or thawed it; and at each change and every 10 seconds of a call, a summary from `getStats`: the path's protocol and its candidates' types, packets and bytes each way, packets lost on the way in, jitter and round-trip time, what it sent a second since the last, what its encoder aims at, and how far its voice processor runs behind the clock. The service logs a note's fields alone, its words only from fixed lists, so no text the page holds reaches the log, and at most two notes a second from a page after the 64 it may keep while its stream is down, which go once it's back, saying how long before.
- In the den, at `debug`: each call's ICE and DTLS states, its path's protocol and its candidates' types, offers sent, answered with how long it took, and unanswered, and restarts asked for.
- The settings' Logs card downloads the newest 32 MiB of the logs, oldest first, as one text file (`GET /api/logs`, for a paired browser alone, as an attachment not kept), and says what they hold, since an installed Dens keeps them in the service's data directory, out of its desktop user's reach. The service writes out what it holds first, and reads under the lock rotation takes, so no file is renamed under it. A server has no page, so `dens logs` saves the same file into the folder it's run in, and `dens logs --level` sets the level as the card does, over the control endpoint.

## Staff in calls

- Staff disconnect members of a lower rank from calls, and mute them, under the rank rules of removal: moderators act on members, and the owner on anyone else.
- A disconnected member can join again at once. One who keeps coming back is removed or banned like anyone else.
- A staff mute makes the den forward nothing from the member, whatever their page sends. It belongs to the member, not the call, so leaving and joining again doesn't shed it. It lasts until staff lift it, the member leaves the den, or the den restarts, since the den keeps it in memory. Everyone who can see the call sees the mark.
- The member's page says what happened, without naming who did it.
- Staff move a member from one call to another: with a mouse, by dragging the member's row in a call onto another voice channel, but for a row whose menu is open, whose volume slider would drag the row along, and anywhere by Move to in the member's menu in the call, a row that opens the den's other voice channels the member could join beside the menu, or on a phone's sheet in its place, a staff-only one only for a member who's staff. Staff move anyone but the owner and themselves, a moderator another moderator too, since a move silences nobody; muting and disconnecting keep the rank rule. The den checks that, and what joining does, that the member sees the channel and its call has room, then sends `voice.move` to the socket holding the member's call. Their page joins the channel as if they'd picked it, sealed, muted and deafened as they were, which ends the old call, a share with it, as picking another channel does; the den doesn't count that join toward the member's limit on joining. A call held while its socket is away can't be told, so the move is refused, and a Dens from before v1.10.0 ignores the event, its member staying where they are. A member in no call, or in that channel's already, stays as they are. The page offers it where the den says it moves members (`move_members`).

## Voice controls

- A member sends their voice one of two ways, chosen in the settings' Voice section. With voice activity, the default, the page sends only while they speak. With push to talk, it sends only while they hold their key. Where the page has the call's button, a touch screen's, its role takes that choice's place (see [The phone layout](phone.md)).
- Voice activity is automatic by default: it opens for whatever RNNoise, which judges every 10 ms frame, takes for speech. A member can instead set a level with a slider, against a live meter of their microphone, and the page sends what's above it. Without RNNoise, the level is the only way.
- The gate runs in the audio thread on every 10 ms frame, after the noise suppressor, so it opens on a word's first syllable, not up to 100 ms late as a meter on the main thread would. It holds for 300 ms after speech stops, so words' ends and the gaps between them aren't cut, and it fades in and out over a few milliseconds, so it doesn't click. With RNNoise off, the same processor runs without it, for the gate alone. Where the processor can't run at all, the microphone goes as it is, with the browser's own suppressor: voice activity then sends everything, push to talk holds the track back itself, and the settings say so.
- Keys: push to talk, toggle mute (which serves as toggle to talk), push to mute, which mutes while held, and toggle deafen. They work while the page has focus, as with any web page, and in other programs through the helper (see [Global shortcuts](helper.md#global-shortcuts)). A key held when the page loses focus counts as released, unless the helper covers it. They don't fire while the member types in a text field, unless the key types nothing there: a function key, or one held with Ctrl, Alt or Meta. A key fires while its own modifiers are held, whatever else is, so a player holding Shift to run still pushes to talk; of two actions on one key, the one with more modifiers takes the press. A mouse's side button can be a key too, and the call's button presses them on a touch screen.
- Push to talk's release delay, from 0 to 2,000 ms in steps of 50, 0 unless set, keeps the gate open and the ring lit that long after a release, the page's, the helper's or the call's button's, and the den hears the release as it ends. A press within the delay keeps it open. The browser keeps it with how the member sends.
- What the gate holds back goes as silence, and every offer asks for Opus's discontinuous transmission (`usedtx=1`), so a member who isn't speaking sends a packet every 400 ms instead of 50 a second. That keeps big calls cheap for the den's upload: silent members cost almost nothing.
- Speaking rings follow what's sent, since they measure what each page plays: a member the gate holds back rings nowhere, and the member's own ring lights while their gate is open. A deafened member's page lights no ring, by the meters, push to talk or a sound, since they hear none of it.
- Push to talk rings while the key is held, sound or not, as Discord's does: a member holding it, unmuted, rings for everyone in the call. Their own page lights it at once. For the others, the page tells the den as the key goes down and up (`voice.talk`), and the den passes on who in the call holds it, to the call's members alone (`voice.talking`). The den lights a member's ring at most five times a second, and never while they're muted or staff muted; letting go, muting, leaving the call or the socket closing puts it out, and a page that takes a call back, or joins again, says once more whether the key is down. Voice activity's rings stay as they are, from what each page plays.
- At the foot of a den's channel list, the member's panel shows their picture and name, which open their profile to edit, and the cog for the settings. The panel is 73 pixels tall, its middle level with the message box's beside it, and keeps the channel list's color.
- While in a call, a block sits above the panel and holds the call in a row: an arrow that opens the rest of its controls, and buttons to share their screen, where the browser can, mute, deafen, open the Voice settings and leave, spread evenly. The arrow points up, and down once open, and opens the rest as a grid of buttons over the row, four across, so the row keeps its width as controls are added: the soundboard, where the den has one, and the call's lock. What one of them opens stands in the grid's place, and the arrow closes it, as a press elsewhere or Escape closes the grid. A row with neither to show has no arrow, and on a call its den doesn't seal the arrow takes the warning's color, as the lock does. How the call stands shows on the speaker of its channel's row in the list, green once connected and yellow while joining, connecting or reconnecting, with the state in the row's label, as "Lounge, connected"; the block shows a line saying how the call stands only while it isn't connected, or while it's in another den, which the line names, a tap on it going there. What the member should know of the call, such as a staff mute, shows in the block above the row, and while they share, the share's controls show there in a row of their own. The block and the message box are a shade lighter than the page, the page's background mixed in OKLab with 4% white (`bg-bar`), which keeps its hue in every browser, as a relative color lost it in a Chromium, and leaves a white page's as white, so a call shows at a glance against the channel list and the panel. On the other pages the call shows boxed, with the channel and how it stands spelled out, and the soundboard to mute's left.
- Settings open as a dialog over whatever the page shows, so a den and a call stay in view. It closes with its ✕, Escape or a click outside it, as every dialog does; a click whose press started inside, as a selection let go past its edge, doesn't count. While the settings wait for a key, Escape or a click elsewhere cancels the wait. Appearance is a page of its own (see [The page's parts](page.md#the-pages-parts)), and the Voice section holds the microphone and speaker, noise suppression, how the member sends, with push to talk's release delay, the keys, and the screen share, which the call's cog opens.
- Joining waits on the microphone, which the browser may first ask the member for, and the call says so after a moment. The voice processor's audio context is made in the click that joins, since a browser may hold back one made after its prompt until another click. Where the service says the page's audio may start without a click, as in Android's app, it's made once the microphone is open, as is the call's, so the phone plays them as a call's. The browser keeps all of these, as it keeps the microphone and speaker, except the keys, which the service keeps so the helper can watch them.

## The call timer

Each call shows its length on its channel's row, to everyone who sees the channel, against the channel's longest call.

- *What it times.* A call, from its first member's join to its last one's leaving, held calls and a member alone counting, since a timer that stops while someone waits alone reads as broken.
- *The den.* A call's room keeps when it began, and `voice.state` and `ready` give each call's `elapsed_ms` and `record_ms`, its channel's longest call as the call began, 0 for none; a den older than v1.13.0 sends neither, and its calls show no length. The record is `den_channels.call_record`, NULL until a call there ends. The den writes a call's length as it ends longer than the record, a den's stop ending it too, and by its timer at each whole minute of the call past the record it kept, so a den that dies loses a minute at most. Each record is decided under the call registry's lock and written once it's let go, in the order decided, and one decided but not yet written wins over the database's as a call starts. It keeps the length alone, not who was in the call or when.
- *Clocks.* The member's service turns `elapsed_ms` into the call's start on its own clock as each frame arrives, `started_at` in the call it gives the page, so a den's clock and a computer's may disagree. A frame's start is late by however long it took to come, so the service keeps the earliest, and a page's timer never steps back; one later by more than five seconds is a new call, or a clock that was set.
- *The row.* A voice channel with a call shows its length at the row's right, `m:ss`, then `h:mm:ss`, in figures of one width, from one timer in the page that reads the clock four times a second while a length shows. Its tip names the record, "Longest call: 1:02:03", or says there's none yet. The row holds no lock; the call's block does, among the rest of its controls (see [Voice controls](#voice-controls)).
- *The record.* While a call is longer than its `record_ms`, its length shows as a rainbow moving along it, each color mixed with the theme's text so it reads on light themes and dark; as it passes, fireworks play over the row for about two seconds on every page showing the row then, and a page that opens later shows the rainbow alone. A channel with no record yet shows neither. Both are the stylesheet's, with no library; under `prefers-reduced-motion` the fireworks don't play and the rainbow holds still.
- *Reset.* A voice channel's settings show staff its longest call, with Reset, which asks first, since every member's timer counts against it. A call going on as it's reset shows from then on with nothing to beat and writes no record, so a call left running, as one member idling over a weekend, can't set it again as it ends.
- *The setting.* Appearance's Calls holds Call timer, on until the member turns it off, which hides the length, the rainbow and the fireworks; the browser keeps it (`DENS_CALL_TIMER`), as it keeps Density.
