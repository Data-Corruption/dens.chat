# Messages

Part of [Dens's design](../design.md): messages, emoji, reactions, polls, @everyone, links and forwarding.

## Text and replies

- Plain text with a small markdown subset, attachments, edits, deletes and replies. No raw HTML, ever.
- The subset: bold, italic, strikethrough, inline code, code blocks, quotes, spoilers, @mentions, bare URLs, and the den's emoji by name (see [Emoji](#emoji)). There are no `[label](url)` links, so a link always shows where it goes, but for a link to a message, whose chip shows where it points in Dens, from what the member's own Dens knows (see [Links to messages](#links-to-messages)). The page parses it into Preact nodes whose text is always text, never markup, and a test fails on any script that uses `innerHTML` or another way to turn a string into markup. Every message is someone else's input, so the parser takes time in proportion to a text's length, whatever its delimiters, and the page parses each text once rather than on every render. Each line's code spans are found first, in one pass, and each delimiter's closers in one pass the first time it opens; a code span takes precedence over a delimiter around it, as CommonMark's do, in the den's copy of the parser too (`CleanLinks`). At 4,000 characters the den's worst case, unclosed openers among links it rewrites, takes 4 ms where the parser before took 74; at 16,000, four times a message's limit, the page parses a hostile line in about a millisecond where it took up to 700.
- A reply quotes its original, with the author and first line, and a click jumps there. The den sends that preview with the reply, so the quote reads without the original loaded, however far back it is. Once the original is deleted, the reply says so.
- A message's actions are in a menu that opens from a ⋯ at its top right: Reply, Forward (see [Forwarding](#forwarding)), Add reaction, Edit, Let others edit on the member's own, Copy text, Copy message link, Remove reactions, staff's, and Delete, each where the member may. The ⋯ ends a bar that shows on hover or focus, of react, a smiley that opens the emoji picker (see [Reactions](#reactions)), Reply, and Edit on the member's own message; the bar takes the focus while hidden, so Tab reaches every message's, and the channels' ⋯ and those of members in a call follow the same rule, showing always on touch screens, which have no hover. A touch screen's messages have no ⋯: a long press opens their menu (see [The phone layout](phone.md)). The menu opens below the ⋯, or above it where the list has more room there; the arrow keys, Home and End move through it, and Escape, Tab or a press elsewhere closes it, Escape giving the focus back to the ⋯. Edit takes the focus to the message with the caret at its end, under it one row of Save, Cancel and the keys, Reply to the composer, and copying says so beside the bar for a moment.
- With a mouse, a right click on a message opens its menu where the pointer is, and on a member's row in the member list or a call opens theirs (see [Profiles](members.md#profiles) and [Voice channels](calls.md#voice-channels)). Over a link, a picture, a video, a player, the message box or text selected in the message, the browser's own menu opens instead, so copying and Save image work as on any page; a member's picture and an emoji open the page's. A menu opened at the pointer turns up or left where the list it's in has no room.
- Deleting asks first, in a dialog that shows the message's author and first line, and says it goes for everyone, with its files, for good. Staff deleting another member's message see whose it is, by name and username. The dialog opens on its Delete button, so Enter deletes, and Escape or Cancel gives the focus back to the ⋯.
- A message's text that runs taller than about 16 lines, such as a pasted log or a wall of blank lines, shows its top under a fade, with Show more; Show less folds it back. It's held at that height from its first paint, so folding never moves the list, and the list's bounds and pages count it at that height. Nothing limits lines beyond the 4,000 characters: the den can't read a DM, and a hostile den or sender wouldn't keep a limit, so the page folds whatever arrives.
- Each member's read position per channel lives on the den, so it follows them across devices. Channels show unread state and mention counts: a channel or DM with messages unread has its name bold at full strength and a pill at its row's left edge, which tells it from one read in any theme and on a phone, while one read is a little fainter, at 85%, which keeps it at 4.5:1 or more against the list in every theme but valentine, whose text is 4.97:1 at full. Notifications are each member's to turn on, by kind (see [Notifications](notifications.md#notifications)). A channel's, a DM's and a group's Mark as read, and Esc in the one shown, move the position to its newest message, and the den's Mark all as read moves every one the member sees in one request, `POST /api/read`, offered where the den's info says `read_all` (see [The page's parts](page.md#the-pages-parts)).
- The den counts mentions and the page highlights them by one rule, written out in [protocol's Messages](../protocol/messages.md#messages) and tested on both sides against the same cases. Mentions in code or inside links don't count, so a link to someone's profile doesn't ping them. Staff's @everyone mentions every member who can see the channel (see [@everyone](#everyone)).
- Edits carry the revision they were made against, and the den refuses a stale one. Nobody's edit silently overwrites another's, whether it comes from a second device or a co-editor. The member sees the newer text, with their own draft kept to reapply.
- **Shared messages:** when posting, the author can name up to 20 other members who may also edit the message, among those who can see the channel. Editors can't delete it, and only the author changes who may edit, from the message's Let others edit, a dialog of its own. It suits shared lists and plans.
- **Task checkboxes:** lines starting with `[ ]` or `[x]` render as checkboxes in any message. Anyone who may edit the message can tick one, which the den applies as a change of its own, so two people ticking different boxes at once never lose a tick. A tick names its task's text, so one against a list that changed meanwhile is refused rather than landing on another line. Everyone else sees the boxes read-only.
- The composer's + sits inside its box at the left, as the smiley does at the right. It opens a small menu above it, or a sheet on the phone layout, for attaching files, choosing who else may edit, in a channel making the message a poll (see [Polls](#polls)), and last Formatting help. The smiley opens the emoji picker, and the box completes emoji, mentions and channels as the member types (see [Emoji](#emoji)). Formatting help is what a member looks up while writing: the markdown subset, each example drawn as a message shows it, and the composer's keys, Enter, Shift+Enter, Ctrl+Enter, ↑, Esc and completing's, with All keyboard shortcuts under them; the rest of writing a message, mentions and @everyone, links and how they're shortened, task lines, shared editing, files and polls, is the site's guide, linked at its foot.
- Enter sends a message, or saves an edit, except with the caret inside a code block, after an opening fence of three backticks with no closing one before it, where it starts a new line, as Shift+Enter always does; Ctrl+Enter sends from anywhere. With a finger, ↵ always starts a line, and a button sends (see [The phone layout](phone.md)). The fence the caret ends counts, so Enter after an opening one starts the code, and after a closing one sends.
- The message box is an island at the foot of its pane, as in other chat apps: on the page's own background, 16 pixels in from the pane's sides and 12 above its foot, in the lighter shade the call's block has (`bg-bar`), with the theme's corners for fields and an edge the theme's border wide, drawn inside it, as strong as a field's and darker while the box has the focus. The messages end at its top edge, and a line's island, 48 pixels, stands level with the member's panel beside it. What goes with the message, a reply, files, a poll and who else may edit, stands inside it above the text. It grows with its text, wrapped lines too, up to half of its pane's height, or of what a phone's keyboard leaves of it, then scrolls; it takes its height from a copy of its text laid out out of sight, so sizing it never moves the list. A message being edited keeps a box of its own, which shows its focus by a darker border alone, without DaisyUI's ring outside it, which doubled the border.
- Delete removes the row and its files; `secure_delete` overwrites the freed pages. A delete event tells clients to purge caches, and the den keeps the message's ID and channel, never its text, author or files, for 30 days, so a client that was away drops its copies when it comes back (see [What changed while away](sync.md#what-changed-while-away)); where staff deleted another member's message, its line in the audit log names the author, for 90 days (see [Staff's tools](members.md#staffs-tools)). Each member the message counted for, in a DM or a group every other member and in a channel those it mentioned, hears their read state: their unread count without it, and where it was all that lay past their read position, the position past it, since a client knows a channel's newest message only as messages come, and a DM whose last message went unread would otherwise stay unread until its next snapshot.
- Optional den-wide retention, off by default: messages older than a period the owner sets are deleted with their files (see [Retention](sync.md#retention)).
- Members react to messages with emoji, which show as chips under each, a DM's sealed by its members' clients (see [Reactions](#reactions)).
- Messages show as Appearance's *Messages* says: Density, Default, as they always have, or Compact, which shows each as a line of its time, its author's name and its text. The name floats at the text's start, so the text wraps under it, and a quote beside it starts past it, its bar too; a reply's quote sits above, and its files, players, polls and reactions below, from the name's start. Every message shows its name, not only a run's first, and none its author's picture, nor does the quote. Phones get it too, with the same long press. The browser keeps the choice, as it keeps Animations, and until the member makes one it's Default.
- A channel's message may be a poll, its text the question, whose members vote on its choices (see [Polls](#polls)).
- A DM's or a group's messages may disappear a while after they're sent, as any of its members sets (see [Disappearing messages](sync.md#disappearing-messages)).
- Removal and bans revoke all of a member's keys and close their sockets at once (see [Leaving, removal and bans](members.md#leaving-removal-and-bans)).

## Emoji

A den has emoji of its own, which staff add, beside Unicode's. Members write them by name in the markdown subset, pick them from a picker beside the composer, and complete them as they type.

- *The den's emoji.* An emoji is one of the den's named files (see [The soundboard](sfu.md#the-soundboard)), of kind `emoji`: a name, an image, and who added it and when. Its name is 2 to 32 of `a-z`, `0-9` and `_`, lowercased on entry as a username is, so `:name:` reads the same in every message, and unique among the den's emoji; a sound may share it, each kind having names of its own. Staff, the moderators and the owner, add an emoji in Manage's Emoji section, beside the Soundboard, by an image and a name, which the page suggests from the file's, and there rename or delete each, the section saying what a rename and a delete do to the messages that wrote it. Its image opens to every member, since every page draws it, where a sound's opens to staff alone. As a sound's does, the image counts toward the den's space, not its uploader's, and stays through retention, the sweep of waiting uploads and its uploader leaving.
- *Its image.* An emoji shows at 48 pixels at most, so its image is at most 128 on its longer side, for dense screens, its shape kept and never enlarged. The page draws a still one into a new PNG of that size, as it crops a picture, which leaves the original's metadata behind; an animated GIF or WebP goes to the member's local service as it is, which makes it a GIF of that size by the path animated pictures take (see [Animated pictures](files.md#animated-pictures)), with `emoji` as its picture. The den takes a still image every page draws, a PNG, JPEG, GIF or WebP, or an animated GIF, which only a member's own service makes; at most 128 pixels each way, and 256 KiB, or 512 KiB animated; an upload of the staff member's own waiting to be used.
- *The owner's limit,* in Manage's Emoji, under its emoji: how many emoji the den keeps, 50 by default, 0 to 500. It holds as an emoji is added: lowering it leaves the emoji already added. 0 turns emoji off and keeps them: messages show their names as text, the picker and completion offer none, and nobody adds one. The den keeps it in a column of its own row, `emojis`, the fourth migration (see [Data across versions](../design.md#data-across-versions)), and sends it in its info as `emoji_limits`, at 0 too, so a page tells emoji turned off from a den older than them, which sends none and shows no Emoji section.
- *In the text.* The text holds an emoji's name, `:custom_heart:`, as Slack's does, rather than its name and ID, `<:custom_heart:123>`, as Discord's does. A notification's text, an older page, a DM's sealed text and Copy text read it as it was written, the markdown subset gains nothing its readers can't read, and the den never parses emoji. The cost: renaming an emoji turns its uses back into text, and a name deleted and given to another emoji shows the new one in old messages.
- *The rule,* the page's alone: `:`, then 2 to 32 of `a-z`, `0-9` and `_` in any case, then `:`, the first colon not after a letter, digit or `_` and the second not before one, so a time such as 10:30:45 holds none; never in code or a link. Within a delimiter's span the span's start counts as no letter, so `_:name:_` is an emoji in italics. A name the den has shows its image, `:name:` in its tip and as its words, so copying a selection with it copies its name and an image that doesn't load reads as written, in a square box of the line's height that the image fills, contained, as it loads, so the list never moves; one it hasn't stays text. A spoiler hides the images in it until it opens. A message of nothing but emoji, ten at most, Unicode's or the den's, with only spaces between, shows them at 48 pixels: the page tells a Unicode emoji by the browser's own `\p{RGI_Emoji}`, so it needs no list for that. Emoji show wherever the markdown subset does: messages, reply quotes, bios and channel descriptions; and a DM, which lives on its den, has its den's.
- *Fetching them.* A page that showed an emoji by fetching its image would tell the den that it showed, and in a DM, which emoji a member was sent. So the member's local service keeps every image of the den's emoji in its cache, with the preview of each that moves: it fetches those the cache lacks as the list arrives in `ready`, and as an emoji is added or its image changes, and serves the page an emoji's image from the cache alone. One the cache let go of, as by its eviction or its being emptied, brings every one the cache lacks at once, so the den can't tell which showed. While emoji are off, no message shows one, and Manage's list fetches each as it shows. The cache records an emoji's files as the emoji's (`named`, beside a message's and a member's), so a deleted emoji takes them, live, and after a snapshot that no longer lists it; the cache's third format added that, and a cache of another format starts again from empty (see [The client's cache](sync.md#the-clients-cache)). The install's own den isn't cached, its files being on its disk. The owner's count bounds what this takes: at most 500 images of at most 512 KiB.
- *Unicode's emoji.* The picker lists them, and the den checks reactions against them, from one list. `scripts/emoji.sh` runs a generator, `scripts/emoji/`, over Emoji 18.0's `emoji-test.txt` and CLDR 49's English annotations of single emoji and of sequences, all three pinned in `scripts/vendor.sh` by SHA-256. It writes, for the page, `internal/ui/assets/emoji.json`: the groups, and each fully qualified emoji with its name, its group, CLDR's keywords beyond its name's words, and where it takes a skin tone its form in each of the five, the forms whose name gives one tone, so an emoji of two people gets the one where both have it; and for `denproto`, `emojilist.go`, every fully qualified emoji, skin tones and all, the forms of two different tones among them, which `denproto.IsEmoji` asks. Both are committed, one emoji a line, and CI writes them again and fails on a difference, as it does for RNNoise's module; a Go test checks the two lists agree. Emoji draw in the system's font, as the rest of the text does, so no image set ships; where nothing but emoji shows, in the picker, completion and a message of emoji alone, the system's color emoji font comes first, since some text fonts, as DejaVu Sans, draw the older emoji in black and white, which Chromium takes over a color font named after them. The list stops at the newest Emoji version, which each line of `emoji-test.txt` names, that Windows 11 and Noto Color Emoji both draw: 16.0, which Windows 11 has drawn since 24H2, while 17.0 reaches it only as an update rolls out; a phone too old for one shows a box. The page fetches its list, 138 KB, once its picker or a completion first needs it, from the hashed address the page's shell names (`data-emoji`), rather than carrying it in its script. Unicode's license is in the notices.
- *The picker.* A smiley in the composer's box, beside the guide's ?, drawn as the page's icons are, opens it above the box, and on the phone layout as a sheet from the screen's foot, as the page's menus are there, its search and its buttons staying at the sheet's top as the emoji scroll (see [The phone layout](phone.md)). At its top are a search, over names and keywords, every word starting a word of either, best first: the name exactly, the name's start, its words', then keywords; a button for each section, which scrolls to it; and the skin tone, ✋ in it, which the browser keeps, for those that take one. Below, a block of the member's most used emoji, up to four rows, then the den's, by name, then Unicode's, by group, nine to a row, the name of the one under the pointer or the focus at the foot. A pick goes in at the caret, or in place of what's selected, and the picker closes, but with Shift: a Unicode emoji as itself, in the member's skin tone where it takes one, so the text holds the emoji, and a den's as `:name:`, which the box, being plain text, shows by name. A mouse's picker starts in its search, where Enter picks the first found; a finger's doesn't, since its keyboard would cover the emoji, and the box takes the focus back after a pick. Escape or a press elsewhere closes it.
- *Most used.* The browser keeps a score for each emoji the member uses: picked in the picker, put in by completing, sent in a message, a phone keyboard's among them, or added as a reaction. Each use adds one, once: an emoji the picker or completion put in counts as it goes in, and the message it went with counts only the emoji it holds beyond those. A Unicode emoji counts without its skin tone, which the member's choice puts back, and one the list doesn't have counts as nothing; a den's counts by its ID, in its den alone, keeping its score through a rename, and its score goes once the den no longer has it. Every score halves in two weeks, so a favorite stays at the top while one used once fades; 300 are kept, the lowest dropping first. The picker's first block and the quick reactions both go by it, the quick reactions filled out with 👍 ❤️ 😂 😮 😢 🎉 while the member has used fewer.
- *Completing.* In a channel or a DM, typing `:` and two characters where an emoji may start, by the rule's first colon, lists above the box the den's emoji whose names match, then Unicode's, by the picker's search, `_` matching a space, eight at most. In a channel, typing `@` where a mention may start, by the mention rule, lists the members who can see it but the member, by a username starting with what follows, then by a word of the display name. In a channel or a DM, typing `#` and a character there lists the den's text channels the member sees, by the start of a name or of a word in it, in the list's order, and puts in the channel's link, which the box shows as its address until it's sent. Nothing completes in code or a link. The first is marked; ↑ and ↓ move through the list, Enter or Tab puts the marked one in, with a space after it, and Escape closes it until the text changes; a click or a tap puts one in, keeping the box's focus, so a finger's keyboard stays up.
- *Animated emoji* play as Animations says (see [Profiles](members.md#profiles)): always, or on hover over their message, and in the picker under the pointer; Manage's list plays each under the pointer, and a bio, in a profile, plays them while it shows. One that isn't playing shows its preview, the den's still of its first frame, which the image covers while it plays.

## Reactions

A reaction is a member's emoji on a message: one of Unicode's, from the list the picker shows (see [Emoji](#emoji)), or one of the den's. A message holds at most 20 different emoji. Reactions notify nobody, and each counts toward its member's other writes.

- *In a channel.* The den keeps a row for each, a member's emoji on a message, checked against Unicode's list (`denproto.IsEmoji`), or as one of the den's emoji by its ID while emoji are on, so a reaction carries no text: no bidi characters, no words spelled in letters. A message holds at most 10,000 reactions in all besides, so one with them stays well within a frame and a page of history. History pages carry a message's reactions, each emoji with the members who reacted with it, in the order each emoji came and each member reacted; live, `reaction.added` and `reaction.removed` each carry one member's one emoji, which a page applies to that emoji's set, so applying one twice changes nothing, where resending a message's whole set on every tap would spend the den's upload. The same reaction again, or one taken back that isn't there, changes nothing and tells nobody. The rows go with their message, deleted or past the den's retention, and with one of the den's emoji as staff delete it, whose `emoji.deleted` has the pages drop its chips. Reactions wait their turn with edits on the den, so an edit's message carries its reactions as they were, and the events go out in the order the changes were made.
- *In a DM.* The den can't read a DM, and reactions in the clear would tell it how one member answered the other, so each member's reactions on a DM's message are one set, which their client seals with the DM key, as it seals the DM's messages: bound to the den, the DM, the message, the member, the key's ID and the set's revision, so the den can't move it to another message, member or DM, or pass an old set off as the newest; and padded to one length, so its size doesn't say how many it holds. It names the den's emoji by name, as a DM's text writes them. The den keeps it in a row for each message and member, replaced whole against the revision it was made against, as an edit is, so a member's two devices don't drop each other's reactions: a set made against another revision is refused as a conflict that carries the set as it is, and the client tries again on it. It sends the set to both members as `dm.reactions`, and in history with its message. A client opening one keeps only Unicode's emoji from the list and names the den's emoji may have, each once, at most 20, and drops anything else; one it can't open shows none. The den sees that a member's reactions changed, and when, never what they are; a set stays once made, empty or not, so it doesn't say that either. Nothing more changes once the other member has left.
- *What changed while away.* A reaction records its message in the change log, as an edit does, though its revision stays, so an install that was away fetches it again with its reactions, and an edit under way meets no conflict (see [What changed while away](sync.md#what-changed-while-away)).
- *Staff* take one emoji's reactions off a channel's message, those of the members they outrank, as their other powers reach, from the message's menu, Remove reactions, which lists its emoji and how many of each they may take (`reaction.cleared`, naming whose went). A DM's are its members' alone. A ban that deletes a member's recent messages takes the reactions they added in that time too, in channels, each told as taken back; a DM's set stays, since the den can't tell one reaction in it from another.
- *On the page.* Under a message, after its files and covers, its reactions show as chips, with the theme's corners for selectors as badges have, each emoji with its count, marked where the member reacted; a click adds the member's or takes it back, and a tip says who: You first, then names, up to 15, then how many more, on hover or focus. A den's emoji shows as its image while emoji are on, and as `:name:` while they're off, as a message shows it; one that a DM's set holds by a name the den no longer has shows as that name. A + at the row's end, a smiley beside the message's ⋯, shown as the ⋯ is, and the ⋯ menu's Add reaction open the picker beside them, above where there's room and below otherwise, or as a sheet on the phone layout; a pick reacts, and an emoji added counts toward the member's most used (see [Emoji](#emoji)). On a touch screen the long press's sheet opens with a row of quick reactions at its top: the member's six most used, filled out with 👍 ❤️ 😂 😮 😢 🎉 while they've used fewer, the den's left out while emoji are off, each marked where they reacted, and a + for the picker. A tap on a chip adds or takes back, and a long press on it says who, which takes the place of the message's own long press. A message's first reaction gives it a row, and more may wrap it onto another; above what the member reads, neither moves the view (see [Message list](sync.md#message-list)). An older page shows no reactions; a den older than reactions, which came with its emoji, sends no `emoji_limits`, and the page offers none there.

## Polls

A channel's message may be a poll, whose members vote on its choices. Its text is the question, which a poll must have, so notifications, reply quotes and older pages read it.

- *Making one.* The composer's + gains Poll in a channel, which opens the poll's editor above the box, where a reply and files stand, the box holding the question, which the editor's first line and the box's placeholder say: 2 to 10 choices, each up to 80 characters of plain text, cleaned as display names are, though each of Unicode's emoji stays whole, its joiners and tags kept, and no two alike in any case; whether a member may pick more than one; how long it runs, 1 hour, 4 or 8 hours, 1 day by default, 3 days, or 1 or 2 weeks, which the den takes as any whole number of hours from 1 to 336; and its two rules, Totals show, then Anonymous voting, which asks who voted for what as anonymity: Yes, never shown; During the poll, shown once it ends; or No, shown with the totals. Cancel takes the poll away. Polls go in channels alone, since a DM's would have its votes sealed from the den that counts them, for a poll of two; and without files or other editors, since neither the message nor its question changes once it's posted. The den keeps a poll in a row beside its message, its choices sealed with the data key as the text is, and each vote, a choice a member picked, in a row of its own, the sixth migration (see [Data across versions](../design.md#data-across-versions)).
- *Who voted for what:* Never, the default, anonymous voting's Yes; Once it ends, During the poll; or Always, No. It's fixed as the poll is posted, and shown on it, since a voter decides with it in mind, and an author mustn't reveal votes cast under Never. The den holds to it: it sends who voted for what to nobody under Never, to everyone at the end under Once it ends, and with the totals under Always. Never keeps it from members, not from the den, which keeps who voted for what to count each member once and let them change their vote; the poll's tip says so.
- *When the totals show:* As votes come in, the default; or After voting. As votes come in shows everyone the totals as they change, and a member changes or takes back their vote until the poll ends. After voting shows a member the totals once they've voted, and makes their vote final: changing it after seeing them would let a member move to the winning side. The poll says so before they vote. The den holds to this too: until the poll ends it sends the totals only to those who voted, and to the rest only how many have. So a poll as the den sends it, in history, in what changed while away and in its events, is as its member may see it, their own votes among it.
- *Voting.* The den checks that the poll is open, the choices are its own, one of them unless several are allowed, and under After voting that the member hasn't voted; the same vote again changes nothing. A vote counts toward the member's other writes. The member's own sessions hear their vote at once, with the poll as they now see it; everyone else hears the totals in batches every second and a half, as presence is batched, so a busy poll sends an event a batch rather than one a vote, each batch to those who may see it: under After voting, an audience of the poll's voters among those who see its channel, beside the hub's audiences of one member and of two, and the rest of the channel's, who hear how many voted. A vote changes its message without changing its revision, as a reaction does: it records the message in the change log (see [What changed while away](sync.md#what-changed-while-away)), and waits its turn with edits and reactions, so its events go out in order with the batches and the end.
- *Ending.* The den closes a poll at its end, by a timer for the next to end, set as hosting starts and as polls are posted and ended, and sends everyone who sees the channel the final totals, with who voted for what where the poll shows them then; a poll past its end reads as ended before the timer's turn comes, and takes no vote. Its author ends it early, which moves its end to then. A poll can't be edited once posted, neither its question nor its choices, nor a task in its question ticked, so no vote stands under a question it wasn't cast for. It goes with its message, deleted or past the den's retention; a member's votes stay when they leave, as their messages do.
- *On the page.* Under its question, the poll shows each choice, with a bar of its share of those who voted once the member may see the totals, and their own marked; how many voted, and how long it has left, or that it ended; and its rules in a few words, with a tip of a sentence or two: Anonymous, Votes show when it ends or Votes show, and with After voting, Results after you vote, votes final. While the member may vote, each choice is a radio button, or a check box where several may be picked, whose row is its label, and Vote votes; once they've voted as votes come in, Change vote and Remove vote; and its author ends it early with End now, which asks once more. Where the poll shows them, a choice's voters show on hover or focus over its count, and by a tap on a touch screen, You first. The page applies the den's events and the answers to the member's own votes as they come, the newest last, an ended poll keeping its final totals, and holds the view where it is as a poll above it changes height (see [Message list](sync.md#message-list)).
- *Older ones.* An older page shows the question alone. A den older than polls is older than emoji, which came with them, and sends no `emoji_limits`, so the page offers Poll only where it has them.

## @everyone

- Staff, the moderators and the owner, send `@everyone` in a channel, where the mention rule finds it (see [Text and replies](#text-and-replies)). It counts as a mention of each member who can see the channel but the author, a row each as for a mention by name, so the unread mention counts, Home's and older pages' among them, need nothing new; a den of 500 members writes 500 rows for it, which go with the message. The den marks the message (`everyone`), so the page highlights the @everyone for every reader, as it highlights a mention of the reader, and the member's service tells it from a mention by name.
- It counts while the text holds it and staff put it there: an edit that keeps it keeps the mark, a co-editor who isn't staff takes it out of a shared message but can't put it in, and an edit recounts its rows, as for a name, mentioning neither the author nor the one who edits. Notifications come as a message arrives, so an edit that adds it notifies nobody, as for any mention.
- From anyone else it's a mention by name, of a member named `everyone`: the name is a valid username, so a den may have one from before, who keeps it, and new members can't take it, the den answering as for a name that's taken.
- Completing `@` offers it first to staff in a channel, and the site's guide says who sends it and how to silence it.
- *Silencing it.* The Notifications card has a switch under Mentions, @everyone, on by default, which applies while mentions are on: off, an @everyone notifies nothing and plays no sound, and turning it off takes back what it showed, though an @everyone that names the member too notifies as their mention. Muting the den or the channel silences it, as it silences mentions. Either way it counts toward the channel's mentions, since leaving it out would take a second count in read states. With the text hidden, its notification says it mentioned everyone.

## Compact links

When a message is sent or edited, or a channel's description or a member's bio is set, the den rewrites the links in it. Links to Reddit, YouTube and X, and Amazon's product pages, become one short form, which keeps only what identifies the post, video or product, and every other link loses its tracking parameters. In a private DM the sending service rewrites the text before sealing it, since the den can't read it. A rewritten link stays in the text as an ordinary URL, so everything that reads text reads it as before: the markdown subset, mentions, tasks, reply previews, edits and sealed DMs.

| Site | Kept | Written as |
| --- | --- | --- |
| YouTube | video ID, start time (`t`); a Short's ID | `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=…&t=…`, a Short as `https://www.youtube.com/shorts/…` |
| Reddit | post ID, optional comment ID | `https://www.reddit.com/comments/…`, shown on `old.reddit.com` for members who prefer it |
| X | status ID | `https://x.com/i/status/…` |
| Amazon | product ID (ASIN), on the same store | `https://www.amazon.…/dp/…` |

- Tracking parameters come off every link: each site's own (YouTube's `si`, X's `s` and `t`, Reddit's `share_id`, Amazon's `ref` and its search and referral parameters, Steam's `snr` on `steampowered.com` and `steamcommunity.com`), and a fixed list of those that track on any site, such as `utm_*`, `fbclid` and `gclid`. [The protocol](../protocol/messages.md#links) lists the forms each site's links take, and every parameter.
- Amazon's referral tag (`tag`) comes off with the rest, as it would with any link cleaner: it pays whoever shared the link, and tracks the click to them.
- A Reddit share link (`reddit.com/r/…/s/…`) stays as it is, apart from tracking parameters. Its code is Reddit's own record of who shared the link, and only Reddit can turn it into a post ID, which would mean Dens fetching from Reddit. Amazon's short links (`amzn.to/…`, `a.co/d/…`) stay as they are for the same reason.
- Hosts match exactly, as a URL parser reads them, so a lookalike link is never rewritten into a real one.
- The page shows links without knowing the rule, so the rule can take in more sites later without a protocol change. Links get shorter too, often by 20 to 80 bytes, but that's a side effect.
- No link previews but YouTube's players and Steam's cards (see [Link previews](embeds.md#link-previews)), whose sites' addresses are fixed. Fetching a preview of any link would reveal the den's or members' IPs to whoever runs the linked site, which any member can choose.

## Links to messages

Copy message link, in a message's menu, copies the den's address with the den's ID, the channel and the message in its fragment: `https://den.example.com/#m/<den ID>/41/90210`. A link opens by the den's ID, which the den proves with its identity key on every connection, so it still opens after the den moves to another address, and never in another den that takes the old one; the address is there for a browser, and to name a den the member hasn't joined. A browser never sends a fragment, so a link opened outside Dens tells the den nothing of which message. The den's ID is no secret: its invites carry it, and anyone at its address can ask for its key.

- In a message, a link in that form shows as a chip naming where it points, with the address on hover: the channel, as `#general`, or `@bob` for a DM, after the den's name when it's another den's, `#unknown` for a channel gone or out of sight, and the host of a den the member hasn't joined. Another den's names come from its state on this computer, fetched when a chip first needs them, and again once its channels or members change. Reply quotes and previews show the address, as they do any link.
- A click opens it once the local service has checked it, as a reply's quote opens its original: the den and the channel, where the page loads the history around the message and lights it for a moment, or, in the channel already open, the jump in place. When it can't, the chip says why, beside it, for a few seconds: the member hasn't joined that den; the channel is gone or they can't see it, which look alike, so a staff-only channel stays hidden; or the message is gone, deleted or past the den's retention. The check asks for the page `around` the message, one message long, which holds the message itself while it's there.
- A link into a den that's offline opens the channel from the cache, at the message if this computer kept it, and otherwise at the newest it kept, saying why.
- The chip's words are the member's own Dens's, never the sender's, and a click never sends the browser to the address, so a chip can't dress one link up as another. A click with Ctrl or Shift does as the browser would, opening the address, where the den answers with a line saying it's a den on Dens, whose links open in Dens.
- A channel's and a message's IDs are the den's to give, once each: deleting a message or a channel leaves its ID unused for good, and a restore moves the counters on (see [Backup, restore, move](auth.md#backup-restore-move)), so a link never comes to name another message.
- An event's Copy link writes the same form with `#e/` and the event's ID, `https://den.example.com/#e/<den ID>/12`, which shows as a chip naming the event and as its card under the message (see [Events](members.md#events)).
- A channel's Copy link, in its menu, writes it with `#c/` and the channel's ID, `https://den.example.com/#c/<den ID>/41`, as `#` completes it. A channel's name is free text and needn't be the only one, so a message holds the link, never `#name`, and the link outlasts a rename and works from a DM and from another den. It shows as a message's link's chip without its bubble, and opens the channel at its newest message, a DM's and a group's for their members alone. A voice channel's reads `#unknown`, so no link joins a call.

## Forwarding

A message's Forward sends its text and files to another place the member writes in, in its den or another this install is in, as the member's own, marked forwarded.

- *Where.* Forward opens the switcher's box over the places of every den connected now, all of them before anything's typed, the open den's first: text channels where the den's info says `forward`, and on a device holding the member's seal, DMs with members still in the den, groups, and the member's notes, made as they're picked. A pick sends; the message's row then says where it went, for a moment. On the phone layout it's a sheet.
- *What goes.* The member's Dens reads each file from its cache or the den and uploads it to the place as the member's own, stripped again, and sealed anew under a key of its own where the place is sealed; then it sends the text and files. A spoiler stays one, in notes too. A poll doesn't forward, nor what this device can't open, and a reply's quote, reactions and editors stay behind. A video the media module can't decode takes the preview its original had, which a page drew, or goes without.
- *Limits.* The place's limits hold before anything goes: a place that's no text channel or sealed place the member's Dens holds, a sealed one with no key to send under, as a DM that holds since its member's keys changed, a file larger than its den takes, sealed where the place is sealed, or files past the member's space or the den's, say so and send nothing. An upload made before a later failure goes at once.
- *The mark.* Forwarded shows over the message, with nothing of where it came from, to the den or in the seal: who wrote a DM's message, and where, isn't the forwarder's to tell. A channel's mark is the den's, `forwarded`, the twentieth migration (see [Data across versions](../design.md#data-across-versions)); a sealed place's is inside the seal, so the den isn't told, though a file's sealed size and its timing can match one it holds, and a client takes none the den sets on a sealed message. A forward carries no grant, so in a group it opens as its sender's.
- *After.* A forward is its sender's to delete, not to edit, tick or share: the den refuses a channel's, and the sender's Dens a sealed one's. Its sender takes a file off it from their files, which frees their space, and swaps none (see [Managing files](files.md#managing-files)). Mentions in it count where it goes, staff's @everyone among them, as in any message the member sends. An older Dens reads a sealed forward unmarked, and one of the sender's can edit it, dropping the mark; an older den drops a channel's mark, so Forward offers none of its channels.
