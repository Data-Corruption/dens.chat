# The page

Part of [Dens's design](../design.md): its parts, its words and its look, and custom themes.

## Client UI

The page is a Preact app (about 10 KB) built by the pinned esbuild into one hashed bundle. Preact injects no scripts or styles at runtime, so the CSP stays `script-src 'self' 'wasm-unsafe-eval'`, which takes WebAssembly for RNNoise, with no inline code. The stylesheet carries every DaisyUI theme, which a member picks in the settings and the browser keeps; until they pick one, the page follows the device's light or dark preference, with Light and Dark. A member makes themes of their own too, and sets a picture behind their chat (see [Custom themes](#custom-themes)). A member's name shows in the font and colors they gave it, the fonts the binary's own, each fetched as a name on screen uses it (see [Name styles](members.md#name-styles)). Every link the page draws, a message's, a cover's or its own, is set apart by its color, and underlined only under a pointer that hovers or with the keyboard's focus, by one rule for DaisyUI's `link` in the utilities' own cascade layer, which outranks DaisyUI's, nested within it; a touch screen's tap leaves no underline behind. The page has no header: a den's channel list holds the way home, at the left of the den's name, which shows the den's status only while it isn't connected, and the cog for the settings sits in the member's panel at its foot; home has a cog of its own, in Your dens' header beside Add a den, and a development instance's tab names it. The page keeps one WebSocket to the local service, and one that hasn't opened within a few seconds counts as down, so the page says it's out of touch rather than looking fine while it hears nothing. Views reload once it's back, and a reload applies again what changed while it was out, since the snapshot it fetched may be older. Every request names the version of Dens that served the page, and a service of another version, as one an update restarted, refuses it, so the page loads again from it rather than ask in a shape it may not know. WebSockets, both den and local, use `github.com/coder/websocket`: small, context-first and without dependencies, since the standard library has none.

## The page's parts

- The settings are a dialog over whatever the page shows, so a den and a call stay in view, in the one frame Manage shares: their sections listed down its left, which the phone layout lists as pages (see [The phone layout](phone.md)). On a den's page, or opened for a den, as by an alert's Change password, they list that den's sections of the member's own first, under its name: Devices, Password and recovery, Your code and Your files, with Leave den at the foot, but in the den this computer hosts; the app's follow under App. Appearance is a page that takes the window at any width (`appearance.jsx`), in the shape of DaisyUI's theme generator: the settings' list folds to ← back, which returns to the section open before, as Escape and Back do, and ✕ closes the settings; so on a wide window the settings open at Chat. At its left the themes: New theme and Paste a code, My themes, then the built-in ones, System, Light and Dark first. In the middle three tabs: Theme, the theme chosen in the list (see [Custom themes](#custom-themes)); Messages, with Density, the sizes of photos, videos and YouTube's players, Name styles, Animations (see [Profiles](members.md#profiles)), the Verified mark, Staff marks, but on a touch screen File names on hover (see [Serving](files.md#serving)), and Chat background; and Calls, with Call timer (see [The call timer](calls.md#the-call-timer)). At its right a small den in the theme chosen, which shows every other Appearance setting as it's chosen: the chat's background, Density, styled names, staff's marks, the verified mark, the call timer, the photo's and the player's sizes, at a sixth of theirs, and the photo's name on hover. Messages and Calls apply as they're changed; a theme waits for Save. From 80rem the three stand side by side; under that the themes and the editor share the left column, as tabs along its foot; and on the phone layout each of the three is a tab along the page's foot, Themes, Editor and Preview, the den then standing its channel list above its chat, as it does in any column narrower than 36rem. Each choice is a label over its row of joined buttons, or its slider for a size, its hint in a tip. Chat holds compressing photos and videos, links, YouTube's players and Steam's cards; Notifications, what notifies the member, with a line only where something keeps them from showing or the helper shows them, and the page's sounds, each on one line on a wide window (see [Notifications](notifications.md#notifications) and [Sounds](notifications.md#sounds)); Voice, the microphone and speaker, noise suppression, how the member sends their voice, their shortcuts and their screen share; Relay, the install's links with the member's other devices (see [Relay](helper.md#relay)); and System: Hosting, whose switch, Host a den on this computer, stands beside a line on what a den needs and the hosting guide's i, with the address Caddy forwards to and the media ports while hosting is on; in the Flatpak, On this computer, with Remember on this computer, starting at sign-in and Quit; Backup; the cache, update checks, the local password, the logs, signing this browser out, or in the Android app this page, and the version. `openSettings` keeps its section ids, so the links into them hold.
- Home is a launcher. Each den is a row: a dot for its state, named in words when it isn't connected, who the member is there and their role, a large Open, and a small Manage and Add device, which shows the den's sign-in code (see [Sign-in codes](auth.md#sign-in-codes)), under a header with Add a den and the settings' cog. With no den, Home is Dens's mark, a line, Add a den and, where this install can host one, Host one on this computer under it, in the middle of the window, as the first run's screens are: pairing, the local password, unlocking, link previews in a browser new to Dens (see [Link previews](embeds.md#link-previews)) and the recovery codes. An update's notice heads it, and sign-ins on this device that wait for another's approval show above the list. Above it all, in a call or while Relay plays a computer's sound, one box holds Relay's bar over the call, as the foot of a den's channel list holds them. Add a den has three ways in, an "or" between them: Scan, where the page finds a camera, which reads Add device's code from another of the member's devices; the den's address, with or without `https://`; and an invite, each field with its Continue to its right. It goes on a screen at a time, each titled with the den's name, from an invite or a den signed in to before, or until the den says it its address's host, the address small under it. Creating the den this install hosts is a card above the list until its owner account exists, once hosting is on. While hosting is off, the den's row says so and leads to the switch, and so does the end of Add a den where this install can host one.
- Manage is a dialog for each den, for what staff manage, which the phone layout lists as pages. The den's name at the top of its list opens the den's menu, an arrow beside it and a pointing hand over it, as a server's name does in other chat apps: Manage, which opens it, on the phone layout at the list of its sections, and for staff New channel and New group; Home's Manage opens it too, and the cog beside the member's name stays the app's settings, so what's the den's and what's this device's Dens stay apart. It holds General, with the den's address and Den ID for every member and the owner's edits; Invites, Bans, the Soundboard and Emoji, for staff, the owner's limits for each in its own section; and the owner's Events, who makes them, and Limits, for uploads, messages and calls, whose presets are a row of tiles. Their edits gather under one Save changes bar, and closing with changes unsaved asks first. A member's own sections of the den are the settings'. A den that isn't connected shows General alone.
- On a den's page, an update's notice shows above the channel, and on the phone layout above whichever pane is open, since a member with one den may never open Home, and so does a line for each sign-in waiting for the member's approval there whose dialog they closed, which opens it again. The notice is Home's, with its command or its button, and Dismiss, which the browser keeps for that notice of that running version (`DENS_UPDATE_DISMISSED`), so the next update shows again; Home shows it whatever was dismissed.
- On a den's page the member list starts open on screens wide enough to show it beside the channel, and channels' kinds, the soundboard, the lock on DMs and the shield on staff-only channels are drawn icons of one size, not emoji. The lock is end-to-end encryption's alone, a DM's, a group's or a call's, so a staff-only channel has a shield.
- Dates take one form, the medium one in the reader's locale, as Oct 12, 2026, with the time where it matters, as when an invite expires.
- Every menu draws its rows one way, each with an icon of the page's one style before its label: a dropdown under its button, a menu at the pointer for a right click, or on the phone layout a sheet. A row whose items open beside it, as Move to in a call, opens them to whichever side of the window has room, on a mouse's hover, a click, Enter or the right arrow, and the left arrow or Escape closes them; a sheet opens them in its own place, under a row back.
- The Den tab and the Direct messages tab each show a dot while something in the other waits, the Direct messages tab's red, as a mention's count is, and the row ends in a +: New group on Direct messages, and staff's New channel and New group on Den.
- The browser's tab shows Dens's mark with a red dot while a DM or a mention waits in any den, from the dens' statuses, whose mentions count every message in a DM. The page's shell carries both icons, and the page swaps them; the Android app has no tab.

## The page's words

The page explains itself where it can, so the docs needn't (see [What the site holds](../../README.md#what-the-site-holds)):

- Helper text is one line at a desktop's width, which the settings check measures. The label does the work, and details, such as 2,560 pixels or 30 ms of delay, go in a tooltip, or in a line to the guide where the guide already says it.
- It uses the words people know, such as compress, cache, resolution and shortcuts, and no implementation names: noise suppression, not RNNoise; the DM recovery key, the name other encrypted apps taught people, for the DM seal.
- It describes only what applies: the page knows its browser and system, and shows a figure, such as how much the cache holds, rather than a sentence about it.
- A warning comes with the action it's about, in its confirmation, rather than ahead of it in a paragraph.
- A change that reaches other members asks first: a DM's or a group's disappearing time in a dialog, staff's den settings through Manage's Save bar, retention's with a preview of what it deletes, and Delete channel and a call record's Reset by a confirmation in place.
- A step that waits on something elsewhere says so as it starts, with a spinner, as a sign-in waiting for another device does.
- What takes more than a line, such as where to compare codes and why, or what disappearing messages do, is the guide's: the page says what to do, minimally, and links to the guide, which opens in a new tab, rather than explaining in place.
- Nothing says the den or its owner can see something, which members take as given in a place someone else hosts; the page marks what's end-to-end encrypted, and the docs say who can read what, once.

## The page's look

Each kind of control has one look, made by one component, most in `components.jsx`, so a look means the same wherever it shows:

- Buttons come in four kinds, by `Button` or, for a link or a picker's label, `buttonClass`: primary, filled in the theme's color, the one action a view or a dialog leads with (`btn-primary`); secondary, filled neutral, any other button that stands alone (`btn-soft`); quiet, with no fill until hovered, a row's, a list's or a toolbar's actions and the ✕ (`btn-ghost`); and danger, red words among a row's actions or as the button that opens a confirmation (`btn-ghost text-error`), filled red only as the button that confirms, in a dialog or a step of one (`btn-error`). No button is outlined. The `settings` and `phone` checks read each button's kind by these classes.
- A dialog's actions, and a step's within a section, are a row at its foot (`Actions`): the primary first, at the left, then Cancel or Back; in a phone's sheet they share its width.
- One slider (`Slider`, the `slider` utility): a 4-pixel track filled in the primary color up to a 16-pixel thumb, in a box 32 pixels tall for a finger. A video's seek bar and the player's volume keep their own, drawn over the picture. A choice of a few in an order, as a size, is a slider with a stop for each, ticked under it, the one chosen named at its right (`Steps`).
- A choice of a few is one row of joined buttons (`Choices`, DaisyUI's join), each a radio drawn as a button whose words are its label, a word or two: secondary until chosen, then filled in the primary color, and 40 pixels tall for a finger. Voice's way of sending, the screen share's Optimize for, Relay's Buffer, a name's Color and an event's Where are such rows, as Appearance's are.
- A label is regular weight, in the text's color, above its control (`Field`, `Choices`); a toggle is one size, its label beside it (`Toggle`); a hint is a line under, in the muted text color.
- Status reads in the text's color, which holds its contrast on every theme: its state is an icon and an edge in the state's color, a box of words (`Notice`, `ErrorText`) or a line beside what it's about (`StatusText`). Green, amber, red and cyan text are left for marks too small to be read as words.
- The settings and Manage are one frame (`SectionsFrame` in `pages.jsx`): on a wide window a card over the page, its sections listed down its left under each group's heading, a danger one at the column's foot; on the phone layout a list of rows, each section a page headed by its name. A section's groups are cards, which a phone shows plain.
- A file is picked through the page's own button, its name beside it (`FilePick`), and a date and a time in fields of the page's own, in `datefield.jsx` (`DateField`, a month's grid that opens under the field, and `TimeField`, a select of quarter hours), the same in every browser.
- A message's time shows without a leading zero, as 6:09 PM, and a spoiler's cover is the neutral surface's color, `base-300`, hiding as well on a dark theme and a light one.
- Every menu has one surface (`MENU` in `menus.jsx`): `base-200`, a step off the page's color, with a hairline edge and a shadow, so it reads as a layer over a message or a list on every theme.

## Custom themes

A member makes themes of their own in Appearance, the page of the settings that lists every theme, shares one as a code, and sets a picture behind their chat.

- *A theme* is a name, up to 40 characters; Light or Dark; and DaisyUI's variables, each in a field of its own (`themes.js`). It always holds ten colors, the background, the text, primary, secondary, accent, neutral and the four statuses, each `#rrggbb` or `oklch(L% C H)`, and the three radii, for selectors, fields and boxes, in rem from 0 to 2. It holds the rest where a pasted block or the theme it started from gave them: the two deeper backgrounds, each color's text, the two sizes, the border, depth and noise, each a number within the range DaisyUI's theme generator gives. What it leaves out derives: the deeper backgrounds each a step of OKLCH lightness darker, or lighter under a background too dark to show a darker one, as DaisyUI's Black goes; each color's text near white or near black, whichever contrasts more; and the rest is Light's or Dark's, which it shows over. A theme v1.13.0 kept names its corners, Square, Rounded or Round, in place of the radii, which those give it, so it looks as it did.
- *The page wears it* as DaisyUI's variables, set on the document's root through the CSSOM, which `style-src 'self'` allows as it allows every style the page sets, over `data-theme` Light or Dark for what a theme leaves to DaisyUI and the browser's own controls. A share's window takes the variables as it takes the theme's name, and the Android app's bars follow the background as they follow any theme's.
- *Choosing and wearing*. A theme chosen in Appearance's list shows in the editor and the den beside it alone, and Use wears it; the list ticks the one worn, which the editor says In use. A built-in theme shows its name, its colors and Customize, which copies it, every variable as its box computes them, into My themes, under its own name; System shows Light's or Dark's, as the device is set. New theme, Paste a code and Customize each add a theme to My themes and show it, unworn.
- *The editor* is the middle column's Theme, beside the den, which is the page's own components on a box that wears the theme alone, its variables for one of the member's own and `data-theme` for a built-in one, so the page keeps its theme until Use: the channel list with an unread channel, a mention and the page's call with its controls, a channel's header, the chat, the composer, a button of each kind, a notice of each status, a toggle and a slider; nothing in it acts, and styled names in it read against its colors. New theme starts at the theme the page shows, each variable read from the computed style, a color written otherwise read through a canvas. The controls are those the page draws with, in the generator's shapes: the name in a row with Use; the colors as tiles in the generator's rows, the background beside the two deeper ones and the text, an A on it, then each tint beside its text, an A on it, which derives and isn't set; a tile chosen takes its color below the tiles, the browser's `<input type="color">` with the color as text beside it, which takes hex or `oklch()` typed or pasted; the corners of cards, buttons and toggles, each five tiles that draw a box's corner, the one chosen lighter with its edge in the primary color, and a sixth for a value a block gave; the border's width on a slider with its ticks; and depth, noise and Dark as toggles; then Copy code and Remove. The text and primary, whose words the page draws on the background, are marked on their tiles where they read poorly there, under 4.5 to 1 for the text and 3 to 1 for primary, and say so under their field; a status is an icon and an edge, so it says nothing. Changing the background lets the deeper two derive again, and changing a color its text. Edits wait in a bar at the column's foot, Save and Discard, each theme's apart until the page closes, and the list dots a theme with edits unsaved; leaving the page with any asks first, as Manage does.
- *The browser keeps* up to 20, as it keeps the chosen theme: the themes in `DENS_THEMES`, and the choice as `custom:<id>` in `DENS_THEME`, which a page from before v1.11.0 reads as no choice. What the browser kept is checked as it's read, as a code is, and a theme with a value the page wouldn't have written is left out. Remove on the one worn follows the system again.
- *A code* is a block in the shape DaisyUI's theme generator copies: `@plugin "daisyui/theme" {`, the name as a CSS string, `default` and `prefersdark` false, `color-scheme` in quotes, then every variable, held or derived, in the generator's order. Copy code gives the theme in the editor's, edits and all; Paste a code adds a theme and shows it, from a Dens block, the generator's, its declarations without the braces, or a v1.13.0 `dens-theme:1` code, which reads as it did. A block is a member's message, so it's hostile: the page reads the declarations inside its first braces, split at semicolons outside quoted strings, comments left out, and for each variable it knows reads a color as hex or `oklch()`, a number in its unit and range, and writes the value out again itself; anything else, a key it doesn't know or a value it can't read, is left out, and a block with nothing it reads is refused. A color the block leaves out of the ten is Light's or Dark's, by its `color-scheme`, or by its background where it names none; a name that isn't plain text, or is missing, is "Pasted theme". So a style only ever gets a value the page wrote out itself, and the name shows as text. A block shared in a message keeps its text, though its `@plugin` shows as a mention.
- *The chat's background* is a still PNG, JPEG or WebP of the member's, at most 10 MB, which the service strips with `internal/media`, as a file bound for a den is stripped, and keeps in the client's `background` table: one row, sealed with the data key, with when it was set, which names its version. The dim, how far the theme's background covers it, 0 to 90% and 70 until set, is in the configuration (`BackgroundDim`), and outlasts the picture; so does the hover, how far a message under the pointer, or with its menu open, covers it, 0 to 100% in steps of 5 and 100 until set (`BackgroundHover`). The card's Hover sits under Dim. The client router alone serves it, at `/api/background`, as the image it is, sandboxed and never cached, takes it by `POST` and drops it by `DELETE`, writes passing the `Origin` check. The dens view names its version, dim and hover (`background`), so every page paired with the install draws the same, and follows a change as it's made. The page draws it as the message list's own CSS background, the picture filling it under a layer of the theme's background at the dim, so it stays put as messages scroll, and shows in every channel, DM and group and nowhere else; the list's style carries the hover, which a message's row takes for its background under the pointer (`bg-row`). A backup holds it; a den's export doesn't. The table is the fifteenth migration (see [Data across versions](../design.md#data-across-versions)).
- *What it leaves out:* a picture behind every surface, which would take every pane, sheet and menu translucent; a color picker of the page's own, where the browser has one; and themes a den sets for its members. Nothing of a theme reaches another member but its code, which they choose to paste.
