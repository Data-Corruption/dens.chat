# Sync

Part of [Dens's design](../design.md): retention, disappearing messages, presence, the message list, the client's cache and what changed while away.

## Retention

An owner can have the den forget old conversations: with retention on, every message older than a period they set is deleted, with its files. A new den has it off.

- The period is a number of days, from 1 to 3,650, set in the den's settings beside the upload limits. Before a change takes effect, the settings say how many messages it deletes at once, and how much space their files take.
- It covers channels and DMs alike. The den can't read a DM, but it knows when each message was sent, which is all retention needs.
- A message's age counts from when it was sent. Edits don't extend it, and neither do replies: a reply to a message that has passed the period says the original is gone, as with any deleted message.
- The den deletes messages within the hour after they pass the period, the way it deletes anything: rows removed with `secure_delete` on, and files taken off the disk. It deletes the oldest first, in the order they were sent, a few hundred per transaction, so a first pass through years of history never holds the database for long. A change of period starts a pass at once, and so does starting the den, so one restored from a backup deletes what passed the period meanwhile.
- Clients drop what passed the period too. After each pass the den sends one event, not a delete for every message, naming the last message it deleted: every message up to it, in every channel, is gone. Each client drops the messages it holds up to that one, and the files on them, from memory and from its cache. `ready` says how far retention has reached, so a client that was away drops them as it comes back. A channel whose last message went holds none, so nothing in it is unread.
- Members see the period in Manage's General, and a channel's history starts with a line saying how long messages are kept, where it would otherwise say the channel starts.
- Profiles, channel descriptions and uploads waiting to be sent aren't messages, and retention leaves them alone. A backup keeps what the den held when it was made, and outlives the period until it's deleted; the docs tell owners so.
- A DM's message that disappears goes at its own time (see [Disappearing messages](#disappearing-messages)), and retention takes it first where the period passes sooner.

## Disappearing messages

Either member of a DM sets its messages to disappear 1 hour, 1 day, 1 week or 4 weeks after they're sent, or Off, which a new one starts at. What members ask of disappearing messages is that they're gone, from the den and every device, so that's what Dens does: they stay sealed under the DM's key, as every other message there is.

- *The setting.* Beside the DM, under its other member's profile: Disappearing messages, a list of the five, which applies to the messages sent after it. A pick asks first, since it applies to the other member too, in a dialog that says what the new time does and links to the guide. A line in the conversation, at the time it was set, says who last set it, on which day, and to what: "On Nov 5, 2026, Bob set messages to disappear 1 day after they're sent", or turned them off; the day stays said once the messages around it are gone. The header shows the time in a few letters beside the lock, 1d, which opens what's beside it. A DM whose other member left takes none, as it takes no message.
- *The den knows it,* as it must to delete by it: it keeps the time in the clear, with who set it and when, and a time counts from sending, which it knows and both members see alike. A development instance's den takes any time of seconds up to four weeks, for the browser checks.
- *Each message* carries when it goes (`expires_at`), its channel's time as it was sent added to its sending, which an edit doesn't extend. Its row shows a timer beside its time, whose tip says when, and on a touch screen its sheet says it. A run of messages, which shows its timer in its header, ends where the next message disappears after another time or not at all, and where a line sits between two messages, as the one where the time changed, so every message that disappears shows its timer.
- *Deleting.* The den deletes each as its time comes, as its author's delete would: its row, with its files and reactions, `secure_delete` overwriting them, `message.deleted` to its members, and the change log, so an install that was away drops it as it comes back. It runs on the den's timer, the one that ends polls, set for whatever comes due next, sooner as a message under a time is sent, and again as the den starts, so one restored from a backup deletes what passed meanwhile; a pass deletes 500 a transaction. A reply to one says it's gone, as for a deleted one.
- *Unread.* A message counts toward its DM's unread count as any does. As one goes, each member it counted for hears their read state, as for any delete (see [Text and replies](messages.md#text-and-replies)): their count without it, and where what went was all that lay past their read position, the position past it, so a DM whose last messages went unread isn't left unread, while one still holding a message unread from before a shorter time was set stays so.
- *Each member's Dens* drops what it holds at its time, by the computer's clock, whether the den can be reached or not: the cache lets it go with its files and the replies' quotes of it, and keeps out a copy past its time that a page or an event brings; its pages drop it and its notification is taken back; history leaves it out, and one that arrives past its time notifies nobody. A page drops it as its time passes by its own timer too, so a den offline shows none of it.
- *What it doesn't do.* A den changed to keep copies would keep their sealed keys too, which anyone who later held a member's DM recovery key, or a device of theirs, could open. Forward secrecy for them would take keys each device makes for itself, changed every day, a second way of sealing keys beside identities, which [loose-ideas.md](../loose-ideas.md) keeps; the [privacy model](../design.md#privacy-model) already leaves compromised devices and targeted investigations outside what Dens protects against. Anyone in the conversation can copy a message before it goes, which the setting says.
- *Older ones.* The den's info says it deletes them (`disappearing`), and the page offers the setting only there. An older Dens in a DM with a time shows its messages without saying they go, and drops them as the den deletes them, by `message.deleted` while connected and the change log after, but not while it can't reach the den.

## Presence at 500 online

- One WebSocket per member; presence changes are coalesced and broadcast in batches about every 1.5 seconds.
- Typing indicators are throttled per channel and only sent to members viewing that channel.

## Message list

- A channel view holds one contiguous run of messages, never the whole channel: at most 200, and none that lie more than eight screens beyond what's shown. Scrolling near either end loads the next page (`before` or `after` the edge message) and cuts the far end, so memory stays flat however far back a member scrolls. A page is 50 messages, or as many as fill about three screens when the rows near that end are tall, but at least 10. The cut goes by the rows as they're laid out, so a run of photos, each a few hundred pixels tall, holds a few dozen, where one of short messages holds 200. Rows near the view are never cut, so what the member reads stays put.
- The view is attached to the live tail while it holds the newest message, and new messages append. Jumping to an old message (a reply's quote, a link to a message, a mention, later search) loads the page `around` it and detaches the view. New messages then only update a "new messages, jump to present" bar, and scrolling forward to the newest page reattaches it. A member reading further up a run that's full detaches the same way when the next message arrives, rather than the run growing under them.
- A channel opened with unread messages shows a New divider where they start, which stays there while it's open. One opened with nothing unread shows none, so messages that arrive while it's open get none.
- A load that replaces the run (opening a channel, a jump, coming back after a drop) applies again what changed while it was out, since the service may have taken its page first, so a message that arrives as a channel opens isn't lost.
- With the window bounded, every loaded message is in the DOM. There is no per-row virtualization: it would stop a playing video whose row scrolled out of range, and keep the browser's find from seeing what's loaded.
- The layout doesn't jump. Images carry their dimensions from upload, so placeholders take their final size, and loading older pages keeps the view anchored on the message being read. At the newest message, the view stays there while its box resizes or its text rewraps: a growing composer, a rotated phone. A message's reactions change its height without a page: its first gives it a row of chips, and more may wrap it onto another, so as a reaction lands the list holds the message being read where it is, unless the view is at the newest, which it stays at; as does a poll's change, as its bars and voters come.
- The read position moves once the newest message has been on screen, at most every two seconds per channel, and at once when the member leaves the channel. A busy channel costs the den one small write every few seconds, not one per message.
- Message IDs increase with time within a den and are never reused, so `before`, `after` and `around` are single index lookups.

## Sync and bandwidth

Dens usually run on home connections, where upload bandwidth is scarce and every message goes out once per online member. The protocol keeps that fan-out small:

- **Resume, don't refetch.** Every change a member can see is an event with a sequence number. A client that reconnects sends the last one it saw and gets only what it missed, from an in-memory ring of recent events: the last 50,000 within 10 minutes, and within 64 MiB, so a member sending large messages fast can't grow the den's memory without bound. After a den restart, or a gap longer than the ring, the den sends a fresh snapshot instead (a resync), and the client refetches only what's on screen.
- **Snapshots without a scan.** `ready`'s newest message in each channel comes from a query per channel the member sees, which the `(channel_id, id)` index answers at once. A scan of every message took 38 ms at a million, for each of the up to 500 snapshots a den restart or a channel turning staff-only sends at once.
- **Orderly restarts.** A stopping den closes its listener first, then tells every client it is restarting (WebSocket close 1012). Otherwise a quick client reconnects to the dying process, as a spike showed. Clients wait a random delay of up to a few seconds, so 500 of them don't reconnect at once.
- **Scoped ephemeral traffic.** Presence is coalesced into batches about every 1.5 seconds, and typing goes only to members viewing that channel.
- **Thumbnails, not originals.** Uploads get a small preview when they arrive. The message list shows previews, and originals load only when opened, or where the member chose to show photos larger than a preview. The local service keeps both, encrypted on disk (see [The client's cache](#the-clients-cache)), and serves them to the browser with `Cache-Control: no-store`, so they never sit unencrypted in the browser's cache.
- **Compression, measured.** Compressing each message separately (permessage-deflate) saves only 22% on chat events. Context takeover saves 65%, but costs about 820 KB of den memory per connection, around 400 MB at 500 online. A static dictionary shared by client and den, with deflate per frame, reaches 43% of raw with no per-connection memory, and the den compresses each event once for every recipient. Batching events under load brings it to 29%. The den socket uses permessage-deflate without context takeover, for frames of more than 512 bytes: `ready`, replays and busy batches shrink as history pages do, to under a third, while a single chat event, too small to gain much, goes as it is. It holds no memory per connection: the WebSocket library takes a compressor from a pool for each compressed write. History pages over HTTP are gzipped, to 29% of raw. A dictionary for small frames waits until measurements show live events matter.
- **The client's cache and the changes since.** A client keeps what it fetched from other dens, encrypted on disk, and after a gap asks the den only what changed, so a client restarted after a day away downloads only that (see [The client's cache](#the-clients-cache) and [What changed while away](#what-changed-while-away)).

## The client's cache

A member's Dens keeps what it fetches from other dens: the files and previews the page showed, and the messages it read and those that arrived while it was connected. A photo seen once never downloads again, a channel read before opens without asking the den, and a restart of the client or the den costs only what changed meanwhile.

- The cache is a SQLite database and a directory of sealed files of its own, the data directory's `cache`, apart from the install's database. It holds only copies of what dens hold, so backups leave it out, a new format of it starts again from empty rather than migrating, and its writes never wait behind the den's on an owner's install.
- Everything in it is sealed with the data key: each message, and each 64 KiB chunk of a file, bound to its den, its ID and its place, so nothing moves between entries unnoticed. A chunk that doesn't open takes its file out of the cache, and the page gets the file from the den. A cache sealed under another key, or of another format, is emptied, not read.
- The install's own den isn't cached: the client reaches it over loopback, and its files are on the same disk, so a cache would only double the owner's storage. The client keeps the small memory cache it always had for it.
- It holds 2 GiB unless the member chooses another size, from 512 MiB to 50 GiB, in the settings' System section, whose Cache card also shows how much it holds and empties it. Past its size, what was used longest ago goes first: a whole file, or a channel's messages, by when the member last read the channel. A file larger than a quarter of the size isn't kept, and a channel keeps its newest 20,000 messages at most, so a busy one followed for years can't take the whole cache.
- A file is kept as it's read, a chunk at a time, so a video keeps the parts that played, and seeking fetches only the chunks it lacks. The chunks are the 64 KiB the den seals its uploads in, so a fetch decrypts exactly the den's own chunks. They're written in the order they arrive, each with its index, rather than at their place in a sparse file, which Windows would allocate in full. A file being read stays until its last reader closes it, since Windows can't delete an open file.
- What the page asks for comes from the cache first and the den second, and what the den sends goes into the cache on its way to the page. Files reach the page with the headers they always had, so the browser still caches none of them.
- Each cached file records what it's on: a message and its channel, a member's picture, or one of the den's emoji, which the client notes from what it passes the page and from the den's state. A deleted message, a file taken off one, a deleted channel or one the member no longer sees, a retention pass, a replaced picture and a deleted emoji each take out exactly their files. A file the client can't place, it doesn't keep, since it couldn't tell when the file goes. The page asks for a message's file under that message and its channel, and a file kept under them answers only such a request, and one kept under none, a picture or an emoji, only a request naming none, so a DM's file the client opened under its message is never read, or filled, as the den's bytes fetched another way (see [Serving](files.md#serving)).
- A den's cache goes when the member leaves it, is removed or banned, or the device is signed out, and when the member forgets the den.
- Messages: the cache holds them as the den sends them, a DM's still sealed, and opens them as the page reads them, with the keys the client already holds. Beside them it keeps runs, the ranges of IDs in a channel for which it holds every message the den has. IDs increase with time, so an old range can lose messages but never gain one.
- A message's reactions are kept with it, and change without its revision: each reaction's event changes the copy the cache holds, applied to its emoji's set, and a DM's set replaces the member's where its revision is newer. So does its poll, as the member may see it, which each poll's event changes: the totals, and who voted for what with them, where the event carries them, the member's own votes where it's to their sessions, and the final totals at its end, after which no update changes it. What the den sends of a message, a page of history or what changed while away, replaces the copy at its own revision too, with the reactions' and polls' events that came while it was asked for applied again, in order, since the den may have read it before them; an edit's event replaces it at a newer revision alone. A DM's sets each keep the newer of the two copies', by their revision.
- A page of history comes from a run that covers it, and otherwise from the den, which then joins the cache, run and all. While connected, every new message goes into the cache, in every channel the member sees, so a channel that was busy while they were online opens without a request.
- A run that reaches its channel's newest message says so for one connection, the one it reached it in: while that lasts, every new message arrives as an event and joins it, so the newest page comes from the cache. A snapshot starts a new connection, and the runs that still reach their channel's newest message, as its read states say it, carry on in it. A channel that had new messages while the client was away asks the den for those after the newest it holds, a page or two, rather than its whole newest page; past two pages, it takes the newest page, and leaves a gap the member fills by scrolling.
- The cache follows the page's rules for a page fetched while events arrive: the higher revision wins, a message deleted meanwhile never comes back, and nothing retention reached goes in. A reply's quote follows its original as it changes or goes.
- The client keeps the den's state with it: its members, channels and groups, read states and DM keys, and nothing of an older den's group DMs, whose messages leave the cache too (see [Messages and photos](e2ee.md#messages-and-photos)); a kept state an older Dens wrote reads with what it held of group DMs left out. It names the version its list of members is current as of on the socket's upgrade, and `ready` then sends only the members changed since, which with 1,000 members is most of its size.
- Offline reading: a den the client can't reach shows as it last was, and what the cache holds of each channel reads as usual. A note over the messages says the den is offline and these are cached messages; it shows once the den has failed to connect, not while it connects, as at every start. A page the cache doesn't hold says the den is offline at once, rather than waiting out the den. Sending, calls and anything else that needs the den wait for it to come back.
- After a snapshot, the changes since reach the page too, as edits and deletes would: it may have loaded a channel from the cache before they came.
- A message that disappears keeps when it goes beside it, which the cache's fourth format added, so every cache started again from empty as the update landed. The cache lets it go then, by the computer's clock, with its files and the replies' quotes of it, whether the den can be reached or not, on a timer of its own set for the next to go, and keeps out a copy past its time; the client hears which went, and takes them off its pages and out of the notifications (see [Disappearing messages](#disappearing-messages)).

## What changed while away

A client away from a den longer than its ring of events, or across the den's restart, gets a fresh `ready`, and needs to know what changed in what it cached. The den doesn't keep its events for that: they carry every message's text, so a store of them would be a second copy that every delete and retention pass would have to find, and replaying it would send a day of every channel, read or not. It keeps a change log of IDs instead.

- The log records each message edited or deleted, with its channel, those that disappeared among them, each member whose record changed, each message whose reactions changed, and each poll whose votes changed or that ended, its revision as it was: one row for each, its latest change, numbered in the order the changes happened. The newest number is the den's version. A retention pass and a deleted channel add nothing, since `ready` says how far retention reached and a deleted channel is missing from it.
- `ready` carries the den's sync epoch, its version, the floor (the oldest version a client may still ask from) and the last message retention deleted. A client that synced in the same epoch at a version no older than the floor asks for what changed since: the messages edited or deleted since then, up to the newest it holds, in channels it can see now. That is usually a few hundred bytes, in one request.
- The den filters by what the member can see now, not when the change happened, so a role change, or a channel that became staff-only and back, needs nothing special: two visibility levels and DMs make that one condition. A channel missing from `ready` takes its cache with it.
- New messages aren't in it. A channel the member opens fetches its newest page, and pages back from there, as it always did, from the cache where it can, so a busy channel nobody opens costs nothing.
- The log keeps 30 days of changes. A client whose version is older than the floor, or from another epoch, drops what it cached for the den and starts again, keeping the den's state there again at once: it holds the kinds of the den's channels, which the client keeps for good, and which stay in the cache alone where the rest of a kept state doesn't check at a start. A client connected all along asks for the changes once a day, which keeps its version within the 30 days, and applies them as it applies events: twice changes nothing.
- A den restored from a backup starts a new epoch. The backup carries its database's counter of message IDs, so the restored den can give a new message an ID that a client holds for another message, one the backup never had; a client of another epoch drops its cache for the den instead.
- Live events still keep a connected client's cache current, and the ring still resumes a short gap without a `ready`. The change log covers what the ring can't.
