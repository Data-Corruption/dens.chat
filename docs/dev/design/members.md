# Members

Part of [Dens's design](../design.md): roles, handing the den on, leaving and bans, staff's tools, the den's structure, profiles, names and their styles, and events with their badges.

Three fixed rank roles, optional custom roles for channel visibility, one level of channel groups, text and voice channels, and DMs.

## Roles

| Role | Can |
| --- | --- |
| Member | Read and post in visible channels, DM other members, join voice, upload within limits |
| Moderator | Everything a member can, plus delete members' messages, remove and ban members, create invites, manage custom roles, channels and groups, see every channel, read the audit log |
| Owner | Everything a moderator can, plus manage moderators, den settings and limits |

Staff act only on those of a lower rank, so moderators can't remove, ban or delete the messages of other moderators or the owner. Moving a member from one call to another is the exception: staff move anyone but the owner and themselves (see [Staff in calls](calls.md#staff-in-calls)). Their powers stop at DMs, whose text they can't see: in a DM, only a message's author deletes it.

### Custom roles

Custom roles control channel visibility alone — they carry no moderation powers, colors or hierarchy.

- *Limits.* A den has at most 50 custom roles (`den_roles`, `den_member_roles`, `den_channel_roles` and `den_event_roles`, the twenty-third migration; see [Data across versions](../design.md#data-across-versions)). Names are 1 to 32 characters, cleaned like display names, and unique case-insensitively. Staff (moderators and the owner) create, rename and delete custom roles in Manage's Roles section, and assign them to active members from Manage's Members section, a profile card's ⋯, or the member list's right-click menu.
- *Visibility modes.* A text or voice channel is visible to **Everyone** (`staff_only` false, no roles), **Staff only** (`staff_only` true, no roles), or **Selected roles** (`staff_only` false, one or more roles: visible to staff and any member holding at least one listed role). Staff always see every channel regardless of their custom roles. Role-restricted channels carry a tag mark (distinct from staff-only's shield and E2EE's lock) whose tip lists the allowed roles.
- *Uniform enforcement.* Every channel operation applies the same visibility rule: snapshots, history, sends, edits, deletes, task ticks, reactions, polls (including voter/non-voter audiences), files, change-log sync, Mark all as read, `@everyone` and mention counts, co-editors, typing and focus, voice joins, moves, and events in voice channels. On the page, `@` mention completion, the co-editor picker, and staff's voice drag-and-drop / Move to filter candidates by the channel's visibility.
- *Voice kicks.* Losing a role, deleting a role, being demoted from moderator, or narrowing a voice channel's roles disconnects affected non-staff callers immediately (`forbidden`), including held calls.
- *Fail-closed deletion.* Deleting a role never opens a restricted channel to everyone: in the same database transaction, any channel whose only role was the deleted one flips to `staff_only = 1` (and the confirmation dialog lists those channels beforehand). Channels with other roles keep them. Ended events whose voice channel was deleted snapshot its roles into `den_event_roles`; if their sole role is deleted, those ended events flip to `staff_only = 1` too.
- *Non-staff privacy.* Non-staff snapshots and profiles carry only roles referenced by channels the viewer can see, with each member's `role_ids` filtered to those roles. Hidden role names and memberships never reach non-staff over the socket or profile route.
- *Targeted refreshes.* The event hub maps each custom role to a bit slot (`uint64`) so role audiences evaluate in O(1) without heap allocations (`internal/den/hub_bench_test.go`: 2.6 ns/check at 500 online and 50 roles), and restricted channel/member visibility changes send a fresh `ready` only to affected non-staff rather than refreshing everyone (`3.7 µs` vs. `13.3 µs` full non-staff refresh). `SetMemberRoles` and `DeleteRole` also mark offline holders in `memberRefresh`/`roleRefresh` so stale sessions cannot resume past lost access, and retired bit slots stay quarantined until every event carrying them leaves the ring.
- *Audit log.* Creating, renaming and deleting a role, changing a member's custom roles, and changing a channel's roles all write audit lines; `role.deleted` records any channels flipped to staff-only.
- *Older dens.* A den announces custom roles with `roles: true` in its info. Against an older den, Manage hides Roles and channel settings keep the Staff only toggle.

## Handing the den on

The den's machine has the last word on who owns it. Its desktop user, with the local password, makes a one-time owner invite with `dens den owner-invite`, which lasts an hour. Whoever redeems it joins as the owner, and the owner before them becomes a moderator. It hands on the owner's role, not the den: the den stays on the machine it runs on, which keeps the last word, since it can make another owner invite at any time. Moving the den to someone else's machine is its export, restored there, after which this machine removes its copy (see [Backup, restore, move](auth.md#backup-restore-move)). An owner invite is also the way back for an owner locked out of their account, as by signing out their own device with no recovery codes left: they join again under a new username. `dens den owner-invite` refuses while hosting is off, when nobody could redeem the invite.

A den can also start on a server, from the command line. `dens den create NAME ADDRESS`, with hosting serving and the local password, creates it with no account on that computer, and prints a one-time owner invite, which whoever will own it redeems from their own Dens, as any owner invite; `dens den owner-invite` makes another once it lapses. Until it's redeemed the den has no owner, and the server's page, if anyone opens it, offers to finish setting it up. On a computer with a desktop, Create your den on the page does the same and joins as the owner there.

## Leaving, removal and bans

- Removing a member (a kick) signs them out everywhere at once and takes them off the member list. Their record stays, so their messages keep a name, and nobody else can take their username.
- A removed member can come back only with a new invite, since invites are the only way in. They come back as themselves, history included, by giving their den password with their old username.
- A ban does the same and keeps that username out, even with a valid invite. It can also delete their recent messages, in DMs too, since the den deletes a DM's messages without reading them and that's the only way to clear DM spam, and revoke the invite they joined with. The UI offers removal and banning as one action with a ban option.
- Lifting a ban takes staff of the rank of whoever placed it, or higher: a ban the owner placed, only the owner lifts.
- A ban can't stop someone from joining under a new name with a new invite: the den keeps no IP addresses, and each join uses a fresh key. Invites are the real door, which is why the removal dialog offers to revoke the one they used.
- Leaving on your own works like a removal, without the kick.
- Leaving needs the den to answer. A den this computer can't reach, as one whose server is gone for good, comes off it from its Leave den, in the settings on its page, without the den hearing of it, as one that signed this device out or let its member go does, whoever the member is there: this computer forgets its keys there and what it cached, and the account stays in the den, so if the den comes back, the member signs in again as from a new device. A den that answers is left with Leave, and the den this computer hosts goes only by moving it. Home's row doesn't offer it, since a den on a desktop that sleeps is offline as a matter of course, and nothing drops a den by itself after a while, which would need a record of when it last answered.

## Staff's tools

- *Members,* in Manage for staff, above Invites: everyone in the den in a table, their names, role, joined and last seen, sorted by a click on a column's head, and narrowed by a box that matches the start of a username or a display name, or of a word in it, as completion matches a member. A row's ⋯ holds what the card's ⋯ offers staff, Compare codes staying on the card; its Remove opens over Manage, and Escape there closes the removal alone. It reads the view's members. On the phone layout a row is the names over the rest, and a list picks the order.
- *Last seen* is when any of a member's devices last signed in or renewed its session, which its socket does each hour it stays up (`den_devices.last_seen_at`). Staff read each member's latest, as its day in UTC, at `GET /api/members/seen`, where the den's info says `members_seen`: enough to find who hasn't been on in a year, and less than presence says. A member who's online reads Online. It stays out of `ready`.
- *The audit log* holds staff's acts in the den, a line each: who, what, on whom or where, and when. A member removed or banned, with how much of their messages went and whether their invite did; a ban lifted; a role changed, the owner's by an owner invite among them, whose line names the den's machine as acting and who took over; an invite made or revoked; a channel or a group made, changed or deleted, a move within its list aside; another member's message deleted, or its reactions taken off, each naming the message's author; a member disconnected, muted, unmuted or moved in a call; a call's record reset; an emoji or a sound added, renamed or deleted; another member's event changed, canceled or ended; and the den's name, address, a limit or retention changed, with the values before and after. An act that changes nothing writes none. Never a message's text, nothing of a DM, where staff have no powers, and nothing a member does to their own.
- *Its table* is the eighteenth migration (see [Data across versions](../design.md#data-across-versions)): a line's time, kind, and who acted and on whom, by member ID, since members' rows stay, so it outlives what it was about; the rest is JSON sealed with the data key, bound to the line, so a kind added later takes no step. A line commits with its act. The den's export and backups carry the table.
- *How long.* 90 days: the den's timer, set for the oldest line, sweeps each as its time passes, and catches up as hosting starts. A line past its time stays out of what staff read though the timer is late, as a host that slept makes it.
- *Reading it.* Manage's Audit log, for moderators and the owner, newest first, 50 lines a page (`GET /api/audit`), where the den's info says `audit_log`. Each kind reads in its words, the settings it changed under it, and a kind the page doesn't know by its name. Nothing of it goes over the socket.

## Structure

- Channel groups are one level deep and contain text and voice channels. A member folds a group by its name, and unfolds it the same way; the browser keeps which are folded, by den. A folded group still shows the channel that's open and any voice channel with a call in it, and its row shows its other channels' unread state and mentions.
- Staff open a channel's menu from the ⋯ beside it in the channel list, which shows on hover or focus, from a right click on its row, or from a long press on a touch screen, which shows no ⋯ there: Edit channel, which opens its settings, and Move up and Move down, disabled at its list's ends. The settings hold its name, its group, whether it's staff-only, a text channel's description, a voice channel's bitrate, and deleting it. A group's menu holds New channel, which makes one in that group, Edit group, and Move up and Move down. A voice channel can't be opened, since clicking it joins its call, so its menu is the way to its settings.
- Staff make a channel or a group from the Den tab's +, the den's name's menu, and a right click on the list's empty space, each offering New channel and New group; touch screens and keyboards reach the first two, having no right click.
- With a mouse, staff drag a channel to another place in its group or into another group, and a group among the groups, by HTML's drag and drop, as the message pane takes files. A channel dropped beside another lands above or below it, by which half of its row the pointer is over, and one dropped on a group's heading goes first in that group, or past the last of the channels in no group onto that list; a line above or below a row, or a ring round a heading, marks where it would land. The drop sends what Move up and Move down in its menu and Group in its settings send, the channel's new place in its list (`PATCH` with `position` and, where it changes, `group_id`), which the den already takes, and a group's its place among the groups; a drop where the channel or group is already sends nothing. Touch screens and keyboards keep the menus' Move up and Move down. A drag the list didn't start, from another window or with files, lands nowhere in it.
- Text channels can have a description in the same markdown subset, up to 4,000 characters. Its first line shows next to the channel name, and a click expands or collapses the rest.
- DMs are one-to-one between members of the same den, stored on the den and end-to-end encrypted (see [End-to-end encryption](e2ee.md)). Closing a DM hides it until a new message arrives in it, and that follows the member across devices, like read positions.

## Profiles

- Each den has its own profile for each member, since identity is per den: a display name and a bio of up to 300 characters in the markdown subset. Dens can't link a member's profiles across dens.
- Bios travel only when a profile card opens, which keeps snapshots small for 500-member dens, and so do the badges a member earned (see [Events](#events)).
- A *status* is a line of the profile, up to 80 characters of plain text, cleaned as a display name is with each of Unicode's emoji whole, and when it ends: 30 minutes, 1 hour or 4 hours on, midnight by the member's device's clock, or never. The member sets it in Edit profile, where the den's info says `status`: Status, and under it once there are words Clear after, which opens at the end a status already has. A save that changes neither leaves the status and its end as they are, and one that empties Status clears it. It travels on the member's record, so it shows under their name in the member list, on their card and in a DM's profile, and only members who set one carry it. Each page stops showing it at its end by its own clock, and the den sends none past it and removes it in its hourly pass, telling nobody, so no status needs a timer and a den that slept sends nothing stale. The den keeps it sealed with the data key, as the bio, and leaving the den takes it.
- A profile can carry a picture and a banner: uploads the member crops in the page to a square and to three times as wide as high, zooming and dragging the image in a frame. The page draws the framed part into a new image, which leaves the original's metadata behind, and uploads that like any other image. Until someone sets a picture, their avatar is a circle colored by their member ID with the first letter or digit of their username, so every device draws the same one without the den storing or sending anything.
- A picture or a banner can be animated. The crop dialog frames an animated GIF or WebP by its first frame, as it frames a still, and says it keeps moving; the page then sends the file as it is, with the part framed in its pixels, to the member's local service, which makes each frame again as a GIF of the picture's size and uploads that (see [Animated pictures](files.md#animated-pictures)). An animated picture is 256 pixels square at most, and an animated banner 600 × 200, half a still's sides, since each of a GIF's frames costs its full size, and they're at most 2 MiB and 4 MiB. An animated PNG, rare among pictures, frames as a still of its first frame. A member's picture says whether it's animated (`animated`, beside its size), from what the den keeps of every image.
- Animated pictures play as Appearance's *Animations* says: Always, or Hover, which the browser keeps, as it keeps the theme; Always unless the system asks for less motion (`prefers-reduced-motion`), and Hover then. Hover plays a picture while the pointer is over its row, not over the picture alone: its message, or the one a reply quotes, its member in the list or a call, its DM in the list or a DM's header, the member's own panel; a profile card, the profile beside a DM and the member's own as they edit it play theirs while they show, since the member opened them. A touch screen has no hover, so there Hover plays pictures in profiles alone, a finger's touch, which browsers report as a hover, not counting. An animated picture that isn't playing shows its preview, the den's still of its first frame, which the picture covers while it plays, so it never blinks out as it starts, and which hides once the picture over it has loaded, so none of it shows where the picture is transparent; one component draws them all, avatars, banners, emoji and badges. A GIF in a message plays as Animations says too (see [Previews](files.md#previews)).
- A member who leaves or is removed loses their pictures along with their devices, so staff removing someone also takes down what they had on their profile.
- Edit profile keeps Save and Cancel at its foot while its fields scroll, and on a wide window shows the member's card beside them as others will see it, changing as they type, framed as a preview.
- A profile card shows its member as a card. Their banner runs across its top to its edges, or, where they have none, a band of the theme's quiet color; their picture sits across the banner's foot, ringed in the card's own background, so the two read as one; and their display name and username go under it. Beside the display name, before the verified check, a crown marks the owner and a shield a moderator, in the icons' style, each with its tip, Owner or Moderator; a member has none. They're drawn at 80% of the text's color, darker than the icons' 60%, which falls under a graphic's 3:1 against the member list in some themes, as the `calls` check measures in every theme. The member list's rows take the same marks, and so do a call's rows and messages' names where the member turns on Appearance's Staff marks, off until then, which the browser keeps (`DENS_STAFF_MARKS`), as it keeps Density; their tips show on a mouse's hover, since a row or a name is a button that opens the card, which a tap gives. One component draws them all, centered on the name, so they stand at one height everywhere. No role shows as a pill.
- In the member list, a name too long for its row scrolls to its end while a mouse rests on the row, and back as it leaves, at 40 pixels a second after 400 ms, or at once where the system asks for less motion. A touch screen has no hover, so names stay cut there, whole on the card. A right click on a row opens, at the pointer, what the member's card offers: Profile, then Message, or on one's own Edit profile and Notes, then what the card's ⋯ holds.
- The badges a member shows go beside their names, under Badges, wrapping into as many rows as they take; on a card narrower than about 26rem, as on a phone, under the names (see [Events](#events)). Under a thin line comes the bio, as text rather than in a box like a field, and under it, quietly, Member since, or No longer in this den since, with a calendar's icon.
- One button stands out on a card: Message on another member's, and on one's own Edit profile, with Notes beside it. The rest is under ⋯, a menu as a message's is, which touch and the keyboard reach, a sheet on the phone layout: Compare codes, or Compare codes again; the owner's Promote to moderator and Demote to member; and Remove from den…, or Ban… for one who left, for staff of a higher rank. A card's title shows nowhere; it names the dialog for screen readers. On a wide window the card opens beside the name or picture clicked, to its right, or its left where only that side has room, its top at theirs, with nothing dimmed; a press outside it, a second click on what opened it, Escape and Back close it. A phone keeps it a sheet. The profile beside a DM takes the same look, with Compare codes and the DM's disappearing setting under it, and the DM's header opens it with a one-person icon, where a channel's has people.

## Names

- Usernames are unique, 2 to 32 characters of `a-z`, `0-9` and `_`, lowercased on entry and fixed at join.
- Display names are free-form Unicode up to 32 characters. Control characters and Unicode's format characters (`Cf`, the bidi overrides and zero-width ones among them) are stripped, so a name can't disguise itself or reorder the text around it, and so are the fillers that render blank (U+115F, U+1160, U+3164, U+FFA0 and U+2800); a name must keep something visible.

## Name styles

A member gives their name, in a den, a font, a color or a gradient, which everyone there sees.

- *A style* is the profile's `name_style`: a font, one of eight by name (`denproto.NameFonts`), or none; a color, `#rrggbb` in lowercase, or none; and with a color a second, which the name runs to from its start to its end. The den checks it as it's set and keeps it on the member's row as the JSON their record carries, the sixteenth migration (see [Data across versions](../design.md#data-across-versions)). It travels with the member in `ready` and every event, left out where there's none, so an older Dens shows the name plain; a den says it keeps them (`name_styles`), and the page offers one only there.
- *Where it shows:* the member list, messages and the quotes of replies, Compact too, a call's rows, the DM list and a DM's header, the profile card and the member's own panel. Mentions keep their chip, and notifications their plain text.
- *It's a member's, so hostile.* The client takes a den's member only with a font that reads as a name, lowercase letters, digits and hyphens, and colors of six hex digits. The page reads it again (`names.js`): a font by its place in the page's own list, one it lacks showing as none, and a color by the same pattern. So a style only ever gets a font family and a color the page wrote out itself, set through the CSSOM as a theme's are.
- *Reading on every theme.* A color shows as picked where it contrasts at least 3:1 with each of the theme's three backgrounds; one that doesn't has its OKLCH lightness moved toward the text's side, 0.02 at a time, until it does, its hue kept, so a dark red on a dark theme reads as a lighter red. A gradient's ends move each alone. The page reads the theme's backgrounds and text from the computed variables through a canvas as each theme is applied, and keeps each result by color and theme.
- *The fonts* are Pacifico, Fredoka, Zilla Slab, Space Mono, Orbitron, Pixelify Sans, Bangers and Cinzel: each the Latin woff2 of one weight from a Fontsource package pinned in `vendor.sh`, 6 to 32 KB and 145 KB together, which the build copies into the page's assets (`assets/fonts/`), uncommitted as the bundle is. The shell lists their hashed addresses (`data-fonts`), and the page gives each to the browser as a `FontFace`, under `font-src 'self'`, with its Latin range less the space. A browser fetches a face only for a name on screen with a character of it, so a name in another script shows in the page's font and fetches nothing; and it measures a line by the first font that has a space, the page's, so no row changes height for a font. One weight stands for every weight, so the browser makes no bold of its own. Each is under the Open Font License, in the notices.
- *The name's box.* A face's letters reach past the line, and some past a name's ends, as Bangers' slant and Cinzel's Q do, where a row that cuts long names short would cut them and a gradient wouldn't reach: a styled name's box grows 0.3em above and below, and before and after the name by its font's own reach, measured from its Latin letters and digits (`ROOM` in `names.js`), with a negative margin as large, so it takes no more room. A gradient's stops sit at the name's ends, its end colors filling the room past them. The `names` check measures each font's ink against its box.
- *Edit profile* has Name style under the display name: the name as it will show on this theme, whatever the member's own Name styles says; the fonts as tiles in their own faces; and Color, Default, Solid or Gradient, each color the browser's picker with its hex beside it, as the theme editor's.
- *The reader's say.* Appearance's Name styles: Color & font, Color, or Off, which shows every name plain. The browser keeps it (`DENS_NAME_STYLES`), as it keeps Density.

## Events

A den's events: a game night, a watch party, a meeting, each at a time and in a place, which members mark Interested to be told as it starts.

- *The Events row,* at the top of the Den tab's list, opens the den's events in the channel's place (`/den/<den>/events`), on the phone layout a pane of its own as a channel's is: those live first, then the rest by their start, each a card. The row says Live while one runs, else how many are to come; an ended event leaves the list. The den's name still opens Manage, since events are every member's and Manage mostly staff's.
- *An event* has a name, up to 100 characters, cleaned as a poll's choice is, each of Unicode's emoji kept whole; a description, up to 2,000 characters in the markdown subset; an optional banner, cropped three times as wide as high and checked as a profile's banner is, an upload of whoever set it, which a newer one, or canceling the event, deletes; a start, in the member's own time zone in the form, up to a year ahead, or this minute, which starts it at once; a length from 5 minutes to a week, from a list; and a place: one of the den's voice channels, or a line of text, up to 100 characters. The den keeps what members wrote of it, its name, description and place's text, sealed with the data key, as a message's text, in a row of its own, with its banner, voice channel, maker, times and state; who's interested is a row each; and the den's limit for members' events is a column of its own, the twelfth migration (see [Data across versions](../design.md#data-across-versions)). An event that ended stays, out of the list, with its banner, for its cards and its badge, keeping whether its voice channel was staff's alone once the channel goes, so it stays where it was seen.
- *Who makes them.* Staff, and members as far as the owner lets each, in Manage's Events: how many events that haven't ended each member may have, 0 by default, which leaves events to staff, up to 10; lowering it leaves the events made. A den holds 50 that haven't ended, to come and live, so the list, which every snapshot carries, stays bounded. A member changes and cancels their own, and staff those of members they outrank, by the rule calls and messages use; the maker isn't marked Interested by making one. An event in a staff-only voice channel is staff's alone, its banner too, and follows its channel: as the channel turns staff-only or back, or staff move an event into or out of one, members who aren't staff start over from a fresh snapshot, as a channel's visibility has them do, and the count of those interested holds only those who see it. Deleting a voice channel cancels its events to come and ends its live one. A member who leaves or is removed takes their events to come, banners and all, and their marks on the rest; their live events go on to their end.
- *Live,* from its start to its end. Its maker or staff end it early with End now, or move its end from the form, whose start then stays as it is, never before now and within its length's limits. One that started can't be canceled, only ended. The den's timer, which ends polls and deletes disappearing messages, starts and ends events, set for the next start or end as events are made, moved, canceled and ended, and as hosting starts, when it catches up: an event that passed whole while the den was stopped ends without starting, and one partway through starts then. It gives badges too, as each member's stretch in a live event's call comes due.
- *Interested.* Each member's mark, on the card, which counts toward their other writes. Everyone who sees the event sees how many, the card's count, and their own mark; nobody sees who. The den keeps who's interested to tell them as it starts, and forgets it as the event ends. Marking Interested asks the browser to show notifications, where it hasn't asked and events notify, since a browser asks only in answer to a click.
- *Starting.* As an event starts, the den sends `event.started` to its interested members, an audience fixed as it's made, numbered like other events so a socket that resumes soon after still hears it from the ring, and `event.updated`, live, to the rest who see it. The member's Dens notifies them under Events (see [Notifications](notifications.md#notifications)), and logs the event's ID, which says a missed notification wasn't the den's; a Dens that wasn't connected hears nothing later, its next snapshot showing the event live. The den pushes the start rather than each Dens timing it, since Go's timers stop while a phone sleeps; on Android it comes as a message's notification does, and the shell's tap opens the event's path (see [android.md](../android.md#notifications)).
- *On the page.* A card shows the banner, the name and Live while it runs; when, in the member's time zone, as Today, Tomorrow or the day, its start's and end's times, or while live, until when; where, a voice channel's name, which a live event's Join joins, a primary button so it stands out, with the staff-only mark where its channel is staff's, or the line of text; the description; who made it; Interested, a primary button while it asks to be marked, then plain with a tick, ✓ Interested, pressed while marked, and how many are; and to whoever may change it, Edit, and Cancel… or End now, each asking once more. + Event, for a member who may make one, opens the form, a sheet on the phone layout. A notification's click or tap opens the events at the event, lit for a moment. The page applies the den's events to its view as they come, and each Dens keeps the events with the den's state, so they show while the den is away, but for those past their end by the clock, which the den would have ended.
- *Older ones.* An older Dens ignores the events, their routes and `event_limits`; a den older than events sends no `event_limits`, so the page offers no Events there.

An event's link shows its card where it's posted, and an event in a voice channel can carry a badge, which members earn by being in its call:

- *Links and cards.* An event's card in the pane has Copy link, which copies the den's address with the den's ID and the event's in its fragment, `#e/<den ID>/<event>`, as a message's link has `#m/` (see [Links to messages](messages.md#links-to-messages)). In a message, such a link shows as a chip naming the event, after its den's name when it's another den's, which opens it in its den's events, and under the message, after its files, as the event's card: its banner, its name, which opens it too, Live or Ended, when, in the member's time zone, where, its badge, and until it ends how many are interested, with Interested, which marks it as the pane's does. A message shows three cards at most, none for a link in code or a spoiler, and a reply's quote and a preview show the address. The card of one of the open den's events to come comes from its view; any other its Dens answers from the events it keeps, so the den hears of an event to come only as it always does, and asks the den only for the rest, by `GET /api/events/{id}`, an ended one among them, which says it ended and that nobody's interested any more. Once the event changes, the card reads it again. A link to an event the member can't see, or that's gone, which look alike, gets no card, and its chip says so at a click; one in a den they haven't joined names its host, as a message's does. A card in a DM asks the den for an event the member's Dens doesn't keep, as any other does, which tells the den which ended event they looked at, and nothing of the DM.
- *Badges.* Staff attach one to an event in a voice channel, making or editing it: a square picture, cropped as a profile picture is and drawn at an emoji's size, 128 pixels, or an animated GIF the member's local service makes from the crop as it makes an animated emoji, checked as an emoji's image is; and a title of up to 40 characters, cleaned as the event's name is. Members who aren't staff can't, the event's maker among them. The badge shows on the event's cards, with its title. Staff change its title or picture, or take it off, until the event ends, after which it stays as it was; an event with a badge stays in a voice channel, so the den refuses a move elsewhere unless the same request takes the badge off, which staff's form sends with the move and a maker who isn't staff can't send (see [protocol's Cards and badges](../protocol/members.md#cards-and-badges)); and canceling it, or its going as its maker leaves or its channel is deleted, takes the badge too. The picture is one of the den's named files, of kind `badge`, as an emoji's is, so it stays the den's whoever added it, opening to everyone who sees the event and counting toward nobody's space; its name is its file's ID, since titles repeat, and the title is sealed with the data key, as the event's own text is, in a row of the badge's own.
- *Earning one.* A member in the event's call for a minute at a stretch while it's live earns its badge, once, their call held while their connection is away or not. The den times each stretch from when the member's call joined the channel, which the call registry holds in memory as long as the call lasts, or from the event's start for one there before; it writes only the award, the badge, the member and when, in a row of its own, so its database holds nothing else of who was in the call. The den's timer gives each badge as its stretch comes due, a stretch that comes due only after the event's end earning nothing, and the den tells nobody. A development instance's den takes a stretch of three seconds, for the `events` check. Badges and their awards are the thirteenth migration, with an event's staff-only mark (see [Data across versions](../design.md#data-across-versions)).
- *Showing them.* A member's profile card shows the badges they earned, latest first, their pictures under Badges, each with a tip: its title, its event's name and the event's day, in the member's time zone. They travel with the profile card, as its bio does, never in `ready` or a member's record, and only those whose events the viewer sees, so a staff-only event's badge shows to staff alone, even once its channel is gone; a card opened while the den is away shows none. An animated badge plays as an emoji does. Each member shows or hides each of theirs: Edit profile lists every badge they earned, each with an eye at its top right, open while it shows and struck while it's hidden, which a click or a tap turns at once, as a picture changes; their card then shows only those shown, to everyone, the member included. It keeps a card tidy and nothing more, which the guide says: the den still knows who earned what. The den keeps the mark on the award (`hidden`, the fourteenth migration), leaves a hidden badge out of everyone's copy of the profile but the member's own, which marks it for Edit profile, and tells nobody; a badge starts shown. A badge's tip on an event's card says how it's earned: "Join during the event for a minute to earn this badge!"
