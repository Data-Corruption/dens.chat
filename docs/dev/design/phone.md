# The phone layout

Part of [Dens's design](../design.md): every screen of the page at a phone's width, in a browser or the Android app.

- *Which windows.* Below 768 pixels, Tailwind's `md`, the page has the phone layout, whatever the form: a phone's, and a narrow desktop window's, beside another or in a tiling window manager, which gets a layout that fits it rather than a squeezed desktop one. How it's worked follows the pointer: touch's ways for a coarse one, hover for one that hovers. A phone held sideways, or a tablet, wider than that has the desktop's layout, worked by touch.
- *Three panes.* A den's page lays its list, its channel or DM, and its members, or beside a DM the other member's profile, and beside a group DM its members, in a row, one on screen at a time. The side panes take 85% of the width, so the channel shows at their edge, dimmed, and a tap there goes back to it, as phone chat apps do. A pane off screen is `inert`, so focus and a screen reader stay on the one shown. The channel header's ☰ and people buttons move between them, the ☰ marked with what waits elsewhere in the den. A wider window shows the three side by side, the members by their toggle, which the browser keeps.
- *Swipes.* The row scrolls sideways and snaps to each pane (CSS scroll snapping): the browser does the gesture, its momentum and its feel at the ends, and the page goes where it settles, watching `scrollend`, or a pause in scrolling in a WebView older than Chromium 114. A settle counts as a swipe only when the member moved the row: while the page slides it to a pane, as Back or a button asks, it settles elsewhere only as a late snap does, which Firefox's can, until it arrives or the member touches it. Firefox ends such a slide on a whole pixel, short of a snap point between two, as a side pane's 85% often puts one, so once there the page snaps it at once, and the member's next swipe moves the row; and the channel's message box takes the focus as it opens without scrolling to it, which in Firefox would stop the slide. The channel stops a fling (`scroll-snap-stop`), so a fling from the list rests there rather than at the members. Anything that scrolls sideways itself, as a block of code, keeps a swipe that starts on it while it can scroll, as nested scrollers do. Android's gesture navigation takes a swipe from the screen's edge for Back, which goes up a level as a swipe to the right does, so a swipe starts a little in from the edge, as for any Android app's drawer. In a narrow window a touchpad's sideways swipe moves the panes too.
- *Back.* Each entry the page puts in its history holds its trail: the screens from Home, the history's root, to the one the entry shows. On the phone layout a den's list, its channel and its members are each a level; a wider window shows them together, so a den is one level there, its channel's. Each dialog, the settings and Manage among them, and the viewer, is a layer over the screen it opens on. Back closes what's on top, and otherwise goes up a level: from the members to the channel, from the channel to the list, from the list to Home. A move goes back through the history to where the trail shown and the one it asks for part, then forward a step at a time, so Back walks up the trail whatever brought the page there: a channel opened from the list goes above it, one opened by a link or a notification takes the place of the one shown, and one in another den goes above that den's list. A dialog closed by its ✕, Escape or a click outside it takes its entry back, and Manage with changes unsaved asks before Back closes it, as before its ✕ does. A page opened at a channel, as a notification or a link opens one, puts Home and the den's list beneath it. Back at Home leaves the page: the Android app closes, or goes behind the others during a call, and a browser's tab goes to what it showed before. The address names the channel, and the entry the pane, so Forward, a reload and a notification's click each show what they name; a reload drops the dialogs its entries held. The list's own entry keeps the den's address alone, and shows behind it the channel last shown, or what the tab picked there.
- *Between dens.* Home stays the way between dens, Back from a den's list, as on the desktop another den is Home and Open away. Its rows say what waits in each den: its mentions, counted, or a dot for unread messages, and the list's Home button is marked while another den has something. The service counts each den's channels and DMs with unread messages and their mentions, DMs' messages among them, as it counts them for the den's own page, in the dens list it gives every page, which it sends again as reading or a message changes them.
- *A long press.* On a touch screen, at any width, a message has no ⋯, and its text keeps the 32 pixels the ⋯'s column took: a long press on it opens its actions, as phone chat apps do, and on the phone layout its quick reactions with them. A long press on a reaction's chip says who reacted, and isn't the message's. A press held half a second within 10 pixels is a long one; one that moves first, as a scroll or a swipe between panes does, or that the browser takes over, isn't, and the click a browser may send as it's let go goes nowhere, nor onto the sheet opened under the finger. The long press takes the place of the browser's own selection and menu on a message, so copying part of one waits for a Select text, should members miss it. A mouse and the keyboard keep the ⋯, as a call's members keep theirs, in lists with room for them. A list's channels, groups and DMs have no ⋯ on a touch screen either: a long press on a row opens its menu, as on a message, a text channel's, a DM's and a group's for every member, and a voice channel's and a group's for staff.
- *Sheets.* On the phone layout the page's menus open as sheets from the screen's foot, with rows of 48 pixels: a message's actions, headed by its author and its time in full, when it was edited and who else can edit it, so what a hover shows of a message is a long press away, with a row of quick reactions, each 48 pixels square, above them; the composer's +; the menu of a member in a call, with their volume; the switcher, its box at its top, opened from the magnifier beside the den's name, since a touch screen has no Ctrl; and the emoji picker, its emoji in tiles 44 pixels high, nine to a row. A sheet's head, its handle and its title, stays at its top while a long sheet scrolls, so the picker's search and its buttons, which are its title, stay in reach. A tap above a sheet closes it, as do a swipe down past a third of its height or a flick, Back, since it takes an entry in the history, Escape, and its rows' actions. A wider window keeps its menus, touch or not, and there a long press opens the message's menu beside it.
- *Targets.* Nothing a finger or a mouse aims at is under 24 pixels each way, WCAG 2.2's minimum, but words within a line of text, which WCAG leaves to the text's size. With a coarse pointer a list's rows are 44 pixels tall, a menu's items, channels, DMs, members, those in a call and those who may edit a message among them, and small buttons that stand clear of others take room around them to 44 pixels without growing to the eye (`touch-room`, an `::after` around the button).
- *What hover shows.* A tip opens on hover, keyboard focus or a tap, and a tap's tip stays until the next tap, on it or anywhere else. It opens within the window, and within a list around it that scrolls, which would cut it off, moved in from an edge it would run past and narrowed to fit, and above where there's no room below. A guide's i, a link to dens.chat, opens its tip on a tap and the guide on the next. Marks whose meaning a title held get tips: the den's state, staff only's lock and a muted bell beside a channel or a DM, which stand beside its row's button rather than in it, the button reaching under them across the row, a member's verified mark, the marks of a call's members, LIVE where it does nothing else, the call's state, a call's length, whose tip names its channel's longest call, and who watches a share. A touch screen shows a share's controls in the player, and a video in the list shows its own on a tap, as on a click. A title that repeats a button's label stays a title.
- *Touch's hints.* Where a hint names keys, a touch screen gets its own: Formatting help says the button sends and ↵ starts a line, an edit says nothing of keys beside its Save and Cancel, and comparing a photo's copies says to hold a finger on the picture to see the other one, which does it.
- *The composer.* With a coarse pointer ↵ starts a line, since a phone's keyboard has no Shift, and a send button of 48 pixels shows beside the box once there's something to send; pressing it leaves the keyboard up. Ctrl+Enter still sends, as from a tablet's keyboard, and a window with a mouse keeps Enter sending. The box asks the keyboard for a return key, or a send key where Enter sends (`enterkeyhint`). While a touch screen's keyboard is up the box grows to half of what the keyboard leaves of its pane, so the list keeps room, and what stands above it, a reply, files and who else may edit, scrolls within 30% of the window's height. The composer stays above the keyboard, since the Android app's shell gives the WebView the room the keyboard leaves, and the list keeps its newest message in sight as it shrinks.
- *Fields' keyboards.* Each field asks a phone's keyboard for what it takes: usernames, invites, recovery codes, the DM recovery key and den addresses no capitals or corrections, an address the keyboard for addresses too, and counts and sizes digits, with a point where they take fractions.
- *Dialogs.* On the phone layout a dialog is a sheet from the screen's foot, as tall as it needs up to the screen's height less a strip above it, with a handle, its title and ✕ staying at its top while the rest scrolls. A swipe down from its top closes it, as from a menu's sheet, as do a tap above it, its ✕ and Back. One frame, `Dialog`, makes every dialog but the settings, Manage, the viewer and comparing: a channel's and a group's settings, a profile, one's own, removing a member, deleting a message, cropping a picture, adding a den and the writing guide. A wider window keeps them as cards in the page's middle.
- *Settings and Manage.* On the phone layout each takes the screen: a list of its sections, rows a finger fits, each opening its section as a page with ← back, as Android's own settings do. A section's page takes an entry in the history after the frame's own, so Back, ← and Escape go from a page to the list, and Back and the list's ✕ close them; opened at a section, as Home's Turn hosting on opens the System section, the list is beneath it. Every page is headed by its section's name, in the settings as in Manage; the settings on a den's page group that den's sections under its name, then the app's, with Leave den apart, and Manage's bar of unsaved changes stays at the foot of the list and of each page. Their cards lose the dialog's margin and their own padding and background, a line parting each from the last, which gives their text 358 of 390 pixels rather than 278. A wider window keeps their columns of sections. Appearance's page has its columns as tabs along its foot, Themes, Editor and Preview, the themes first, and a theme's unsaved edits in a bar above them.
- *What fits.* Nothing on the phone layout runs past the screen's right edge but within a scroller of its own, as a block of code, which the phone check asserts on every screen. Labels holding a checkbox or a toggle wrap, as DaisyUI keeps them to one line, from a rule in the utilities' own cascade layer, which outranks DaisyUI's, nested within it. Comparing stacks its Smaller and Full size; the recovery codes stand in one column, each whole, two in a wider window; and Home's rows put Manage and Open under the den's name, which keeps the row's width. A DM's divider where its keys changed wraps its words within most of the width, the lines beside it taking the rest, as DaisyUI keeps a divider's words on one line; a channel's message list, which holds whatever scrolls sideways in a scroller of its own, never scrolls sideways itself, which the phone check asserts.
- *The viewer.* On the phone layout the viewer takes the screen, black, the picture the screen's width. A swipe sideways moves to the message's next or last picture, which slides in beside it, as the one before slides out; past the first or the last the picture gives a little and comes back. A swipe down closes it, the screen fading as it goes, as do Back and Escape; a tap beside the picture closes it, as a click there does. A mouse's ‹ and › stand over the picture's sides, and a finger has none. Its name, size, Download and Close sit in a bar at its foot. Zoom works on every form, with the same module comparing uses (`zoom.js`): a view is the picture fitted to the stage, zoomed by a scale, its corner where a pan put it, kept covering the stage each way it's larger and centered each way it's smaller. A pinch's spread scales it, and the spot under its fingers' middle follows them; a double tap zooms two and a half times at the spot, or back to fitted; the wheel zooms at the pointer. Once larger than fitted, a press pans rather than swipes.
- *A phone's settings.* What a phone can't do leaves its settings, as the speaker's choice does where the browser can't pick one. The service says what its form has to the page, as it says the page may play sound without a click: the Android app's page is in Dens's own app (`data-in-app`), which holds its permissions and pairs it, and has no keys its shortcuts may take (`data-keys`). So the phone's Voice has no keyboard shortcuts, and the call's button's role takes the place of how the member sends (see The call's button); where the browser can't share a screen, as the WebView can't, it has no Screen share card; a refused microphone says to allow it for Dens in Android's settings, under Apps, rather than in a browser's site settings; and the System section's card for signing out names this page, which Dens pairs again as it's opened. Add a den offers to host one only where the dens list says the service can (`can_host`), which the Flatpak and the Android app can't.
- *A phone's words.* Where a phone shows the words too, the page says this device rather than this computer: the local password protects Dens on this device, a den is removed from this device, and Dens on this device restarted. "Computer" stays where only a desktop shows it: pairing, hosting, the shortcut helper, and the Flatpak's remembering its key. "This browser" becomes "this page" where the Android app shows it, as when it can't play a video, or the built-in noise suppression where the voice processing couldn't run. A den lists the phone among its member's devices by the name its owner knows it by, Android's device name, with its model (`Build.MODEL`) in parentheses at the label's end, as "Galaxy S24 Ultra (SM-S928U1)", from what the shell gives Dens in `DENS_DEVICE_NAME` and `DENS_DEVICE_MODEL`, where its hostname would say only localhost. A phone with no name of its own, or one that's its model, as a Pixel's is, is its model on Android, as "Pixel 9 Pro (Android)"; a computer is its hostname and its system, as "Pearl (Windows)". Manage's Devices shows a label's name, and what's in its closing parentheses under it, smaller; a label with none, as some an older Dens made, shows whole, and where a label shows in a line of text it shows whole. The label stays one field the den keeps, as it always was, so no den changes for it. It's cut to fit the den's 64 characters, what it is to 32, and a device with no name to show is Dens.
- *The system bars.* The Android app's status and navigation bars take the page's background, with icons to match, so they follow its theme, the phone's light or dark or one the member picks. The page reads its background as the theme applies, through a canvas, since a theme's colors are OKLCH and Android takes sRGB, and says it over its event socket as `bars`, `#rrggbb`, at each change and each time the socket opens. The service keeps the last in memory, refusing anything else, and passes it to the shell over its stream, as the stream opens and as it changes; the shell paints the window behind the bars with it, and has their icons dark on a light one. Until the page says, the bars are the window's, light or dark as the phone is.
- *The call's bar.* On the phone layout a call shows across the top of the channel's pane, under its header, in a bar of 40 pixels, wherever the member reads: the call's speaker, green once connected, where it is and how it stands, as "Connected to Lounge", or "in" another den's name, the call's lock, and buttons for the soundboard, mute, deafen and leave. Mute is there since a narrow window with a mouse has no call's button, and so had no mute on the bar at all, and the soundboard stands at mute's left. A tap on the rest opens the call's pane, the channel's pane here, where its members and its other controls are, as WhatsApp's and Telegram's bars open their calls (see [The call's pane](calls.md#the-calls-pane)); the bar doesn't show over the pane, which is the call. Why the last call ended, or didn't start, shows above the bar, since the click that joins opens the call's pane and the block is off screen. Home keeps its call box, and a wider window the call's block beside the channel.
- *The call's button.* On a touch screen in a call, a round button of 64 pixels holds the microphone, floating at the screen's foot on the right, over every pane, where a thumb rests, as Discord's app has a button to push to talk. A tap toggles mute, or a press mutes while it's held, or talks while it's held, as the member sets its role in Voice, where it takes the place of how they send: Toggle mute, the default, and Push to mute send by voice activity, and Push to talk sends only while it's held, ringing for the call as the key does. The role and the way of sending are kept together, and settings from before the button had a role follow their way of sending, so a member who sent by push to talk still does. The button is one more source of presses for the actions the keys and the helper drive, and a held press lets go however it ends: lifted, taken by the system, as Android's back gesture or its notification shade takes it (`pointercancel`), its pointer's capture lost, the page hidden or out of focus, or the button gone, as with the call; so the microphone never stays open. Its press keeps the focus where it was, so the composer's keyboard stays up, and its touch is the page's alone (its `touchstart` cancelled), so the browser makes no gesture of a held press: no long press, which a phone answers with a buzz, as if to select something, and no menu. It shows the microphone's state: talking in the speaking ring's green, muted, by the member or by staff, struck through in red. It floats 24 pixels above what stands at the channel pane's foot, the composer and what sits above it, or the call's pane's foot, which the pane measures, so it clears who's typing and the send button however the composer grows, and 16 above the screen's foot elsewhere. It floats over the message list's corner, as a floating button does, rather than making the list longer, so the list ends at its newest message; the phone layout's list keeps its call's controls, and its last row, clear of the corner. Below dialogs and sheets, it shows wherever the page has a touch screen, at any width, and in Android's app whatever its pointer, since the app has no keys; a window with a mouse has no button, and keeps its keys. Nothing presses it off screen, so a member on push to talk is silent with the screen off. It shows too for a call another of the member's devices holds, under that device's name, and presses there (see [Relay's The phone](helper.md#the-phone)).
- *The share player.* On the phone layout the shares the member watches dock across the top of a text channel's pane, under the call's bar, or under Home's call box, rather than floating over the call's controls; over the call's pane they show nothing, since its stage plays them. The player takes the screen's width, and the share's shape, 16:9 until its picture says otherwise, up to half the screen's height. It shows one share at a time, the one the member last began to watch, or showed of their own, with a button to the next where there are more; a tap on its picture plays it full screen, which the Android app's shell shows (`onShowCustomView`), turning with the phone, and its ✕ stops watching. Full screen takes the screen's size, which can widen the window past 768 pixels, as a phone turned sideways does, so the page's layout holds from the moment the page asks for full screen until nothing is full screen: the layout changing under the docked player would take away what plays. A wider window keeps the floating player, which stays on the phone layout showing nothing, so a share's window of its own keeps its place there.
- *The call's controls.* The call's block, box and bar show only what the browser can do, so where it can't share a screen, as the WebView can't, there's no share button rather than a greyed one. A member's ⋯ in a call opens a sheet on the phone layout, holding their volume as a slider whose handle is 32 pixels, which a finger holds without the sheet taking the drag for a swipe down. The soundboard opens as a sheet from the screen's foot there, as the page's menus do.
